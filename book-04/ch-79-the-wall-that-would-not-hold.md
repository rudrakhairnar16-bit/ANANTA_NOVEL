# CHAPTER SEVENTY-NINE — THE WALL THAT WOULD NOT HOLD

---

> *"The first report called it another flare.*
> *That was the last report written while anything still covered him."*
>
> *— ward incident chronology, terminology corrected after the night shift*

---

The doctor kept his hand clear of the new dark nick.

He could feel the skin beside it. He could examine Rudra's body. He could not press two edges of light together and call that treatment.

The pressure beneath the breastbone had not eased when the bright segment separated. Rudra described it as a band being pulled tighter from inside. He did not know whether the description was useful, but the nurse wrote down his actual words while the doctor checked the things he could assess.

The ward's urgent team was called. The clinical monitor was brought to the bed. Breathing, circulation, the chest discomfort and the ordinary causes which could not be ignored merely because there was also gold on his skin.

The braced wrist stayed supported. The ankle did not come off its rest.

Rudra watched the doctor's face for a conclusion which had not arrived.

"Mujhe uthna hai," he said.

"Abhi nahi. Jo pressure hai, pehle woh dekhne do."

He stayed.

---

A second short gap opened beside the new one.

The gold between them held for a moment, thin and bright. Then it went dark as well.

The nurse saw it without moving the mirror. So did the doctor. This was no longer only a drawing passed between people who had seen the change at different times.

The older patch above the collarbone remained. Its little lower offshoot had not politely disappeared to make room for the next problem.

Rudra reached towards the rail with the good hand.

Metal gave beneath his fingers.

Not far. A shallow bend, enough for the fitted joint to creak and the nurse to look down at the grip instead of only at his chest.

He opened the hand at once.

The rail stayed bent. The soreness in his fingers arrived after the sound, ordinary and embarrassingly familiar. He had once damaged a cart handle with a grip he had not meant to spend. Knowing that memory did not prevent the new pressure from frightening him.

"Maine kheenchna nahi—"

"Chhodo," the nurse said. "Ab haath idhar rakho."

She gave him a place on the bed which did not require holding another object. The doctor checked the hand too. Nothing about the strength in that instant had healed the wrist resting beside it.

---

The patient in the neighbouring bay heard the metal and called for the nurse.

An attendant went to him. Another member of staff kept the ward passage clear while the duty team decided what needed to be moved. The man had his own treatment, a supported leg and a family member waiting outside. He would not simply be told to walk away from somebody else's extraordinary event.

The team arranged his move to another staffed space with his care and belongings.

Rudra could hear the explanation through the curtain. A bed, not a body carried by frightened visitors. His bag went with him. The person waiting for him was told where he was going.

The wheels rattled at the threshold. Rudra waited until he could hear the other man ask where they were taking his bag, and an attendant answer.

The nurse drew the curtain farther round Rudra's bed and asked the attendant to take loose glass from the nearest table. The oxygen equipment and leads were handled by the team, not dragged aside to make a clear arena.

There was still a patient here.

---

Vedant arrived after the local call and the checked journey from the post.

His forearm was supported. He put the bag down with the other hand, stopped at the place the nurse showed him and listened to the current clinical account before asking Rudra what he had felt.

The separate segment below the breastbone. Two small gaps now joined. Pressure. An unintended grip strong enough to bend the rail.

He looked at the metal. Then at the boy who had already let it go.

"Koi count kiya tha?"

"Nahi."

"Ab bhi mat karna mere liye."

The doctor was still examining him. Vedant did not put a lesson between the clinician's question and Rudra's answer.

---

The fresh blood appeared above the collarbone while the nurse was securing the clinical lead.

Only a small bead at first, where the skin had been closed. It reached the shirt before Rudra felt the sting distinctly from the deeper pressure.

The nurse brought clean gauze. The doctor checked the new break and continued the assessment. Rudra watched the small red mark spread into the cloth. That part, at least, he understood well enough to be afraid of without asking Vedant.

Vedant saw the gold on either side fail to meet.

Below it, another part of the outline brightened through the loosened shirt and broke into two uneven lengths.

Rudra felt himself begin to draw the next breath as if it were something he had to earn.

---

The habit came before a decision.

Four. Then the impossible wish to make the next breath belong to nine instead, as if changing the place where he began might change what was failing above his heart.

The two old arithmetics would not become one because he was frightened enough to need them.

Rudra stopped with the air half held.

The doctor told him to breathe. Vedant said his name once, close enough that he did not have to search the room for it.

"Saans tumhaare liye hai," he said. "Is deewar ke liye nahi."

Rudra let the breath leave.

The gold did not mend. The pressure did not obediently vanish. His lungs simply got the next chance to work, and for the moment that was something a living body needed more urgently than a correct answer.

---

"Sabko door karo," Rudra said when he could speak again.

The nurse had already moved the loose things from the table. The other patient's bed was gone. A member of the urgent team remained by the open approach; the clinician stayed where he could examine Rudra without being caught against the damaged rail.

There was a plan for the space. It did not require abandoning the person in it.

"Tum log bhi."

The doctor looked at him.

"Tumhari jaanch chal rahi hai. Door se jo ho sakta hai, karenge. Jo paas se karna hai, uske liye koi rahega."

He had imagined that sending them away would make him less afraid. Now he could see the nurse checking the clear approach, the doctor watching his face, an attendant waiting for the next instruction. They had heard the metal bend. They were choosing where to stand with that knowledge, and he had no courage left over with which to argue them out of their work.

He kept his hands open on the bed.

Vedant moved when the nurse asked. He did not demonstrate courage by standing in the path she needed.

---

A fine gold trace showed at Rudra's side as the shirt was loosened farther for examination.

The clinician checked the skin beneath it. The nurse kept the covering over the areas she did not need exposed. There was no audience beyond the curtain and no phone raised to make the new light easier to describe tomorrow.

The trace held while the one below the breastbone broke again.

Rudra had seen a branch approach a fracture, a strip fail, a little offshoot grow. He had been able to remember each as a small shape beside another small shape.

Now the changes would not wait their turn.

A length near the ribs went dark while a separate bright junction above it split. The old collar patch remained the place where gold had already stopped answering, not the only place from which the rest could be understood.

Vedant put down the pencil.

The doctor needed the next symptom, not a finished picture.

---

The family update went from the ward with Rudra's agreement.

Worsening visible changes, pressure, a new small bleeding place being treated, urgent assessment continuing. The ward gave the receiving contact and the plan directly. When the nurse described the unintended grip, Rudra looked at the rail again. Her voice did not change. It was something the receiving doctor needed to know, not a reason for her to sound frightened of him.

Rukmini asked whether he could hear her for a moment.

The doctor allowed the short call while the team continued its work. The phone was held where Rudra did not have to lift the supported arm.

"Maa."

"Haan. Main hoon."

He had meant to ask her not to come running through the night. What came out instead was a question he had not allowed himself since the first days of pain had made asking it seem childish.

"Main theek ho jaaunga?"

Rukmini did not fill the room with a promise the doctor had not made.

"Abhi doctor tumhaare paas hain," she said. "Main phone pe hoon. Tumhe chup reh ke sab jhelna nahi hai."

He shut his eyes. The voice remained until the clinician needed an answer, and then his mother let the phone move away without asking for one last reassurance.

---

Behind the closed lids was the old impression of a held bell.

Not another room he could escape into. The same inward hush he had once mistaken for enough distance from his own body.

This time it would not hold the sound outside.

The scrape of the clinical trolley reached it. A nurse asking about the pressure reached it. The ordinary name which belonged to him, spoken without a title or a count, reached it again.

He opened his eyes and answered.

The gold at his side was still bright, but the junction which joined it to the line above had begun to separate.

Rudra felt the old urge to call the form which had once got his mother out of a killing line. He told Vedant about it before turning the urge into another injury.

"Is baar koi raasta nahi rok raha," Vedant said. "Tumhe roop dikha ke mujhe bachana nahi hai."

The nurse asked him to leave the good hand where she could see it. He did, even as another small sound came from the metal at the bed's far joint.

The attendant crouched to look beneath the frame. The bed had not dropped; the supported leg stayed level. He checked the locks while the nurse asked for a replacement to be brought near. Rudra kept his hand on the sheet and listened to the wheels coming down the passage.

---

At the post, Veer was awake before anybody asked whether he should be told.

The phone had rung at the organizer's table. He heard the place and the name, then the quieter voice people used when they were trying not to turn a whole room into a waiting crowd.

He asked for the actual update.

The organizer gave it. Worsening changes, the ward team there, Vedant beside Rudra, the other patient moved with care. No message that everybody should arrive at once.

Veer reached for the walking support, then stopped long enough to ask what the ward wanted from him.

Not another injured visitor in the working space tonight. A current contact at the post, the night arrangements kept covered and the things Rudra might need sent with somebody who could carry them.

He hated the answer for a moment.

Then he called the next volunteer himself and asked what shift he could actually take, rather than handing over a blank promise made in somebody else's name. The man agreed to the post hours. Another person would take the small bag when the ward asked for it.

Vedant could remain through the immediate assessment without leaving Veer or the post dependent on his return.

---

Veer recorded a short message.

There was no lesson in it. No demand that Rudra fight harder, find the old form or hold on until somebody arrived to approve of him.

"Main post pe hoon. Phone yahin hai. Jab sun sake tab sunna. Jawaab dena zaroori nahi."

He stopped, listened and nearly deleted it because it sounded too small for the thing happening at the hospital.

Then he sent it.

The volunteer asked whether he wanted the light left on.

"Thodi. Pair ke paas."

He settled the knee again. Being frightened did not give it a different set of instructions, and making another person attend two unnecessary injuries would not put him closer to his friend.

---

In Mirzapur, Sitara spoke to the ward and then to the girls.

She did not wake them with an order to pack for a journey their bodies and the route had not been cleared to make. They were told what had changed, what the team was doing and how the next update would reach them.

Ira asked for the account to be read aloud. It was outside her ordinary right-eye interval; forcing the screen nearer would add a pain she could predict to a danger she could not solve that way.

Aadhya kept her supported left hand against the folded cloth. Her right fingertips were still numb. She wanted to hold something hard enough for the pressure in her own chest to leave, but the hand could not be made to pay for that feeling.

She asked Sitara to tell Rudra that the next college question could wait.

Not the page, really. Not a permission to give up. A way of saying he was not late for anything which mattered more than being treated tonight.

Sitara sent the actual words, without promising that hearing them would close a fracture in light.

---

Rukmini heard Ram's nurse come out of the step-down space.

He was awake for his own care. The nurse had the receiving update and would help pass on the clinical information the other team needed. Rukmini went in when invited, her good hand empty because she had put the phone down before trying to manage the door as well.

She told him what the doctor had said. Not a quieter version and not an explanation she had not been given.

Ram listened without getting out of bed.

He asked whether Rudra had called for a form. He had not. Whether the new bleeding had been attended. It had.

The nurse helped with the next contact. Rukmini sat where she would not have to hold the aching shoulder against the chair's hard edge.

Her pulse beat in the small hollow above the wrist. She watched the ordinary movement until she could stop counting the spaces between the phone's sounds. It told her only that her own heart was still doing its work; it could not tell her what the boy in another city would survive.

Dadi came with water. Rukmini took it before somebody had to ask twice.

---

Midnight passed in the Prayagraj ward while the assessment continued.

The pressure in Rudra's chest had grown harder to speak through. The doctor reassessed his breathing and ordered the support he needed. The nurse settled the oxygen equipment without disturbing the gauze at the collar or the supported wrist.

Vedant moved back for the team, then returned to the place where Rudra could hear him.

The gold had spread into visibility along more of the old concealing outline. It did not make a smooth surface. Gaps interrupted it at the chest, the side and the curve which ran back towards the shoulder.

Red-black density showed in one of those gaps.

Not a second body. Not a chosen shape laid over the injured one. The colours he had once carried through a desperate rescue were there without an armour of intention around them.

The nurse asked whether he could still hear her.

"Haan," Rudra said.

Another gold junction broke before he finished the word.

---

The next break ran beyond the place Rudra could see.

He felt the pressure change at his side and tried to turn towards it. The nurse stopped the movement before it loaded the wrist. She and the doctor helped him settle within the support he already had; the examination did not require him to twist an injured body until the outline made a better picture.

Gold showed through the loose shirt at the shoulder. Another trace ran down the side, interrupted by dark gaps which were growing while the doctor watched.

The pattern was larger than the little region the mirror had once contained.

Rudra could no longer pretend that keeping his hand off the old wound would keep the rest of it intact.

"Ho kya raha hai?" he asked.

His voice came out thin behind the breathing support.

The clinician answered about the care being given. Vedant answered the smaller part he could see without borrowing a certainty from it.

"Jo dhak raha tha, woh toot raha hai."

Rudra waited for more. Vedant looked at the failing line, then back at his face. For once, there was no next instruction waiting behind the answer.

---

The nurse changed the blood-marked gauze.

The fresh break above the collarbone had not become a great open wound. It still needed attention, and the frightening light did not relieve the team of giving it. The doctor checked the chest pressure again. Rudra answered with a nod when the next sentence would not fit into the breath he had.

The bed sheet had bunched under the supported ankle. An attendant smoothed it away from the pressure point while the nurse kept the leg still.

A ridiculous detail, Rudra thought. Then the relief at the skin reminded him that the ordinary body had not stepped aside for the extraordinary thing happening through it.

The pain in his fingers remained. The wrist remained a wrist which could not safely bear weight. Nothing in the red-black showing through the gaps volunteered to repair either.

He kept the hands open.

---

The gold above the breastbone brightened.

For an instant, the surviving lengths all seemed to answer it. Light ran along the broken contour at the chest, over the visible shoulder, down the side beneath the cloth. The gaps did not close. The remaining pieces were simply lit at once.

Rudra mistook that instant for help.

He looked at Vedant with a hope so sudden it hurt worse than being frightened.

Then the bright joining length divided.

No blade entered the room. No hand touched it to make the separation happen.

The lower edge failed next. A little later, the place which had held above the old collar patch.

Rudra tried to follow them with his eyes. There were too many places failing for one frightened gaze to keep them in order.

---

The doctor asked his name.

"Rudra."

Where was he?

He gave the hospital, the city. The nurse's hand remained where she could check him without asking his braced wrist for help.

He wanted to ask whether the room was still safe for her. He wanted to tell Vedant to get farther away. He wanted his mother to say the little reassuring words she had refused to make dishonest.

What he managed was the next answer the doctor needed.

The old inward hush of the held bell offered no passage out. He was here, in a bed, with people whose names he knew and people whose work he had depended on before he learned them.

He looked at the nearest face and stayed with the question.

The impulse to catch somebody's hand came so strongly that his fingers moved before he stopped them. He remembered the worker's arm he had once hurt with pressure he had not understood. He did not know what a frightened grip would do now.

He left the palm flat.

"Baat karte rehna," he asked.

The nurse did. His name, the next question, the ordinary fact that she was moving the lead clear of his shoulder. He could accept a voice without asking another body to become something he could hold on to.

---

A tremor passed through him.

The mattress took it. The supported leg shifted against the padding, and the nurse steadied the position without making the ankle an anchor for the rest of his body.

Vedant reached towards the sound side, then stopped at the clinician's instruction. He could speak to the boy. He was not to wrestle the involuntary movement still or make his own injured forearm into a brace which would fail under it.

"Main yahin hoon," he said.

The remaining gold lengths flashed.

Not a new coat of light laid over the fractures. The fragments which had still joined one part of the concealing shape to another were coming apart at their own ends.

Rudra could feel the old expectation of a boundary, the thing against which the pressure had always met him, and then he could feel it in fewer places.

The room did not go quiet.

The monitor sounded. The doctor called for the next check. The nurse answered him. Someone moved a piece of equipment clear of the hand which had begun to tremble against the sheet.

The last connected length of gold broke.

The kavach shattered.

---

There was no intact cover left behind the falling light.

The familiar broken contour was not waiting underneath a brighter one. Across the whole concealing pattern, what had joined no longer held. Bright pieces thinned as they came away, leaving nothing solid for a hand to catch or put back.

Red-black density opened into the place the gold had covered.

Gold still moved within it, raw and distinct. It did not reassemble into the old edges. The colours which had once been visible through a desperate first form were exposed without Rudra choosing that form or knowing how to hold what was now uncovered.

He had not gained an answer.

He had lost the thing which had kept the question hidden.

---

The first pulse passed through the room without asking him to move.

The metal tray rang against the trolley. Cloth lifted from the empty edge of the nearest stand and settled again. The already damaged rail gave a short answering creak.

The doctor stayed with the patient. The attendant kept the cleared approach clear. The nurse asked the second attendant to hold the trolley clear of the foot support.

Rudra's good fingers curled against the sheet, then opened when the nurse asked. The movement had cost him concentration he could no longer spare from breathing.

Red-black and gold showed over the ordinary injured body, distinct enough that Vedant could see where each colour moved. There was no smooth new armour between them and the room.

The nurse asked whether the pressure had changed.

Rudra tried to answer.

He could hear the question. The next breath came. The word did not.

The clinician leaned closer and called his name.

---

Vedant had thought he would recognize the moment if it ever came.

He recognized what had gone, not a remedy for it.

There was no remaining joined contour to tell Rudra to keep inside. No final undamaged strip around the chest which a better number might spare. The old patch and branches had ceased to be holes in an otherwise concealing whole because that whole no longer existed.

He said what he could actually tell the clinical team.

"Poora toot gaya. Pehle waala cover nahi hai."

The doctor did not stop the assessment to make the words into a diagnosis of everything happening beneath them.

The fresh bleeding was attended. Breathing support stayed in place. The wrist and ankle were protected through the involuntary tremor. Rudra had a pulse beneath the nurse's fingers; he was no longer answering the way he had a few moments earlier.

They responded to that change at once.

Vedant stepped where he was asked, close enough to speak and clear of the hands doing the work. He gave the ward contact the current family number again and did not make the news wait until he could offer a better ending.

---

The light reached the curtain.

It coloured the cloth, the metal frame, the ordinary floor beyond the bed. Then it receded from those surfaces without knitting a single gold edge back over the body.

The local flare was fading. The hiding did not return with the darker room.

Rudra's name was still on a hospital chart. His wrist still needed its brace, his ankle its support, the blood at the collar its clean dressing. The world of ordinary injury had not agreed to release him because something vast had stopped concealing what else he carried.

At the bed, the doctor asked for the next observation. The nurse answered. The attendant brought what had been called for without stumbling into the cleared leads.

The room had work to do before anybody could decide what the sky had learned.

---

Beyond the hospital, the exposure did not stop at the limits of the visible light.

It was not a beam travelling down the road or an aperture opening above it. The source remained a living boy in a ward. What had hidden his true signature was gone.

Across the Lokas, the same red-black and gold stood exposed.

The colours did not merge into a new one. The old concealment did not survive at a distance merely because no human eye could follow the way the exposure was received.

In a chamber with no Earth window, the surface of a still brass bowl darkened at its centre. A fine gold light remained at the margin of the red-black mark. A hand which had been resting beside it lifted.

No face came into view.

Elsewhere, a suspended strip of metal began to ring against an empty stand. The hand reaching to still it stopped before the fingers closed. The red-black reflection had reached its polished side too.

No single room was the whole of what had received him.

---

In Prayagraj, the ward team was still giving care.

A nurse held the oxygen support steady while the doctor checked Rudra's response. The gauze above the collarbone had a new red stain; the light beneath it had no enclosing outline.

Vedant spoke his ordinary name again.

There was a small movement of the good fingers against the sheet. Not a signal anyone could turn into permission to stand. Not a new form answering a command. Enough for the nurse to note while the assessment continued.

He was here.

What had covered him was not.

---

In the chamber beyond Earth, the lifted hand returned to the ledge beside the bowl.

There was a sheet of writing there, not a connection to the hospital chart. The pulse had brought no bed number, no name of a street, no visiting-hour instructions. Whatever could read the exposed signature would have to answer it with its own knowledge and its own acts.

The fingers rested beside the new mark for a moment.

Then the lamp next to the bowl was put out.

The trace did not disappear.
