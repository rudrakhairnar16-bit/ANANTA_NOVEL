# CHAPTER SEVENTY-SEVEN — THE LAST UNSPENT POINT

---

> *"Eight points had owners.*
> *The ninth had a crowd.*
> *What broke between those two facts was left out of the first announcement."*
>
> *— corrected semifinal account, retained with the grounds report*

---

Yellow let the guard go.

The runner fell through the space it had occupied. His arm was free before the planted palm could draw it any farther, but the bearded man's other hand was already at his shoulder.

Fingers touched cloth.

The referee stopped the exchange. The root slackened at once. Yellow straightened, looked towards the line judge and nodded when the second official confirmed what he had felt.

One–nil to the Prakriti contender.

The man took his working hand out of the earth and shook loose the grit. There was a narrow red indentation across two fingers where the pressure of planting had pressed the skin against a small stone.

He picked the stone out of the surface and gave it to the grounds helper before returning to his corner.

One small obstruction removed. Not the whole earth made obedient.

---

From the verandah, Veer had seen Yellow choose the right escape and lose the point anyway.

The volunteer beside him asked whether releasing sooner would have saved it.

"Shayad," Veer said. "Pehle chhodta toh tab tak doosra haath kahaan hota, yeh nahi dekha maine."

He had seen one exchange, not all the exchanges which might have happened instead. It was a more difficult way of watching than deciding in advance which body ought to be clever enough for everything.

Below, Yellow took the water. The movement was ordinary. His holding forearm needed its rest, and the gap between points was not an insult he had to fill with another display.

The bearded man's corner rubbed a little dry earth off the good shoulder tab so the mark could still be seen. Then both men came back at the referee's call.

---

Yellow did not go near the grass on his second approach.

He circled into the dry centre and waited.

The Prakriti man followed far enough to keep the shoulder within a useful distance, but the planted hand had to come up. There was no runner stretched under the whole ring waiting for him to think of it.

Yellow took one short step forward.

His opponent shifted the nearer shoulder back and turned the other one towards the edge of the little guard beginning at Yellow's forearm.

The curve stayed small.

The man did not retreat from it. He stepped towards the unguarded side instead, trying to make Yellow turn his whole body while the free fingers went towards a tab.

Yellow turned only the guarding arm.

For an instant the two stood almost shoulder to shoulder, each far hand too close to its own body to finish the obvious touch.

The bearded contender tried to pass behind the pale face.

Yellow lowered it before the contact could press against his opponent's neck, changed the leading foot and came out beside him. His free fingertips brushed the far shoulder tab in the turn.

The referee's signal arrived before either could make the movement larger.

One each.

---

The man near Veer's chair made a satisfied noise.

"Ab samjha. Mitti chhuda do bas."

Below, the Prakriti contestant was smiling at the scoring official. He made a little circling motion with one foot, checking where he had let the other man's turn pass him, then went back for his water.

Veer watched that too.

"Ab woh bhi samjhega," he said.

The spectator looked at him as if this were a less useful answer than allegiance.

The volunteer shifted the folded cloth under Veer's knee after asking. Veer let him. Following a good turn did not require his own injured joint to attempt one in sympathy.

---

At the next reset, both corners were quiet.

A boy carrying the scoring board had nearly wiped the first mark while adding the second. The steward steadied it for him, counted the two separate scores aloud and gave it back. The crowd did not need a phantom point added by a moving thumb.

The bearded contestant stood a little closer to the centre than he had at the first bell.

Yellow came towards him without hurrying.

The Prakriti man's palm lowered, stopped just above the earth and rose again.

Yellow's foot checked.

Nothing had moved beneath it.

The man used the pause to come up through his own legs and send the free fingers straight towards the tab above the little curve.

Yellow caught the first approach on the guard's face. He turned it out of the path instead of driving the arm back into the man.

His opponent was already changing feet.

The second touch came with the other hand, bare of soil because it had never quite reached the ground.

The shoulder tab flattened beneath two fingertips.

Two–one to the bearded man.

Veer laughed aloud before the official had finished calling it.

---

The spectator beside Veer did not laugh.

"Tumhaare waale ka point gaya."

"Dekha."

"Phir?"

Veer watched the bearded contestant return to the mark, the hand which had never touched the earth still held openly at his side.

"Mera Kul hai," he said. "Meri aankh band nahi hai."

Below, Yellow was asking his corner for the water. The free fingers made a small twisting gesture, repeating the last change of hands. His companion answered with an empty-handed movement, no live shape raised beside the people waiting for the next round.

The next call brought both men back before the argument on the verandah could ask either of them to represent it.

---

Yellow showed a wide route towards the grass.

His opponent did not lower the palm immediately. He came across the centre to keep the shoulder targets in view, using the small difference in their reach to make Yellow take the longer arc.

Yellow stopped at the edge of that arc.

The bearded man stopped too.

For a moment there were two upright bodies and a clean stretch of earth between them, neither belonging to the shape the other had hoped to fight.

Yellow put the leading foot down closer to the man's unplanted side.

The pale curve appeared at an angle across his own forearm. It covered the nearest tab but left the far one apparently open.

The bearded contender changed hands again.

This time Yellow had waited for the change. He moved with the first hand instead of looking after the second, let the curve go before it could squeeze the space between them and touched the shoulder passing closest to his free fingers.

The referee stopped them with his body turned sideways through the gap.

Two each.

Neither athlete tried to turn a well-read exchange into an extra second of possession.

---

The scheduled medical pause came before the next point.

Yellow's holding forearm was warm and tired. He answered the clinician's questions without letting the corner answer for him. The examination found no new reason to end his bout at that stage; he took the rest and the instructions rather than treating the permission as proof that the arm could no longer fail him.

Across the ring, the bearded man held out the working palm. The little indentation remained. Grit was cleaned away, the fingers checked, then he sat while the helper brought water.

Their corners got time to look at the bodies doing the work.

The crowd had to wait.

Veer used the pause to adjust the knee with the volunteer's help. He checked the clock. The session was still within his agreed interval. If it ran beyond it, the driver would not become responsible for deciding whether an interesting match outweighed the clinician's limit.

The man beside him asked whether he thought Yellow's next guard would be bigger.

"Chhota hi kaafi kharcha kar raha hai," Veer said.

---

The fifth exchange began nearer the usable grass.

The bearded man offered the high shoulder, then lowered through his legs as Yellow came for it. The working palm reached the earth this time. Yellow saw it land and shortened the step.

The runner lifted at the outside of his boot.

He did not try to catch it on the guard again. He gave the foot a clear place inside the ring and turned the curve across the approaching free hand.

The first touch went into the pale face and stopped short.

The bearded contender shifted his planted weight. A short pull through the existing runner narrowed the place Yellow had left for the next foot. It did not take the boot prisoner. It made staying where he was a worse answer than moving.

Yellow moved.

He cleared the strand. His whole foot came down beyond the chalk.

The referee stopped them before the man could follow the shoulder.

Three–two to the Prakriti contender.

---

The runner settled lower after it was released.

Its bend near the base did not straighten with the athlete's spine. The grounds helper marked where the surface had been disturbed and checked that the next approach would not be taken over a loose clod. The living strip remained a finite strip which had already been worked, not a reward endlessly replaced between bells.

The bearded man wiped his mouth with the corner cloth and looked at Yellow.

Yellow nodded once, still breathing through his mouth.

Neither looked surprised to find the other there.

On the verandah, the spectator who had wanted a bigger guard shifted impatiently. Veer watched the modest pale shape vanish as its owner went to sit, and thought how easily a crowd could demand more from a forearm than it had ever asked of its own.

The volunteer asked if he wanted to leave at the next interval.

"Time hai abhi," Veer said after checking again. "Jab hoga, chalenge."

---

In the hospital that morning, Rudra asked for his college messages.

The volunteer had been reading the practical ones to him: family, ward, next appointment, the people still asking where to send a bag. He wanted the other group as well, the one which had once seemed capable of filling an entire day with complaints about an unfinished assignment.

There were more messages than he could manage in one interval.

He asked for the latest handout and the actual work still outstanding, not every missed conversation at once. The first reply asked whether he was back.

He dictated the answer.

*Abhi hospital. Notes bhej do. Waapas aane ki date nahi hai.*

He listened to the readback before it was sent. The last sentence hurt more than asking for the notes. Leaving it out would have made somebody else begin arranging a return he could not promise.

A file arrived while the nurse was settling the ankle after its prescribed movement. Rudra asked to see the first page on the phone propped in front of him. The nurse helped him find a comfortable position, then reminded him to take the rest they had agreed.

The page was full of ordinary things he did not immediately remember.

For once, not understanding something did not mean that it lived beneath his skin.

---

Vedant came during the ward's agreed visiting interval.

He read the first question from the handout and waited for Rudra to answer. Rudra stared at him.

"Aapko aata hai?"

"Nahi. Isliye poochha."

Rudra laughed, then asked for the phone to be lowered. His supported wrist had stayed out of the task; fatigue had not.

The morning's clinical observations remained in the ward record. There had been no further visible flare during those checks. The drawings stayed available for the team, not displayed as an exercise he had to reproduce because a visitor was willing to watch.

Vedant asked whether he wanted to talk about the last change.

"Baad mein," Rudra said. "Abhi doosra sawaal padho."

He did not need an answer immediately. He wanted a person to remain beside him while a different question existed.

Vedant read it, admitted he still did not know and stayed until the nurse called the next rest interval.

---

In Mirzapur, Ira chose a short piece of the same ordinary college material for her next permitted right-eye interval.

Sitara helped arrange a clear copy. Ira read only the selected part, left eye covered, then put it down while she could still distinguish choosing to stop from being forced to by pain.

Aadhya listened to what she had understood.

They got one part wrong between them. The correction came from the actual notes, and for a few minutes the argument was only about a page. No one needed Ira to see two versions of the room or Aadhya to put a palm through the floor before they were allowed to be useful to each other.

They sent Rudra the corrected answer with a warning that he should check it himself.

His reply arrived later.

*Tum dono ne milke galat kiya tha?*

Aadhya asked for the response to be sent exactly as she said it.

*Teamwork.*

Ira laughed and gave the page back. The rest interval started on time.

---

At the porter's home, the morning took longer than the distance from the bed to the door suggested it should.

His companion moved the water where the good hand could reach it. The supported arm was not to be used to pull himself upright. The clinic's review was later that day, and the vehicle had been confirmed instead of left as a promise somebody might remember after work.

He had the money for the completed introduction in a separate fold. It would help. It would not become a replacement wrist because he counted it twice.

Someone from the club called to ask whether he wanted the public result sent to him.

He wanted to know who was playing first.

The bearded Prakriti man and the Rakshak contestant who had beaten Green.

The porter said they could send the score. He did not have to hate the ring to know that he would not be entering it again on this injury.

Before the call ended, he asked for the time of his own review to be repeated to the person taking him. That mattered more than being told his courage had already healed the old strain.

---

At the akhada, the referee checked that both contestants were ready for the sixth exchange.

The bearded man stood three–two ahead. The grass at his side had a new low bend where the runner had been worked. Yellow looked at the mark, then at the hand which had drawn it.

He opened and closed the free hand while the holding forearm rested low at his side.

His corner gave him the water. He rinsed his mouth, returned the bottle and came to the line.

From the verandah, Veer could see the slight tiredness in both men's knees when they settled into their waiting positions.

Neither had changed into a larger version of himself.

The next point would cost what their remaining bodies and the remaining strip of earth could actually provide.

---

Yellow opened the sixth exchange by drawing his opponent away from the source.

He showed the approach towards the grass, then changed the leading foot before the Prakriti man could settle beside it. His opponent stayed upright instead of reaching for earth which was now farther away.

Yellow stayed with the nearer shoulder.

The pale guard covered only the near approach. He brought the free hand towards the far tab, stopped it when the bearded man turned and used the turn itself to carry him towards the edge.

He gained ground through three changes of angle, until the chalk was behind the other man's rear foot.

No shove.

Two final advancing steps, each small enough to stop.

The bearded contender tried to pass across the front of the guard. Yellow gave him that opening and kept his own foot inside the chalk. The other man's retreat crossed the boundary before his fingertips found the shoulder he had been reaching for.

The line judge raised an arm.

Three each.

Yellow let the shape go while the other man was still bringing the outside foot back. He did not leave a live edge waiting where someone would put his body after the stop.

---

Veer had lifted the good hand before he remembered what he meant to do with it.

He brought it down against his own thigh, gently enough not to make the gesture a problem for the supported shoulder. It was not a grand enough clap to cross the ring.

He enjoyed it anyway.

Beside him, the volunteer checked whether the next pause would be a good time to move the knee cloth. Veer said yes. Nobody needed to pretend the small ache had disappeared because the score had become interesting.

Below, the bearded man was talking to his corner. He drew a short arc in the air with an empty hand, then a straight line across it.

Yellow was watching too.

No teacher stood outside the ring handing either of them the next answer.

---

The seventh exchange began without a shape.

The bearded man came forward upright, leaving the grass behind him. Yellow waited for the lowered palm. When it did not come, he raised the little guard towards the near shoulder and gave himself room to turn.

The man used that room.

One hand brushed the outside of Yellow's forearm, moving with it rather than clamping the wrist. His other hand travelled towards the high tab.

Yellow turned the curve across the fingers.

The first hand changed from a brush into a brief guiding pressure above the elbow. The man stepped beside the guard instead of trying to break it. His free fingers reached the shoulder before Yellow could bring the far side away.

The referee was close enough to see the cloth depress.

Stop.

Four–three to the Prakriti contender.

The guiding hand opened immediately. No lock remained on the elbow. Yellow stepped out without being dragged into a throw for the crowd's sake.

The two men looked at each other, close enough now that each could hear the other's breathing.

One point from a final.

Not one point from owning the person who had made it hard.

---

The man near Veer's chair stood with both hands on the rope.

A steward asked him to move back. He did, reluctantly, still telling Yellow to finish it as though the deficit were a personal insult delivered to everyone on the verandah.

Veer looked past him to the clinic side. The medical helper had fresh water and the clean cloth ready. The referee checked both corners before giving them another mark.

The bearded man could win on this exchange.

Yellow needed it simply to keep playing.

The difference changed the sound from the crowd, not the targets sewn onto the two vests.

---

Yellow came towards the living strip on his eighth approach.

He held the guard higher than before. His opponent lowered through the knees and put the working palm down. The source was there, the runner already near Yellow's next step.

Yellow showed the heel.

The strand tightened across the place it ought to land.

He lifted the foot before setting it into the change. The boot came down a little inside the ring, close enough to keep the bearded man waiting beside the planted palm.

The free hand came for Yellow's shoulder.

Yellow did not retreat.

He turned that shoulder out of the fingers' path and let the curve cover the returning hand. His own free arm stayed close until the turn was almost finished.

Then he reached for the tab above the planted arm.

The bearded man began to rise. The root slackened as his fingers left the earth, but his shoulder had already come into the touch.

The referee stopped them.

Four each.

---

For a moment, the crowd was nothing but air leaving bodies.

Then the shouting returned twice as loud.

Yellow went to his corner without the guard. The bearded man took his working hand to the medical helper and asked for the grit to be cleaned before it dried in the folds.

Veer saw both movements before he looked towards the score.

Eight marks. Four beside each number.

No borrowed point from a famous name. No absent boy made necessary to the two men who had actually earned them.

The last point was still unspent.

---

The referee did not ring the next bell into the shouting.

He sent both men to the medical pause first.

Yellow held the forearm out before being asked. The clinician checked the tired muscles, the wrist and the answers about pain or altered feeling. He had fatigue to report, not a new numb hand. The permitted movements were still there. The clinician wanted the next exchange stopped at once if that changed, not another point first and the account afterward.

The bearded man's palm was reddened from the earth. He let the helper clean it, then answered for himself when the clinician asked whether he could continue. The examination and the limit belonged together. The official did not send him back merely because the corner wanted a final.

Each man could stop now. Neither asked the other to do it for him.

When the clinician gave the current decision, the referee brought them together for the final-point instructions.

---

The grounds helper checked the working strip during the pause.

One short pulled section was excluded from the usable edge. The remaining rooted length was inspected with both contestants present. Nobody brought in a fresh carpet of grass to make the earlier strain disappear. The earth still held the marks of the first eight points.

Yellow looked down the line of his bare holding forearm, then at the grass.

The bearded man put an empty hand over the remaining source without pressing. Then he stood back. That would be the place he could reach if the exchange took him there. It would not follow him into the centre because he had one more point to win.

Both men went back to the marks.

On the verandah, Veer checked the time and the waiting vehicle message. The session was still inside the shorter limit. He told the volunteer they would leave at its end even if the bout had not found a winner.

The volunteer folded the knee cloth back into place.

---

A child near the lower step asked whether both men had nearly won.

"Dono ko ek chahiye," his father said.

The child considered that, then stopped shouting for a moment, as though the arithmetic had made it harder to decide what kindness ought to sound like.

Veer watched Yellow take his place. He wanted the Rakshak contestant to win. He no longer needed to make the other man's last four points disappear in order to admit it.

He could see the bearded man's chest moving beneath the tab. He could see the small care with which Yellow placed the holding arm before raising a shape.

Whatever happened next, there would still be two bodies to look after when the noise had finished with them.

---

The bell sounded.

Yellow came off the mark first.

His opponent met him upright, away from the grass. Two feints passed without a touch. Yellow brought the little curve up, lowered it and let it go when the other man refused the gap.

No point.

They separated by a step, not to opposite corners.

The bearded contender moved nearer the permitted source. Yellow followed just far enough to make turning away cost the shoulder.

The palm went down.

A runner rose across the next foot's approach. Yellow stopped short, then lowered the guard towards it as he had in the opening exchange.

The crowd recognized the position and shouted before either man had finished choosing it.

Veer saw the difference.

Yellow's back foot was under the hip this time. His body had not followed the lowered arm into the other man's reach. The free hand stayed close to his own chest, ready to move when the holding arm turned.

He had not come to repeat the first lost point.

---

The runner fell across the low curve.

The bearded man's fingers pressed into the soil. The strand tightened beneath the rim, close to the earth, and the guard stopped at the angle both men now remembered.

Yellow let it turn a little instead of wrenching against it.

His free foot moved inside the turn. He was trying to pass towards the shoulder above the planted arm, the route which had tied the score, while keeping the caught curve low.

The Prakriti man shifted through his legs without lifting the working palm. His free hand came towards Yellow's nearer shoulder.

Yellow met the reaching forearm with his own bare free palm and turned the hand aside, just short of the tab.

For that brief contact, both free hands were occupied. The guard stayed low, caught against the strand while Yellow stepped farther towards the planted side. The referee moved along the outside edge to keep the hands in sight.

The bearded man drew through the rooted strand again.

The loop shortened. The base of the grass pulled against earth already loosened by the earlier exchanges. Yellow's holding forearm trembled, but he kept the curve small and began to turn past its face.

The soil beneath the planted heel of the palm rose in a shallow ridge.

---

Veer saw the ridge before he understood it.

The bearded man's weight had come forward over the working hand. Yellow was already bringing the low curve up with the turn of his body, trusting the resistance beneath its rim for the moment it would take him to pass.

A pale fibre showed at the runner's base.

It parted.

The small clod beneath the planted palm shifted. The crouching man's shoulder and chin dipped towards the arm which had been held low a breath earlier.

Yellow's guard came free at once.

It was still coming up.

"Chhodo!" Veer called from the chair.

The referee's hands rose. The medical helper stood from the table beyond him.

Yellow's free fingers had not reached the tab. The board still showed four beside each number.

The live rim was rising beneath the other man's unprotected jaw.
