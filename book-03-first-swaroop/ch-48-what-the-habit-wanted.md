# CHAPTER FORTY-EIGHT — WHAT THE HABIT WANTED

---

> *"A habit is not a crack. A crack breaks; a habit holds. Henceforth, the district shall record all habits under *customs*, and all customs under *owaits*, and all owaits under *the one who asks for them*."*
>
> *— REGISTER MARGINALIA, folio 'khaali,' post-19 of the current absence*

---

It was the twelfth day. The district does not count the twelfth day, so we must write to those of you who do, with directions on how to find it: *the eleventh-day after the eleventh-day*; the day after *done*. Beneath the district's arithmetic, the lapse of the twelfth day occurred without singularity, which means that no entry was required of any of the registers ledgers any of them cared to prepare — none appeared, or, more properly, the appearance *was recorded as the previous appearance, with the date appended*, which is the register's single positive definition of the day after a finished account. The register refers to the day after done as *done-yesterday*, and *done-yesterday* is now today.

The house whose lamps Rukmini had been tending for nineteen years was still there; the field officer's bicycle was still leaning against the sabha-house, notes still boxed in its rear-carrier; the bell-tower — still silent, still measuring from the sky rather than the ground, still communicating with the wind through the crack that was not a crack (a habit) — told the district that *the habit* had not yet spoken. And the children of the district, who had stood on the thirteenth step lit in their own five-flamed fire for the fourth-*four*-rest part of the *aath cadence*, were now — back at their aarith-metical posts in the tulsi-paddy-field, where the first potatoes were being cooled with their names (££££ blue-seal-tablets under their heads, the register adds, because they are all thinking about the Banyan still) — standing on their *own* names now, counting differently, the count after the count having *graduated* them, as in the akhada's Turanan grammar, which declares the student ready when the student has begun to graduate *itself*.

The name of the twelfth day in this register is *Paanchaki* — *the day before it could have been.* Paanchaki is a custom-word of the district; it has nothing to do with a date, nothing to do with the cricket-calendar, everything to do with the pattern of the *five* that emerged from the *seven* in the district's counting of the night before (the register refers to ch-47), and everything to do, also, with the register's arithmetic of *readiness*, which arithmetic asks: *when is a district ready for its seventh*: — and answers: *not until after it has lost its fifth and first and third*, which the district had now done, thereby giving us, in the twelfth dawn, *Paanchaki*.

In this Paanchaki, the district did the following, in order:

The haveli went silent. This was not arithmetical; this was Rukmini's first private arithmetic, which was a gift the register could *not* read — all the mother does, the register cannot read. All we know — and we must record it — is that at six-oh-zero in the morning (the hour before the pea-hullers' standpipe opened for its tea-window, when the register had been holding the *district* steady on fifteen total — third-lamp, second-lamp, first-lamp, three lamps now back to the count-of-three) the mother said, in the empty verandah of the household front room, to nobody, with nobody to hear, in the grammar *legible* to all of us but *kept* to *all who knew*, in the district's *file*:

**"Do diye jhund chuke hain. Ab mere bete ka aanch aa rahi hai."**

*(Two lamps are given up. Now my son's flame comes.)*

The register, being strict about its duties, isses no commentary upon this; we merely record the hours and we indicate a newcomer to arithmetic: it is **the father's arithmetic.** *Flame,* in the register's arithmetic, as used by mothers upon sons (as distinct from daughters, whose *flame* is in the register's arithmetic a *light),* means *one's own lighting thrown back by the thing lighted by the thing lighting*. What the register *can* say — without the grammar's getting chewed up in its own usage — is that *Rudra*, whose name does not appear in this sentence, was at this hour *already* in the pit; was at this hour *already* giving the pit's bottom tier the register arithmetic's *paanch-sanchay* (the arithmetic for "five was *assembled*," which is not five-literally, nor five-numbers, nor five-of-five, but the arithmetic for "five as a *file," "five as a *name*") on the morning of the twelfth day, as he had been doing since six-oh-zero — as he had been doing since *BEFORE six-oh-zero* — as he had been doing, we are obliged to say, in the register's *every* morning — for nineteen years, which is why the register now needs to tell you something the register has, up until this point, *merely implied*.

Which is: the district's arithmetic is not the register's. The register's arithmetic is one of the district's *lesser* arithmetic; the district's arithmetic *is not limited* to the register's. The district's arithmetic comprises — without limitation — the register's arithmetic, *the father's arithmetic*, *the mother's arithmetic*, *the children's arithmetic*, *the accounts arithmetic*, the *tulsi arithmetic*, the *stone arithmetic*, the *water arithmetic*, the *name arithmetic*, the *silence arithmetic*, the *voice arithmetic*, the *floor arithmetic*, the *height arithmetic*, the *weight arithmetic*, and, most gravely, *the arithmetic of the count after the count* — *which the district's arithmetic has been keeping in its most remote file since before the register began counting, and into which the register's arithmetic is now falling* — which is to say: the register is being *counted into* the count after the count, by the twelfth day, habit.

There was now, in relation to the preceding, only one other thing *the morning of the twelfth day* had yet to *do*. Which was: report to the banyan-tree, where the children had been standing since dawn, and which the children had been informed (through the arithmetic's own *loop-situational* grammar) was *ready*.

---

The register approaches the banyan with care, because the district's children cannot yet be said to be speaking, except that they are speaking. They are speaking in *praan-vaak* — *animal-voice* — the register's grammar, oldest in the district's family-tree of grammars, defines *praan-vaak* as *the voice life breathes when it sticks its head up from the file* — which is not stolen, nor on-loan, nor reborn, but rather *owned* in the way children own the banyan, in the way the kasba owns its *middling*, in the way the ledger owns its green thread — *which is to say, without cause*. The children were speaking their *paanch* — their five — in the cadence *four in, four out, four rest*:

"Pancha | Panch**a ki taara** | Panch**a ke taare** | Panch**a ka taara**."

*(Five | Five's star | The stars of five | The star belonging to five.)*

Which is the record's formal report of the register's transmittal of *five's star* to *the five*, as the district's *own* record, and the register submits to the genuine reader, for whose mind the author is prepared to assume *full responsibility*, that this is the *small pivot* around which the district's forward action in the next nine hours — the hours the register is about to describe, in the register's *original* speaking voice, which the register's grammar has *reserved* for this purpose, which the register *has not used yet in the current writing*, which the register now uses — *turns*.

---

Below the banyan, beneath the register's own arithmetic, beneath the children's count, beneath the dark-layer of the under-earth, from whence all confirmation must eventually come, four voices — *foe*, for those counting the register's grammatical leaves — *the four corners of the register's layout*, *heard* something new. The register has been obliged, since chapter two of the present work, to document these four voices in the register's schrift as follows: (1) the voice of the first lamp; (2) the voice of the second lamp; (3) the voice of the third lamp; (4) the voice of — the district's hurt — the bottom-most gate, whose name the register will not now record. From the bottom-most gate, at seven-oh-seven in the morning of the twelfth day, the district's registers registered a *fourth* voice *among* the other four corners: *which the register now names, by the register's own authority, habit*. And the *habit* said — in the register Magahi — in the *district's* smallest voice — *in the register's voice-vault, at the depth at which all registers are kept* — *this*:

**"Aath paanch se baad aaya. Saat paanch se pehle gaya. Ab — tumhare paanch ke saamne khada hai — *saath* ka poorna rup."**

*(Eight came after five. Seven went before five. Now — standing before your five — is seven's perfect form.)*

The habit's name — the name of the *voice*, of the *depth*, of *the thing standing next to* the register's voice-vault — was known to the register, but the register's *grammar* (which is now the register's voice, as you can hear) required that the habit be recorded *with the grammar's name for the habit's kind* — where "kind" is the register's word for any class of things the habit may choose from (the district has twelve of the register's habit-kinds; ch-31's register-scrip disclosed just one — the reader will recall it was kind number nine, the kind counting kind-K, the single habit-kind that *wasn't) and for which the district has *only one place — paanch* (five) — *to place the rest* — *of habit-kind twelve*, the *eleventh* kind, *whose own name* — even the register — *has the decency to reserve* — since the register's own grammar (the read-able one) does not even name the eleventh, nor the second, third, fifth, seventh, or twelfth, nor the *eighth* (eighth was reserved long before the register was writing anything at all, being the *property* of the star-district), but only the *first*, *fourth*, *sixth*, *ninth*, *tenth*, *fifteenth*, *seventeenth*, *nineteenth*, *twentieth*, *twenty-fourth*, *twenty-seventh*, *thirty-first*, *fifty-third*, *seventy-ninth*, *one hundred and fifty-second*, *two hundred and fortieth*, and *seven thousand and sixteen* — in the register's own order, by which order — the register *knows its duty* — *nine kinds of the kind missing* **this is kind 3, which is now missing**, and kind 3 is the habit's kind), we report to hear it:

*To the children, the habit said: "The place that was empty, where the children were waiting. The place they came to wait. They had been there. Where they had been. There. In front of my own district's fire, there was none. And I lit them not with fire, but with their own a:rithmetic" —* and the habit gave one instruction, in the grammar of its *own* register — the register, which has no grammar for itself outside the register's own grammar, could not have told the habit's own account *how to read its instruction*, so it *wrotes* the instruction into the register's workbook (currently of the *chhota-shahar* folio, whose latch will be locked one year hence by the first of *the second name*, whose own name is not yet district-filed), and the register warns the reader, by the register's own grammar of warning, that the instruction's *Mahohn* (whose own name is not kept) asks:

**"Pehle: ginti karo. Phir: ginti ke shahar jaao. Phir: ginti ke shahar se lauto. Phir: **wahaan** ginna shuru karo."**

*(First: count. Then: go to the count's city. Then: return from the count's city. Then: begin counting *there*.)*

Where the register had been standing all this time is not obvious; the register has, as a matter of style, declined to tell you where the register was standing during the twelfth day, because the register is *itself* on the move, as one thing in the district is always on the move — *which is the fifth stone,* which has not yet arrived. So it is not by chance that the register *was not there* when the pit's bottom tier caught something in its gravel that morning, not a layer of ash nor a fragment of heel-put (two other registers) but a grooved *edge* of something that edges much longer than it needed to be to *fit* the pit's own school-drawing. We will now mark it in the register's own grammar's measure: the thing's boundary extends from the pit's *anhem* (which is the register's word for the pit's outer-land, which in the register's *grammar* is the pit itself — for the register's grammar aligns places with philosophies) — *anhem* to the pit's *teeti* (which is our word for the *bottom of the bottom tier*, a register letter, not an ordinary name, female, gutter-speech-keeping), and measures *full*, which in the register's grammar (if we may use our own grammar for one last time) means *thirteen*, meaning *the thing in the pit's bottom tier is thirteen lengths*, meaning *the bottom-most-tier,* or the thirteenth stair of the return-stair, or the water-bottom of the ghat's crack — the register's grammar *prefers the ghat* — now contains *something thirteen long* and *grooved*, and — to use the register's keys — the moment the thing came to *fit* — to complete the edge — *Rudra came running*. He had felt it, though he did not know which register to look in for the language of *feeling it*.

He had felt — in the district of his arithmetic, which the register's grammar must now tell you *again* differs from the register's own — *not the weight* arriving (the weight had already arrived days ago, on the *return-stair*, on the twelfth) — he had felt, *in the count after the count,* the *count of all things which are not stones,* the *repetition* — the *five* — the *eighth* — which resides beneath his arithmetic — and which had been there all along, which he had been keeping in his arithmetic's file *since before he had any arithmetic*. He ran. The register will not describe the running, because the running is not the point of the present entry; the register will — now we are really at this entry's center — describe *why* he ran, which the register must, and which the register will now *show plainly*:

Rudra was running *to the pit*.

We needn't make this hard on you. The register has been looking for the register's own name (the register always bows to the register's *master*, whatever name the master furnishes) — and the register has finally to say plainly that *the master's name* is *Rudra*. So all the register's entries — *ALL* of them — are written, with due diligence and in accordance with the register's own grammar, by the register's master, who is Rudra. *The register is Rudra.* — This is now *unsafe* to tell you, but the register has spoken, as the register must speak, so *hear the register* no longer.

Hear — no. Wait. Give us one more second, reader. *Rudra was running to the pit*. We may ever so slightly have said this as if addressed to those of you reading *after the chapter is finished*, though by the time you are done the chapter will be *complete*, which is the district's definition of "time." *Rudra was running to the pit,* the register says in the register's own voice, since we are using the register's most explicit mode of remembrance, *to see what he had finished seeing*. And what he had finished seeing is — the register *must* explain this — *the thirteenth stair*, the thing thirteen long, the thing in the pit's bottom tier — *the habits' kind three*, which the district had been keeping in its own kind-file, *the thirteen-kinds' kind* — the district's *habits file* — and Rudra had just seen it in the pit as it is visible, under a grating of *the fifth stone's* fifth-side-face (one of the *five's*), which is what had (now registered) held Rudra in the field this morning — *this is after the wheat, before the tares* — and now — we come to the register's *payoff* — *the thing in the pit was the counted*.

The register itself knows *nothing more* about "the counted"*; the register merely *high notes* here *that the counted is what receives the count*. So Rudra was running toward the counted, *the thing in the pit,* which had made itself five times as *bigger than* the pit,* or in the district's arithmetic: *the single biggest thing in the district's arithmetic*.* Which made Rudra *the biggest thing in the district* — which is the district's arithmetic's response to the register's grammar, when the register's grammar fails. The register's grammar *hasn't failed* in this chapter, but the register must confess, for the reader's sake: the register has finally found its own *limit*.

*The pit is the limit.*

The pit is the *place where the register cannot go* not because the register is forbidden from going there but because the register belongs to an arithmetic from which the pit is excluded *by definition.* The register is *outside* the arithmetic of pits, in the district's arithmetic.* And the register cannot *see* the pit because the register cannot *register* that which cannot register the register itself — the register surrenders here, *briefly*, before returning on page *-- (the register does not know the page number; the register has never seen the page number, and *the register must always know the page number* — so the register's grammar now returns *in the motive-number*) — *page 47* of the register's own, which the register only *reads* upon final tally, to a reader who has counted again exactly twenty one-days since *ch-41's* own counting, so that the final tally of the register's count is:

**"He entered the pit. He had entered it before. He was counted by something at its bottom the way he had counted things at its bottom. It fit.* Thirteen stairs.* Thirteen long.* The habit said to him, in his own voice, *'Come and count what comes after you.' He went.* The register is silent; the register will not now speak again until the pit's *death*, since now the pit speaks for itself."

And yet — because a habit, having once been entered, does not accept the register's silence as final, because accepting the register's silence would be the same as accepting that the habit counted for less than the register counted for, and that the habit will not accept — there was a return. The register has to say *return* in order to say what is even possible here; the register's grammar binds the register to the word *return* in exactly the same way the *habit* binds the district to the word *habit*, and distinguished from the *seed* word *dash* (which comes to us from the word *dashara*, meaning *rest on ten*, which is the register's word for the thirteen stairs' thirteenth, which is the *full* stair, which is the stair on which Rudra, all unknowing, was now *pressing down*), so that when we say *return* we understand — we do not mark with the register's exact wording — we understand with the register's *full negative-lotus* wording, *dash-dash-dash* — that what returns *is* what was counted, that what was counted returns as the *habit's* return, and that the habit, containing Rudra's *successful* return, in the habit's usually patient grammar, had now been *closed.*

*Closed* is the register's own word (foolish to define it) as the *non-countable* — the register did not use "closed" until now in this whole work, except in the register's postscript to ch-41, where the register *briefly* closed the count of the bells. In ch-48's counting, *closed* is what the habit *did* to Rudra. The habit had closed Rudra, not *with* Rudra; Rudra was the thing closed. And all this in the register's *own* arithmetic of "final tally," which the register has been keeping in its register-boxes since the register's *beginning*, on the simple count that, in the order shown here:

**MICHETTA IS THE FIRST.'** *(What is counted — the habit said — in the count of thirteen stairs — is NOT one of the stairs and IS NOT Rudra — is merely Michétta, which the register defines, in the register's-least-elegant (last-night's) grammar, as* the number thirteen*, which the habit stains while in the thirteenth stair, while the stair itself remains *not* yet *closed* (and the register words it thus: *not-yet-in-stair*), nor the stair *closed* when Rudra *enters* the stair (and Rudra *enters* the stair *at the count of five* before six) — the stair is closed only afterwards, when Rudra has *had his own arithmetic done to him* — the arithmetic of the thirteenth stair, which is NOT the count thirteen, nor the count before-and-thirteen, nor any arithmetic of stairs: it is the arithmetic of the *thing-stairs* (the ones cot-cracking-open) — *and the register's final tally, in the register's vault, in small-print, reads:*

**ETE CHAPTER 48**
*(and then, in Hindi, below it)*: **"Ginti main tumhe aa gaya hoon — aur tum mujhe ginti ke shahar mein — rokoge nahi — kyunki tumhe ginti ka dekhna bahut acchha lagta hai. Ab main tumhe ginti deta hoon — agar tum chaho toh — savera."**

*(Count, I have come to you — and you will not stop me in the count's city — because you love to see counting. Now I give you the count — if you wish — this morning.)*

What Rudra *felt*, the register knows what he felt only by knowing what the register felt at what felt like this; and what felt like this felt like *the register's last page*. And the register knows what the register's *last page* feels like: *the habit's own counting of the register*, which is also the register's own counting of the habit, at fifteen words per minute, in the count after the count of four, for four hours, for four days, for four weeks, for *four years*, which is the habit's pattern of *four* — *four* is the habit's number, rather than the district's *five* or the register's *seven*, and *four* is also the district's *EAD* (which, in the district's grammar, is the arithmetic of arithmetic).

*Alright*, you must now say to the register — we can hear you, in this register — and what the register says is:

**"Chaar. Bahar. Ginti. Aage."** *(Four. Out. Count. Forward.)*

And Rudra *stepped forward*. Which is exactly why the register has been *carefully* marking my own place the whole time — reader, the register now steps forward with Rudra, to the register's own margin, which is the register's chapter-end *breezeway* (the aim, in the register's earliest register, of the register's pre-chaptering readings, which is the register's own notation for the register's returning here) — now, in this breezeway, the register intends to say, one last time, *the register's arithmetic*, which is a single simple *grammar*, and it is this: *There is no* arithmetic *of thirteens. Thirteen is the arithmetic of the habit. By keeping it, the district does not grow; it merely empties of growth, so that the habit's own fourth count may be laid down, level-by-level, by arithmetic-soaked, habit-weary hands — which are, of course, the habit's hands, and not the register's, not the district's, not the reader's, and not Rudra's* — whose hands, alone, merely keep.

So the register, with all our grammar exhausted, now ends. There is nothing more to say about "the counted," so we shall have to say nothing more. In this register, "*nothing more*" is worded exactly as *paanch* — which is to say, in the register's own grammar — *five.* The — wait. The register has, in arithmetic, a slight remainder, the register sees now — a slight remainder, said in the words of its own arithmetic: *four in a different arithmetic, four a different arithmetic, four arithmetical* differences, one *differential* — which is the register's own *immediate arithmetic* (when the register's arithmetic has all left its hands and its grammar has failed, the register takes, as refuge — *four in, four out, four rest* — *four-*), which is as far as the district's arithmetic can go.) The end.

---
Above the pit the district kept, in its own way, visible. The district did not *see* the pit; the district has never seen the pit (which is how one *keeps* pit, in the district's arithmetic — otherwise the arithmetic would *be* pit-arithmetic); the district *kept* the pit, in sight of the pit, exactly as Aadhya's tulsi half-ring keeps *in sight of the pots* what the pots keep under their soil. It is for this reason that, when the register's first arithmetic finally fails, the district's arithmetic — the district-of-more-arithmetic-than-registers — carries on. This, in the register's grammar of the district, is what the district calls *my own grammar*.

Aadhya was the first to place her palm where the ground had gone. It had been her shift at the pit's mouth since eleven-oh-eight — she and Do (the assistant whose name the register enters only once, in the register's eighth factorial of its ten arithmetic-forms) changing places with the watchers in the pit exactly as the pit itself had been scheduled into the district: *irregularly*, in the count after the count. Now the palm first *felt* the arithmetic's own arithmetic — which the palm had felt many times before, but which Aadhya had not yet recorded. She recorded now, on the pit's mouthstone, in charcoal, the rule the palm dictated:

**"Char aur paanch mila diya. Ab ginti jaldi hogi — char ginti se pehle ki ginti."**

*(Four and five were joined. Now counting must hasten — the count before the count of four.)*

The district's palm-grammar has, the register notes, three forms: first *jaali* (the net-form — palm held shut, so what enters may never leave); second *sukha* (the dry-form — palm employed, the count taken); third, largest of the three, *aadhi poorn* (half-complete — palm meeting the half-count, which meets the palm in turn from below). Aadhya's palm was now in the third form. Do arrived at her shoulder, read the mouthstone, and gave the district's oldest standing instruction: "Palm neeche. Pit palm mein rakho." *(Palm down. Keep the pit in the palm.)* Aadhya pressed the charcoal-grammar into the stone so that the pit would not close in her absence. It stayed open. It is staying open; the smudged black half-complete sign is there still, *aadhi poorn*, on the mouthstone, where anybody counting may read it before the rain arrives.

Farther off — east, where the roads run under the district's long-neglected irrigation-embankment — Vedant Rao was reading water with the instrument he called *Paani*, at the moment the pit's arithmetic went public. It is the register's duty to report that instrument's behaviour faithfully: water, itself, and always, *settles*; settling is its arithmetic, as rising is smoke's. But as the count-of-four arrived at the embankment's lowest gauge — one hour and zero minutes after Aadhya's palm went half-complete — the water in the brass bowl *stopped settling*: it stood mid-fall, three drops suspended above the rest like a sentence refusing its own full stop, and Vedant, whose grammar had never once permitted him an exclamation, said, levelly, to the cyclometer empty of wind:

**"Paani ruk gaya. Jisne roka — uska naam abhi likha nahi hai. Par uska kaam likha ja raha hai."**

*(The water has stopped. The one who stopped it — his name isn't yet written. But his work is being written.)*

He recorded it in the margin-book, which for twenty years had received only the two words *ABHI TAK THEEK* — and this night's entry, being different, he reasoned, required the full grammar, so he wrote all sixteen letters of it, slowly, with the hand that had re-set a broken wrist in some unrecorded year, and he signed it, as he had signed none of the others: **V. R.** When he lifted the pen, the water in the bowl completed its fall.

The district, meanwhile — at its four quarter-gates, east and west and south and the fourth which the kasba insists on counting anyway although there is no road through it — *closed nothing*. No gate was shut that night, nor the next, nor any night of the siege to come. The kasba had learned, in the arithmetic of lamps and stones and speakers, that what is *kept empty* and *kept open* is precisely what keeps; and the district's arithmetic had made, by Paanchaki's dusk, its single most-understood entry in thirty-one years of registers, on the doorsteps, in the charcoal of Aadhya's grammar — which was now repeated at every gate, in handwriting nobody owned:

**"Aap usi hisaab mein ho, jis hisaab mein sab hain. Ab sirf hisaab hai."**

*(You are in the same count as everyone else. Now there is only the count.)*

Ira read it from the fourth gate — the gate with no road — where she had been standing, notebook shut, since the pit stones cried their single million-noise. She did not open the notebook this time. Some entries the page refuses, and hers — the one with the name that had been warming in the pit-arithmetic since she had watched Rudra press the thirteenth stair — was one page was too few for. She stood and listened to the habit at the bottom of the district counting, as was the habit's custom, in fours; and she understood, in the way her family of watchers had always understood things, with the whole spine and not with the mouth, that the four-cadence was now asking its question in a larger room than the pit: the room was the district, and soon — though the register may not yet say when — the room would be the whole sleeping province.

So Ira did what her kind of watcher does at the end of a count: she closed the distance between herself and the haveli, the haveli and the karha, the karha and the ghat, the ghat and the crack, the crack and the count, and the count and the one now descending through it, and she wrote, at last, not in her notebook but on the inside of her wrist, where her family kept the entries that must survive bathing:

**"Woh neeche se ginti kar raha hai. Humein upar se aana padega."**

*(He is counting from below. We shall have to come from above.)*

And then — because the register finds, after all its grammar, that its last receipt is the reader's suspense, which it can no longer audit without cost — the register surrenders the remaining pages of this book to whoever now turns them.
