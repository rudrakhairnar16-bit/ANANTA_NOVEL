# CHAPTER FIFTY-SEVEN — THE LINE THEY COULD CUT

---

> *"One return line isolated. Upper passage released. Main assembly still live.*
> *Do not file the first two entries under failed demolition."*
>
> *— riverwatch cable-recovery sheet, first entry, countersigned after the fighting*

---

Rudra held the breath.

The fourth needle stopped. Beneath it, a toothed wheel clicked twice more. The opening between the rings continued widening.

"Saans lo," Ram said.

Rudra shook his head.

"Dekho pehle. Saans ruki. Darwaaza nahi."

His father pointed with the bandaged hand. The needle was still, but the wheel below it had caught on another tooth. At the desk the Right Hand moved a short handle, and the lower ring advanced without waiting for Rudra's chest.

Rudra let the air out.

It scraped along the raw line above his collar. Red-black pressure tightened over his ribs, crossed by gold which held the outline together without making it painless. The next intake came too quickly. He had to put a hand against the broken sighting bracket to steady himself.

The bracket bent under his palm.

He took the hand away.

"Main isko chala raha hoon?" he asked.

"Abhi itna pata hai ki tumko padh raha hai," Ram said. "Baaki hum dekhenge. Tum saans lo."

Ira had risen beside the terrace wall. Veer kept his good hand beneath her elbow until she found the edge with the back of her own hand. Her left eye remained covered. The cloth had dried stiff where it touched her cheek.

"Needle aur neeche ka wheel alag hain," she said. "Main dono ek saath nahi dekh paa rahi."

"Wheel main dekhunga," Veer answered.

He tapped the wood across his knees each time it moved. Ira watched the needle.

Two sounds, a pause, one sound. Then another pause in which the needle moved and Veer's hand did not.

Rudra could hear the difference from the stair.

The sighting plate had followed his chest. The winch could still turn without it. Holding his breath was not a way out.

He looked towards the courtyard through which his mother had gone.

A man came back from its inner door carrying the empty board that had bridged the courtyard drain. He held it upright to keep its wet end off his clothes. Behind him, the ambulance's light moved across the upper wall and disappeared.

"Gaadi nikal gayi," he called. "Bahan saath mein hain."

Ram turned. "Saans?"

The man understood. "Chal rahi thi. Darwaaza band kiya tab boli bhi."

He could tell them no more. Sitara had gone with her. The road beyond the courtyard remained open to the ambulance.

Ram nodded once. The muscles beside his jaw loosened and tightened again.

Rudra wanted a whole report. Blood pressure, words spoken, how the attendant had looked when the doors shut. He had one man's honest account of a few seconds. He made himself accept it without asking the man to turn it into certainty.

Aadhya came back through the courtyard after him. She had let the ambulance crew take the cart and had stayed to keep the turn clear until it left. A strip of fresh cloth covered the heel of her left palm. The right thumb and index finger were still held straight against the side of her hand, useless for gripping.

"Maa ke saath Sitara hain," she told Rudra.

"Suna."

"Pata hai. Phir bhi."

She took the wet board from the householder with her forearms rather than her fingers and laid it beside the opened channel.

They would need somewhere to kneel that did not move.

---

At the frame, the fourth plate turned a little farther towards Rudra. Its cracked face caught the gold along his neck. Beneath it a fine covered thread entered the back of the scale housing and ran down beside a cable thick as his wrist.

The narrow sleeve could have passed for a strand coming loose from the larger cable. It had its own fastening.

"Woh patla wala," he said.

Vedant looked up from the channel. "Dikha. Chhoona mat."

Rudra pointed without reaching into the gap.

The thick cable took strain from the winch to an iron saddle beneath the stair. The fine thread did not pull taut with it. Even when the larger cable shuddered, the little sleeve stayed loose.

Vedant stepped onto the dry strip and examined the place where the two entered the housing.

"Ek bojh utha raha hai," he said. "Doosra hukm le jaa raha hai. Kaunsa hukm, pehle woh dekhenge."

Across the frame the Right Hand lifted a card from its tray and changed its position under the punch. His injured finger left a dark smear on the edge. The fourth needle steadied towards Rudra again.

Water stood higher along the occupied lane.

Rudra started towards the plate.

A foot of paving lifted before his good leg came down. He changed direction, caught the edge of the sound step, and felt his cut right ankle take the turn badly. Pain struck through the form. Gold flared along his throat.

The water followed the change before he could choose the next step.

He stopped with one hand above it, not touching.

At the terrace Veer tapped once. The wheel had moved.

"Tumhare badalne se pehle jagah badal raha hai," Ira said. "Isi liye seedhe nahi pahunch paaoge."

Rudra could have driven through. He could feel enough force in his shoulder to try it. He could also see the householder still standing at the courtyard door, with people moving behind him.

Vedant pointed along the thick cable. Its saddle was bolted through the foot of the house wall. With the next click of the wheel, plaster shook loose above the bolt.

"Seedha kheenchoge toh udhar aayega," he said.

The Right Hand laid his uninjured palm on the main lever. No movement yet. Just a hand resting where everyone could see it.

Rudra lowered his shoulder.

They needed a cut that did not take the street with it.

---

Ira asked for mud before she asked anyone for power.

Aadhya scraped wet silt from the channel with the end of the courtyard board. Ram took a palmful and pressed it onto a flat scrap from the broken sighting cover. He slid the scrap towards the fourth plate along the sound edge of the plinth.

The needle slowed.

At the desk a handle moved. The plate tilted, shedding the mud off its face. The needle found Rudra again.

Veer tapped the wood once. The wheel below had moved too.

"Poora dhakna padega," Ira said.

With Veer naming the edge, Ira lowered herself onto the terrace step. Aadhya pushed the board's wet end beside her knee.

Ram withdrew the scrap before the next adjustment could trap his hand. Mud covered his bandage. The little thread behind the scale remained clean.

Covering the face would not hold. In the courtyard, the whole fitting had sat inside a clay bowl packed with wet sand. Here the operator could shake a loose covering away.

Ira could see the front. She could not see round the casing, and turning her head only shifted the missing portion of her vision to another place. The left-eye dressing did not become transparent because she wanted it to.

"Peechhe paani hai?" she asked.

Rudra moved his head without moving his feet. "Channel. Casing ke neeche tak."

"Kitna neeche?"

He held thumb and finger apart. "Itna."

She asked him to describe the gap twice. Not because she doubted him. Because the first description had made her imagine it deeper than it was.

Vedant was listening from the opened drain.

"Upar aake bhaag raha hai," he said. "Rokoge toh peechhe ki jheel banegi. Chhoti."

A small pool, using water already rising under pressure. Enough to carry silt into the lower slots and hold it there while Ram packed the face.

"Mujhe ek shape chahiye," Ira said. "Tumhare paani ki."

Veer glanced at her. "Mera wala nahi?"

"Usko pehchaanta hai. Aur tumhara haath—"

"Theek. Reason pehle wala hi rakho."

She looked at the sling, then at his freshly wrapped elbow. He knew what she was doing. For a moment his mouth almost smiled.

He settled the wooden length across his good thigh. She waited until his right elbow rested on the folded cloth again before turning towards Vedant.

---

Vedant brought his basin close without abandoning the drain. One hand remained against the old outlet stone; the water beyond it ran low instead of climbing into the lane.

With the other he held a shallow curve at the basin's rim. The water rose against that curve and stayed, a transparent lip a palm wide and no higher than Ira's little finger.

She did not reach for the water itself.

The shape holding it touched her sight. Softer than Veer's shield. Its edge changed a little even while it held still, accommodating the movement through it rather than resisting every grain.

She received that one edge.

The narrow silver lines returned to her uncovered iris. The pain behind the dressing answered at once.

A second holding curve appeared over a puddle on the board beside her knee. The puddle rose, thinned, and spilled over the side.

Too much angle.

Vedant lowered his hand to steady the basin. The reflected edge fell with his, before Ira chose to release it. Water spread into the sand beneath the board.

"Mat kheenchna," Vedant said. "Jo aa raha hai, usko thehraana."

Hold what arrived. Do not drag more towards it.

She tried again, receiving the same small pattern. This time the puddle's remaining edge stood against a pale seam in the air.

It stayed until she closed her sight.

A strike rang against stone farther down the stair. Ira looked up with ordinary vision.

Another workman had driven a long hook towards Rudra's ankle. Rudra caught its iron neck against the broken bracket, forcing the point down instead of letting the whole shaft swing towards the lane. His neck was bleeding into the collar again.

Ram moved to his other side. Between them they kept the man from reaching the rear passage.

The tests had cost seconds. Other people were paying for those seconds with their bodies.

"Ab casing," Ira said.

Vedant kept the little curve above his basin. Ira received it again and turned the new reflection towards the gap Rudra had described.

He watched it arrive. "Neeche. Ab meri taraf. Bas."

The water behind the scale began to gather. Not new water: the same grey trickle from the channel, stopped before it could run away. Silt clouded the little pool.

Ram slid the muddy scrap along the front again.

At the desk the Right Hand moved to tilt the plate.

It could turn its face. It could not empty the water held against its back.

Grit packed the lower slots. Ram pressed another fold of silt into the front seam with the flat metal, keeping his fingers out of the mounting jaws.

Ira could feel the whole shape only while Vedant maintained it. Each tiny alteration at his basin ran through the reflection. She could not make it steadier than he could supply.

"Ek aur saans," he said.

She gave it.

The fourth needle shivered, travelled halfway towards Rudra, and stopped.

The toothed wheel below clicked again.

"Needle ruki," Ira said.

"Wheel nahi," Veer answered.

That was the answer. Small, exact, worth the pain beneath her covered eye.

She released the reflected edge. Vedant lowered his own hand, and the lip of water fell back into his basin. The little pool behind the casing drained, leaving packed wet silt in its slots.

The needle stayed still.

Rudra's next breath did not move it.

A tremor passed through Ira's drawing hand so hard that her knuckles struck the board. She kept that hand on the wood until it stopped knocking.

"Main theek—" she began.

"Mat bolo," Veer said. "Baitho."

She was already sitting. He meant stay there, and let someone else look next.

She did.

---

Rudra put his good foot on the next dry stone.

This time the paving did not rise to meet it.

The water turned after him. Late enough that he saw the edge coming and could draw his injured foot clear instead of wrenching the ankle sideways.

At the desk, the Right Hand took his palm off the main lever and worked the smaller handle twice. Mud shook from the fourth casing.

"Zyada waqt nahi," Ira called.

The hook trapped against the broken bracket scraped upwards. The workman was trying to free it while Rudra watched the plate.

Ram caught the shaft. His bandage slid against the wood.

Rudra turned back. He put his forearm over the shaft and bore down, meaning only to keep it still. The man's grip opened with a cry.

Rudra lifted the pressure immediately.

The hook clattered onto stone. The man pulled his arm against his chest, fingers moving, and backed towards the stair. A pair of short cable-shears swung from his belt.

"Woh chhod," Ram said, pointing.

The man stared at him.

"Auzaar. Yahin. Phir upar."

He unclipped the shears with his unhurt hand and put them down. Ram kicked the hook beyond his reach. At the first turning Dhruv held out an arm, stopping the man from running into the mourners. A watchman took hold of his collar.

Rudra watched until the man was behind the upper wall.

He had not meant to hurt that arm. He could not call the cry an accident and stop thinking about it.

"Rudra," Ram said.

He turned back to the people still in reach.

---

The fine sleeve ran through a little iron clip before entering the scale housing. Beside it the thick cable shuddered with every tooth of the winch.

Ram set the shears down with the jaws open.

"Dono ko alag karna hai. Pehle."

He picked up a piece of the split hook-shaft Rudra had dropped beside the sighting assembly. Its blunt end fitted between the two sleeves. Pushed a little farther, it lifted the narrow one clear of the loaded cable.

Rudra reached to hold it.

The wood cracked under his fingers.

He stopped.

For a moment he wanted to throw the useless piece away and take the whole housing off the wall. Iron had bent for him. The small thing was asking more patience than the iron.

Ram moved the wood under the flat of his son's forearm.

"Pakadna nahi. Yahan rakho."

Rudra rested his arm where he was shown. No closed fist. No pull towards his chest. The wood stayed in one piece.

A bead of water ran out of the casing, carrying grey grit. Then another. The small handle at the desk moved again.

Ira's good eye followed the needle.

"Abhi ruki hai," she said. "Jaldi nahi. Saaf kaato."

Ram worked one blade beneath the isolated sleeve. The other rested above it. Thick cable on the far side of the wooden spacer. Empty air inside the jaws except for the thin line they had chosen.

His hand shook.

He took it off the handle, wiped the blood from his palm against his trousers and set both hands on the shears.

Behind Rudra the water lifted.

Veer came off the folded sack onto his good foot. A pale curve rose between the wet edge and Ram's back, no wider than it had been in the doorway.

The water struck its weak lower corner.

Veer angled it rather than asking the damaged edge to become whole. The blow slid down onto the empty stone by his knee. He grunted and kept the curve there.

"Ab wheel tum dekho," he told Ira.

He could not watch the wheel and keep the curve in place. She shifted her gaze down from the stalled needle.

"Chal raha hai," she said. "Ek daant aur."

Rudra felt that next movement in the thick cable beneath his arm. The wooden spacer dragged against his skin.

He did not press harder.

Ram closed the shears.

The covering split. The bright strand inside bent without parting.

The needle jerked once.

A thin wet line raced up the step. Veer's curve intercepted it, leaving a dark score in the stone beyond his heel.

Rudra's neck seam widened as he turned his shoulder into the remaining force. He could feel warm blood under the gold. For one breath the red-black thinned over his wrist, then gathered again without taking the wrist away from where he had put it.

Ram shifted the strand deeper between the blades.

The second squeeze cut it.

One end dropped against the wooden spacer. The other recoiled no farther than the width of Ram's thumb.

The thick cable stayed taut.

Above it, no bolt moved in the house wall.

"Hua," Ram said.

Rudra took his arm away slowly. The spacer fell free. He left both cut ends where he could see them.

Veer lowered his hand and sat down hard on the sack. His right elbow left a red patch on his shirt.

At the desk, the small handle went all the way over.

Nothing followed Rudra's next step.

Mud dripped from the fourth plate. Its needle began to move again, a small trembling answer to his chest.

Farther along the lane, the standing water kept its old angle.

The reading still existed at the plate. On this line, it could go no farther than the cut.

Rudra took one more breath, watching both the cut ends.

"Saans rokni nahi thi," he said.

Ram picked up the shears. "Rasta kaatna tha."

Below them the toothed wheel clicked again.

---

The upper passage was still blocked.

Water stood across it in three short ridges. Cutting the return line had not emptied the channel or taken away the angles already set into the plates. A loose rag touched the nearest ridge and came apart.

Vedant left it there as a warning.

"Ab yeh tumhare peechhe nahi ghoom raha," he told Rudra. "Isko neeche mod sakte hain. Wahan."

He pointed towards the cleared lower service steps, not the river stair where Dhruv's people were still moving.

Aadhya looked at the peepal root beside the grain-store threshold. The stripped portion lay pale against the broken paving. She did not put her palm over it again.

Instead she dragged the courtyard board to the channel on her forearms. She set one end on sound stone, then asked the householder to move the other until it rested beyond the undermined edge. It restored the missing foothold at the wall; the middle of the lane still had sound paving.

"Iske neeche paani jaayega," she said. "Upar ek-ek aadmi. Abhi koi nahi."

Rudra stepped into the narrow dry strip beside it. The form tightened over his shins. His right ankle had begun to swell against the sandal strap.

He brought his forearm against the wet ridge.

The first touch bit through the outline over his skin. He drew back a fraction, found the angle at which the pressure ran past rather than straight into him, and turned his shoulder with it.

Not farther than he needed.

Water struck the first empty step below them. It cut a white line through the grit.

Vedant lowered his basin. The diverted stream caught its moving edge and went down the service channel instead of circling back towards Rudra's legs.

The ridge tried to close from its old lower angle.

Rudra could see where it was coming from now. It had not followed the last movement of his shoulder. He kept the small separation open while Vedant led the released water through it.

The second ridge sank.

At the desk the Right Hand abandoned the small handle. He moved a longer one beside the main lever.

"Baayein," Ira called. "Dekha usne."

Rudra shifted before the new setting reached him. A flat blow struck the stone where his injured foot had been.

It was still a fight. The man at the desk still had eyes and hands. But now he had to see the movement and make his answer after it.

That was enough room for Vedant to finish turning the flow.

The last ridge lowered to an ordinary runnel.

Rudra kept his arm in place until the water stopped striking it. When he lifted it away, the outline shook from wrist to shoulder. The gold drew close along his neck. He tasted the old mouth wound again.

He stood without taking another step until his foot belonged under him.

"Ab," Vedant said.

Aadhya let the householder cross the board first, empty-handed. She watched both ends while he put his full weight over the middle. No rocking. No fresh crack in the stone beneath it.

Then she waved the people inside his door towards them.

An old woman came with a steel container held against her stomach. Behind her, a girl tugged at the key on her neck and turned back towards an inner cupboard. Aadhya put a forearm behind the girl's shoulders and kept her moving without closing her numb fingers round the cord.

"Chaabi rehne do," she said. "Tum chalo."

Ram stood at the far end to receive them.

He looked at the key, then at the house above the loaded saddle. He did not tell the girl she would certainly be able to use it tomorrow.

---

Dhruv brought the remaining bier-bearers across in their own formation. Nobody asked them to tip what they carried to make the turn quicker. The dry width was there now; they used it. The watchmen brought both disarmed workmen up behind the mourners, the second still cradling his arm.

The householder went back once with a watchman, checked the rear room, and came out without anyone behind him. He shut neither courtyard door.

At the watch post the first patient's boy saw people moving towards the upper lane and stood up beside his mother's bench.

"Hum bhi?"

Aadhya asked the men at the road end whether the whole straight passage was clear. She waited for an answer from the far turning, not just a wave from the nearest helper.

"Khula hai," the call came back.

Only then did she send for the padded rails.

They came with the patient still on them. Her injured leg lay straight beneath Rukmini's shawl. Two volunteers took the ends; a third walked beside the injured side without trying to lift or bend it. Her boy stayed near her head, where she could see him.

No cart turn this time. No promise that the pain would stop before the road.

Ram watched the trailing rail clear the board.

"Udhar transport waalon ko bulao," he told the watchman. "Isko yahin mat bitha dena."

The watchman went ahead to the road party. The patient left the ghat passage carried level.

Rudra followed her progress with his eyes until the shawl was out of sight.

They had not closed the opening. They had taken people away from it.

At the frame the fourth plate twitched towards his chest once more. He looked down at the severed strand lying beside its larger neighbour.

He did not need to hold his breath to keep the passage open.

"Wapas mat aana," he told the householder. "Darwaaza khula rehne do."

The man looked at his house. Then he looked at the people who had just crossed.

He gave Ram a torn strip from his own shoulder towel for the bleeding palm and went up the lane.

---

Ram wound the towel strip over his opened bandage. He pulled the knot tight with his teeth, then picked up the shears again.

Rudra looked towards the turning where the workman had gone.

"Uska haath—"

"Doctor dekhega."

"Maine bas roka tha."

Ram looked at the split hook-shaft beneath the casing.

Rudra followed his gaze. The wood had been in his hand too.

"Pata hai," Ram said.

Not permission to forget it. Not a reason to stop helping the people he could still reach. His father placed the shears on the dry step, away from Rudra's feet, and waited until Rudra looked back at the frame.

The main wheel had not missed a tooth during the evacuation.

At the desk, the Right Hand no longer touched the fourth card. It lay crooked under the punch. Both his hands were now at the foot of the main lever, one holding a small metal catch while the other worked something loose beneath it.

The finger-guard had come off. Blood marked the bare knuckle.

A long steel pin emerged from the lever's stop.

"Woh nikaal raha hai," Ira said.

She leaned forward and lost the edge of the terrace in her reduced vision. Veer caught her sleeve without rising. She put her own hand down, found stone, and stayed behind it.

"Pin. Lever ke neeche."

Ram saw it.

"Rudra. Seedha uske paas nahi. Beech ka jod dekho."

Between the nested rings, below the opening's lower edge, an iron coupling turned on a shorter shaft. The outer feet stayed fixed to the paving. The coupling carried the movement from the winch into the turning frame.

Rudra could see only part of it each time the inner band went past.

"Bahar wala kheenchoge toh cable aayega," Ram said. "Pehle isko rokne ki jagah dhoondho."

Not an order to put a hand between moving teeth. A warning against the large easy target.

Rudra moved along the dry strip, keeping the coupling in view.

---

Dhruv came down two steps from the river stair.

"Upar ki service gali khaali," he called. "Aage log hain. Udhar mat modna."

The space they had emptied was not the whole ghat. Beyond the upper turning, people still stood under roofs joined to these walls. The road party was still carrying the injured away.

Aadhya stayed at the passage mouth. She put her back against the house wall and used her shoulder to keep a loose door from swinging across the route. Her hands hung clear.

Vedant watched the channel. The water had begun pulsing against his diversion, each pulse stronger than the last.

"Rudra. Neeche ka rasta abhi khaali hai. Zyada der nahi."

Rudra put his left foot beside the coupling's stone base.

The four struck inside his chest.

The longer allowance answered too late. For an instant his right hand wanted to open while his shoulder drove forward. The gold across his throat thinned to separate bright stitches.

He stopped advancing.

Blood spotted the step below his collar. He touched the seam with his sleeve, looked at the red and kept still until he could choose a step instead of being driven into one.

Ram came level with him, outside the moving band's reach.

"Dikh raha?"

"Beech mein. Har baar aadha."

"Toh aadha dekh ke haath nahi."

They watched a full turn. Then another.

The coupling had a solid shoulder on its stair-facing side. The teeth passed behind it. Rudra lowered his forearm towards that shoulder without touching the moving shaft.

At the desk the steel pin came free.

It struck the lid and rolled against the punch.

The main lever dropped the last few inches into a deeper notch.

---

The winch gave one hard pull.

The courtyard board jumped on the empty passage. Water burst from beneath it and went down the service steps in a sheet. Vedant dropped to one knee, bringing his basin's edge low enough to keep the first surge away from Aadhya's legs.

A bolt under the frame tore out with a square of stone attached.

The outer upright pitched towards the house cable.

Rudra left the coupling and caught the falling iron with both forearms.

The load drove his left knee into the step. His right ankle folded against the stone; pain went white behind his eyes. He could not find either count in it.

The outline held.

Barely. Red-black compressed along his arms. Gold flared across the raw neck seam, and blood fell between his feet while the upright stopped moving.

At the desk the Right Hand took both hands away from the lever.

It stayed down in the notch.

The thick cable began pulling through its saddle again. From the foot of the emptied house came a crack which ran upwards, past the courtyard door they had left open.

Rudra pressed his back against the sound stone of the stair cheek.

He had caught the frame before it fell. He had not stopped the wheel inside it.

The next tooth went over while his arms were still on the iron.
