# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY — WHAT WAS HERS TO ASK

---

> *"A name could be spoken for someone.*
> *An answer could not be lived for them."*
> *— Ira's later register, before another person answered*

---

"Haan," Rukmini said. "Mera janam unke faisle se jhooth nahi ho gaya tha."

Rudra listened.

She had not answered for every person who had ever been forced from a place. She was answering for herself, the woman on this call, whose relation to her own birth had continued through what had been done to her.

"Prakriti mera Kul tha," she said. "Hai. Mujhe nikaalne se meri zindagi ka woh hissa unka nahi ho gaya."

He looked at the supported phone while the small correction from past to present settled.

It was not a claim that she had carried a house untouched inside herself. A word could remain hers while the loss remained a loss. The people who had driven her out had taken something real; they had not needed to take every possible thing for the act to be wrong.

"Ghar yaad aata hai," she said. "Apna keh paane se uski kami band nahi hui thi."

---

He had nearly made her answer braver than it was.

Not because it lacked courage. Because he wanted the kind which ended a difficulty by naming it. A woman kept her lineage, therefore exile had failed completely; a son learned the truth, therefore the years outside it could be folded away.

She had given him neither story.

"Main aapko yeh nahi bol sakta ki phir unhone kuch chheena hi nahi," he said.

"Nahi bol sakte."

"Par jo aapka tha, woh sirf unke kehne se unka bhi nahi ho gaya."

"Wahi keh rahi hoon."

There was relief in being corrected towards the smaller, truer thing. He did not need to defeat her pain in order to hear that she had remained a person through it.

The false accusation was still false. The forced departure had not become a choice because she had continued living afterwards.

Her continued life was not an acquittal of the people who had hurt it.

---

"Main sochta rehta hoon ki aapko sab waapas dena hoga," Rudra admitted.

"Mujhse poochhe bina?"

He let the question reach him.

"Haan. Dimaag mein bhi pehle tai kar leta hoon."

"Abhi poochh rahe ho. Usko chhota mat samjho."

She was not saying that listening repaired exile, or that her wish to see home could be satisfied by a son asking a considerate question from another city. She was refusing to have that wish taken over in the name of fulfilling it.

He could not restore a person by arranging the rest of her life without her.

The thought was not a plan. It was a reason to stop mistaking a plan for a gift before the person receiving it had been allowed to speak.

He said he understood the difference better now.

She did not require him to promise that he would never need to learn it again.

---

The remaining minutes were nearly used.

Rudra did not ask for a date, an itinerary or a description of what waited beyond the public court. His mother's belonging had been answered as her own account. It had not opened a new view into a ruler's room, supplied a current army or changed the limits of an office's report.

"Aaj ke liye itna?" she asked.

"Haan."

He meant the answer, not an agreement to stop caring about it after the call. It would come with him into the evening. She did not need to remain connected in order to keep it alive.

At the ten-minute limit, they said goodbye.

The call closed. The phone setups ended and the devices were put aside. Rukmini took the next care and rest in Varanasi; her shoulder still needed its support. Rudra accepted his own assisted movement in Mirzapur without leaning through the injured wrist or elbow.

He gave the present answer about the back. The nurse attended to the familiar pull and checked again before leaving him settled.

---

Afterwards, he slept for a while.

When he woke, the room contained the same cup, the same stand and the same ordinary work continuing beyond the curtain. His right ankle and left knee still required their positions checked. The weak hand had not learned a new reliability in sleep.

He asked for help with the water rather than attempting to make a family answer visible in a successful reach.

The worker gave the help, heard his answer and went on.

His mother had said that she could still call her lineage hers. Not that nothing had happened to her. Not that what happened must now be paid for by him.

For the first time that afternoon, he let the word hers carry its full weight.

It included the part he could not take over, even by loving her.

By evening, Rudra wanted a voice which was not part of the family conversation.

Not to explain it. He wanted a few minutes in which he did not have to keep following the same thought to the same place. He had asked Veer for glad company yesterday. Tonight he asked whether Ira wanted a little spoken company after her own care, without a page to read or a record to make.

The request went when he was ready to send it. It carried no private name or account.

Ira declined that evening.

Her ordinary right eye had done the short work allowed to it, and she wanted the remaining quiet after care. The left stayed covered. She did not offer a different use of herself to make the refusal easier for him to receive.

"Kal dekh sakte hain," her reply said. "Aaj nahi."

Rudra acknowledged it once.

He did not ask how tired was too tired for three minutes of audio.

---

There was a little disappointment after the phone was put away.

He let himself have it without finding a culprit. Ira had not refused a friendship. She had refused this evening, which was a thing he wanted and had not been promised.

For a while he watched someone carry a stack of clean linen past the opening in the curtain. The top piece was threatening to fall. The worker stopped, put the stack down properly, settled it and lifted it again instead of trying to save the walking time with one more awkward step.

Nothing important happened.

The stack arrived where it was going.

Rudra found himself absurdly pleased by that small refusal to make the task more difficult than it had to be. He did not turn it into a lesson to offer Ira later. She had already managed her own evening without needing him to discover a principle for her.

---

His own supper needed more attention than he first gave it.

He was offered the next mouthful while still considering what he might have said on a call. He asked for a pause. The worker stopped, checked whether there was discomfort and heard that he needed a little more time, not a different diagnosis.

The meal continued at the pace he could actually use.

He had been trying to learn a generous way to hear other people's limits. It would be strange if the result were to conceal his own every time they interfered with the version of himself he wanted to be.

After supper, the supports were checked and he rested.

The unanswered things had not become an emergency because there was no friend listening to him tonight. He could still request company tomorrow. He could also live through this quiet part of the day without turning a sleeping person into somebody who had failed to arrive.

---

He thought briefly of asking a teacher for a different kind of conversation.

There were practical questions which could always be made to sound necessary. A public counter existed, a road led towards the old passage, and he knew people who had actually done the work of going and coming back.

That did not mean another job was waiting inside the completed one.

The teachers had returned, received care, closed the new payments and taken their rest. Their limited report had reached the person permitted to hear it. Nothing about his present impatience reopened their car, their chair or their day.

He did not send a request dressed as an update.

Nor did he tell himself that choosing not to send it meant he must never ask anything again. A new request could be a new request, if he came to want one for a reason he could say honestly. It did not need to be smuggled through somebody else's unfinished sentence.

---

The night brought its ordinary interruptions.

There was help he needed, a check he answered and a moment when the back would not settle until he described the familiar pull instead of waiting for the worker to guess. After the support was corrected, he slept again.

No answer came in the dark to identify the old faint. No name arrived from inside him to explain what an office or his mother had not said.

The two hundred and seventeenth morning was brighter than he wanted at first.

He asked for the light at his side to be reduced while the next care was done. The worker made the change, heard whether it helped and continued. Breakfast and rest followed the current review; there was no new clearance hidden in waking to a different day.

Later, Ira sent her own offer.

---

Three minutes of audio, after both had rested, if he still wanted the company. No reading at either end. She had given her present eye and comfort answers and chosen a supported position with the screen turned away from the ordinary eye.

Rudra wanted it.

He accepted after his own care and actual rest, without treating yesterday's refusal as a debt she was now repaying. The finite phone setup was agreed with the ward help. His wrist stayed out of the work, and nothing was brought from the cupboard for her to examine.

There would be a call because two people had chosen this small part of the morning.

Not because one of them had lost the right to say no last night.

When the call connected, Ira said she could hear him.

Her present position was comfortable. So was his, after the check. The devices were supported for audio; no one needed to look steadily at a lit screen to prove they were attending.

"Kya chal raha hai?" he asked.

"Abhi baat. Baaki kuch nahi."

"Poora answer de diya."

"Teen minute hain. Efficient hoon."

He smiled, and told her about the stack of linen.

Only what he had seen: a worker stopping, putting it down and setting the top piece properly before continuing. By the time he reached the end, he could hear how little story there had been in it.

"Bas?" Ira asked.

"Bas."

"Achha hua gira nahi."

"Main bhi wahi soch raha tha."

The conversation had successfully reached the end of a story in which clean cloth was delivered to the place clean cloth was needed.

Neither of them improved it with a hidden meaning.

---

For a little while they spoke about how difficult it was to leave something merely finished.

Rudra said that when a task ended, his mind often began looking for the part he must have missed. Ira said that was no reason to assign a second task to the person who had just completed the first.

"Main tumhe kuch assign nahi kar raha," he said.

"Pata hai. Isiliye phone uthaya."

He heard the warmth beneath the flatness.

She did not need to make her willingness larger in order for him to trust it. He had asked for company. She had chosen company. The little boundary around it was not evidence that only a little of the friendship remained.

"Aaj main kisi cheez ki summary nahi banaa rahi," she added.

"Mere linen waale account ka bhi nahi?"

"Woh itihaas ko khud sambhaalna padega."

This time the smile came before he had prepared it.

---

At the three minutes, they said goodbye.

The connection ended. The phones were taken out of use, the ward setup tasks finished and each returned to the care and rest already due. Ira's covered left eye did not become available when the screen went dark. Her ordinary right eye kept its own limits, and Rudra did not ask for a message proving she had rested properly afterwards.

The call had given him something he could not make into a report.

Not information. The experience of being heard by a person who had not needed the private cause of every feeling in order to speak with him.

He had not told her a new name, let her infer a family connection or asked her to place his mother's life among the things her notebook must safeguard. The conversation had remained ordinary without being empty.

After his own next care, he slept.

---

Later, with the room quiet, he thought about the end of the call.

There had been no sense that the friendship must be completed in three minutes. They had only completed this conversation. Tomorrow could contain another one, or no call at all, without tonight's company being taken back.

He wished it were as easy to remember that about every unfinished thing.

His mother's account remained open. Her wish to see home had not been cancelled because the present ruler had been named. Her lineage had not made the false framing less real, and the wrong done to her had not made all the questions of his childhood unkind to ask.

They could continue without forcing a final shape upon everything at once.

He did not need a promise about the end before he could choose an honest next question.

---

A worker brought the stand when the short reading interval was ready.

Rudra wanted the ordinary page, not the hearing sheets. The angle was checked; the wrist did not have to hold the material flat. He read for a while and stopped when the allotted time ended, leaving a sentence halfway through its work.

The worker marked the place with the plain slip already being used and moved the page away.

It was an unimportant stopping point. That helped. He did not need a revelation to make stopping respectable, or a whole answer before he could rest.

He gave the present comfort answers and waited through the support adjustment. The meal and the evening came in their usual order. There was nothing to send to the teachers, no missed appointment at the old passage, no driver whose paid time he had forgotten to end.

The work which had actually been taken was finished.

The questions had not all been answered.

---

Before sleep, he considered what he wanted to ask his mother next.

Not a secret name behind the name. Not a method which would turn the knowledge into strength. He was thinking about the difference between knowing what had been said about a person and hearing what that person wanted to say for herself.

The distinction was not new to him at a public window.

It was becoming harder to ignore at home.

He left the phone alone. The person he wanted to ask would have her own morning, and her own choice about the next conversation.

On the two hundred and eighteenth morning, after the review, breakfast and rest, Rudra asked for the two hearing sheets.

Five minutes, if the ward worker had room for the small setup. She agreed to that task and brought the sheets from the cupboard. The stand's position was checked before she placed them; his uncertain hand was not made responsible for keeping an old answer from sliding.

He read the question first.

It was the one they had actually carried, about the failing balance and the basis for what his father had reported. The account kept Ram's source separate from the work-familiar woman's own statement. It had not become an explanation of everything a Devta knew, or a promise that defeating one enemy would repair the whole.

His mother's name was not waiting between those lines.

He had once been grateful for the office's refusal to make a hearing larger than what had actually been asked. He remained grateful now, when a larger account would have been more convenient to him.

---

The pages had a simple attraction.

They were there. They could be brought out, read, put away and brought out again. They did not need to rest after speaking. If he wanted to feel certain that he had not imagined the answer, he could return to the same words.

A person was harder.

His mother might want to stop. She might want to correct the question before answering it. She might tell him a fact which remained true and a feeling which did not stay the same every afternoon. None of that made her a worse source for her own life than the paper.

It made a conversation something he could not finish on her behalf.

He looked at the space after the written account and did not imagine adding a private lineage name to make it more complete. These sheets had done their work. They were not an empty file which every later truth must be made to enter.

---

At the end of the five minutes, he asked for both to be returned.

The worker took them off the stand, placed them back in the cupboard and finished the agreed setup work. The sheets were unchanged. No new copy, marginal addition or borrowed office authority had been produced.

Rudra gave his current comfort answer and took the next rest.

He was not abandoning the old question. The already-failing balance, the limited independent work statement and the uncertainty about repair could remain real without making every person he loved a new piece of evidence in that inquiry.

His mother's false framing was not an illustration waiting for him to attach beneath someone else's report.

It was something which had happened to her.

He could ask what she wanted to do with her own account before he decided which question ought to carry it into the world.

---

Later, he found himself rehearsing a sentence which began with we.

We should tell them. We should ask. We should make somebody hear it.

He stopped at the easy little word.

There were times when it named a choice people had actually made together. There were times when it let the speaker carry another person's name into a decision and claim company before asking for it.

He had not asked his mother for that company.

He did not know which telling she wanted, or whether she wanted one now. He knew she had been falsely accused and forced away. He knew she wanted to see her birthplace again. Those answers did not supply every other answer simply because he could connect them in a sentence.

He could believe her without appointing himself the voice which would correct every stranger.

That was the harder form of belief for him: the one which left her present.

---

The afternoon was allowed to continue without an announcement.

He ate, accepted the care and did not send a message to the counter through a teacher who had not chosen a new task. No vehicle was booked, no worker asked to hold a day, no friend told a private cause so they could help him decide what his mother must want.

The new inquiry's reserve had already been returned. It did not exist as a small purse waiting for the next noble use he could find for it. The damaged ambulance's separate unresolved account was not repaired by his wish to do something else well.

There were real things left open.

He did not have to add an invisible job to them.

---

After his next rest, he asked when Rukmini would want another short private conversation.

She chose eight minutes the following afternoon, after her own review, care and sleep. He accepted. There was no demand that she become ready tonight because he had found a better way to arrange his question.

The request contained no new political report. He wanted to ask her about what she wanted, before thinking about any action which might follow.

That was all he said about it for now.

The phone was put aside after the practical message. He took the evening's care and rest in Mirzapur, leaving his mother her own evening in Varanasi.

For once, the unanswered part was not a decision he had hidden from her.

It was a place he was waiting for her to enter herself.

The two hundred and nineteenth morning began without a message from another realm.

There was the current review, breakfast and the work of getting comfortable enough to rest. Rudra described what he actually felt. The elbow remained protected, the grip uncertain, the old faint without an explanation which a new family word could supply.

He did not ask whether the afternoon's conversation required him to be stronger first.

It required him to be ready to listen, and even that would have to be checked when the time came. His mother had chosen a short interval after her own care and sleep. He could arrive inside that choice without making his need to hear her into a reason she should have no need of her own.

For the morning, he let the body have its ordinary work.

He ate with help and slept. The phone waited out of use.

---

When he woke, he thought of the receiver at the window.

The man had answered a public question about the person named as ruler. It mattered that the answer had been obtained honestly, with its place and time intact. It mattered that the teachers had come back, rested and told him what they had heard rather than what they thought he wanted.

But a public answer could become a different kind of falsehood in the mouth of someone who used it to close every other question.

Daksh was named as ruler by that office on that morning.

It did not follow that the lie against his mother had become true, or that the people harmed by it no longer had an account worth hearing. Nor did it follow that Rudra had been appointed to speak that account wherever he could find someone willing to listen.

The first wrong did not give him a better reason to make the second choice without her.

---

He could imagine a story in which the son heard enough, became certain and left.

It was a story with a clean direction. He could put his anger inside it and never have to discover whether the person he meant to help wanted the ending he had chosen.

His own life had already contained too many decisions made in the name of the child who must survive them.

He did not want to make love the word which prevented his mother from answering back.

He wanted her to have a voice in what came next. Not a ceremonial yes after the work had been arranged, not the task of making his choice feel kind. A place early enough that the answer could still change the shape of the question.

That was harder to rehearse because it did not contain a guaranteed answer.

He left it that way.

---

The afternoon's practical checks were completed at both ends.

Rukmini had taken the review, care and rest she chose before the call. Rudra had done the same in Mirzapur. Each gave a present comfort answer, and each still wanted the private eight minutes. The ordinary ward workers accepted the small setup tasks and settled the phones where neither injured arm had to hold the other person's voice.

There was no audience waiting behind the conversation.

No sheet had been laid ready for a statement. No friend had been asked to remember the words. The two old pages stayed in their cupboard, holding the separate work they had actually done. The current question would not borrow their authority by being spoken near them.

The connection began.

---

"Aaj aap kya chahti ho, uske baare mein poochhna hai," Rudra said.

"Mere liye sochkar aaye ho?"

"Sawaal sochkar aaya hoon. Jawaab nahi."

He heard her quiet acknowledgement.

It was not forgiveness, permission to travel or the first order of a campaign. It was a mother hearing that the son had brought her a place to speak before he brought anybody a plan.

He could still be afraid of the answer. She could still be afraid of what it would cost to give one. Neither fear had to be hidden so that their conversation could sound more complete than it was.

For a little while, the ordinary distance between Varanasi and Mirzapur was just distance. Two people in separate rooms, both supported, both able to end the call when its chosen time was done.

No realm had opened inside the line.

---

Rudra thought of the names he had learned: the one she had been born with, the one she had lived on Earth, the lineage she could still call hers. He thought of the lie which had tried to make her future smaller than the people who told it wanted their own to be.

He could not give the stolen years back by choosing another person's next years for them.

More than half the eight minutes remained. His mother was there, ready to hear the question. He let go of the version in which asking it already meant that she must say yes.

He was not calling for a crown.

He was asking the person whose life had been spoken over whether she wanted a different telling.

"Ma, aap chahti ho ki jo jhooth aapke naam ke saath chhoda gaya, uska jawaab kabhi aapki apni aawaaz mein suna jaaye?"
