# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN — THE QUESTION WITHOUT A FAMILY NAME

---

> *"The question travelled without a pedigree.*
> *The people carrying it did not travel without limits."*
> *— Ira's later register, a journey counted once*

---

"Usi khidki par," Vedant answered. "Pehle bhi sawaal liya tha. Is baar vartamaan shaasak ka naam poochha ja sakta hai. Jawaab wahaan ka aadmi de, tabhi naya jawaab hoga."

Rudra looked at the supported phone.

The source was a place they had actually reached, not a hidden contact who had been waiting off the edge of every earlier account. Vedant was not saying he already knew the answer. He was naming a reasonable public place at which to ask for one.

"Meri taraf se bas itna hi poochhna hai," Rudra said. "Koi niji naam ya kahaani nahi. Agar koi karna chaahe toh."

"Main is ek sawaal ko le jaane ka kaam dekhna chahta hoon," Vedant said. "Apna review, saath jaane waale ki marzi, kharch aur waapsi pehle dekhkar."

It was a conditional offer made by a person, not a journey already performed by the word teacher.

Rudra accepted its size.

---

He did not ask Vedant to persuade Sitara before she had heard the request. He did not offer Dhruv's leg as a spare resource if somebody else declined. The first answer had given the question a possible destination, not assigned the bodies which would reach it.

"Na ho toh bataa dena," he said.

"Bataa doonga."

They reached the end of the eight minutes already chosen. Both said goodbye. The connection closed; the supported phone was put away, and Rudra attended to the next check and rest in Mirzapur rather than holding the line open while Vedant began arranging a different person's day.

Vedant took his own care and rest too.

The student's request could remain real through that pause. It did not need to be kept urgent in order to be remembered.

---

Later, Vedant asked Sitara whether she wanted a short conversation about the public inquiry.

She chose five minutes after her hand care and rest. He accepted. When they connected, both had answered the present comfort questions and neither needed the protected hand or forearm to hold the phone up.

He gave the task exactly as he had received it: one present public ruler-status question at the same counter. No private history had been supplied with it, and none was required from her. No patient would accompany them merely to make the inquiry look serious.

"Aapko jaana hai?" she asked.

"Is seema mein, agar review theek raha. Aapko?"

She considered before answering.

She wanted to find the public answer too, if a fresh review and route check allowed it. That was her reason for choosing the work. She did not need to make somebody's missing history into a debt which only she could pay.

---

They spoke about the cost before they spoke about a date.

Each was willing to consider a single job within a fresh personal contribution capped at two thousand five hundred rupees. That was not permission to draw on old trip money, the patients' current care payments or an account awaiting settlement for the damaged ambulance.

Quotes would have to fit the combined cap, with a return margin left in the money as well as the day. An inquiry abandoned because a body or a floor was not fit would still be an accepted job for the people they hired. The fee could not depend on producing a useful answer.

Sitara said she would not carry equipment with the covered hand. Vedant said his forearm was not available as somebody else's support.

They did not say those things apologetically.

The restrictions were part of the job they were choosing, not faults to be concealed from the people whose work would make it possible.

---

When their five minutes ended, neither had been given the other's tomorrow.

They agreed to seek their own current reviews and then compare the actual findings. They said goodbye, ended the call and took the next care and rest. The cap remained a proposed contribution, not a paid invoice or a promise that a driver should arrive regardless of what the reviews found.

Vedant later sent Rudra a short practical message after asking whether he wanted that update when ready.

Rudra chose to receive it after rest and heard only what had actually been decided: both teachers were willing to consider one small inquiry, subject to current ability, costs, fresh inspection and a return plan. No booking yet, no present ruler's name, no request that the patient become well enough to travel with them.

He acknowledged the message. The phone was put away.

---

The evening continued on Earth.

Sitara's hand still needed its care. Vedant's forearm still needed protection. Dhruv had not been made a walker by somebody discussing a question in a different room. Rudra slept after supper, with his own supports settled and his old faint no less unexplained.

The possibility of an answer had acquired work around it.

That was better than the appearance of a journey without the people who would have to live through it.

Their reviews took place on the two hundred and twelfth morning.

Vedant described the proposed work before anyone discussed permission: ordinary road travel, a short walking passage to be inspected in both directions, seated rests and a public counter which might not answer. No lifting, no use of the injured forearm as a brace, no promise to stay because the question had travelled far.

His current examination did not make the altered sensation disappear. The therapist watched two ordinary walking bouts, with a real seated rest between them, and heard his answers afterwards. Turning without loading the forearm still needed attention.

The limited plan allowed bouts no longer than twelve minutes, no more than forty minutes of walking across the outing, and seated breaks before discomfort became a reason to hurry. Tomorrow's current check could still stop it.

He did not ask whether the permission covered a fight.

It did not.

---

Sitara had her own review, not a copy of his.

Her covered hand stayed out of the work. The walking trial used no hand against a wall, chair or another person. She reported the fatigue she actually felt and took the rest before the next short bout.

Her present limit was ten minutes in a walking bout, forty across the proposed outing, with seated pauses and her own morning check still required. If the passage or the counter demanded more, the task would stop. The route's old familiarity could not add minutes to the body's current allowance.

She said yes to that plan after the explanation.

Both rested after their trials. Neither spent the interval proving that the limits were unnecessarily cautious.

---

They compared the findings later, when both were ready.

The known short passage might fit, if its present entry and return could be inspected without exceeding the smaller limit. They would carry a chair with uninjured help for planned rests, not ask a hurt hand to become a walking aid. The public answer was the part they hoped to obtain, not the condition on which their return would be allowed.

They chose the following morning only after saying those things plainly.

Then came the calls for new quotes.

The old crews had finished their earlier jobs. A new booking would have new people accepting this work as it was described today.

---

The vehicle provider quoted two thousand four hundred rupees for the car and driver, with five separate belted places including the driver's, room for the folded chair and ordinary bags, and the agreed road waiting.

Two uninjured accompanying workers were available for the limited block. One was qualified for the travelling care they requested; the other accepted the equipment, ordinary assistance and chair work. The care worker also agreed to help handle the empty equipment when no clinical task required attention. Both were told there would be an unfamiliar walking passage, no assured phone contact beyond the checked approach and a right to stop at an unsuitable finding.

Each quoted nine hundred rupees for the accepted job.

The chair hire was three hundred. Its worker would check the returned equipment with them; no damage was to disappear beneath a folded seat.

Four thousand five hundred in fixed charges.

It was small enough to fit the cap, substantial enough that nobody would be asked to pretend the work was a favour.

---

The booking block began at a quarter to eight and ended at half past two.

They proposed departure at eight, a return to the road in time for care, food and the driver's own break, and road departure no later than a quarter past twelve. The vehicle's waiting at the approach would not exceed three hours. An unanswered question would not extend the wait.

The driver heard where his role ended: on Earth, beside the ordinary road. He would not be following a missed caller into a passage. There would be a practical contact for the agreed return action if the travellers failed to come back on time.

He accepted the limited booking.

The accompanying workers accepted the same stop conditions and full agreed fees even if the inspection prevented the inquiry. Neither was being asked to earn payment by carrying an injured person through an unfit place.

The hiring worker confirmed the chair and its price.

---

Vedant and Sitara checked the fresh sums they could actually contribute.

Each set aside the two thousand five hundred already chosen. The five thousand was new money for this job, kept separate from patient care payments, completed travel reserves and the old damaged vehicle's unresolved account. Five hundred would remain an unused contingency unless a further cost was explicitly agreed within it.

The driver did not gain an invisible extension because a reserve existed.

They accepted the fixed quotes and the return conditions. A thousand-rupee vehicle deposit would be paid before the trip and deducted once from the vehicle's final charge, not added to it. The remaining fixed payments would be due at the accepted return handover.

For the first time, there was a job definite enough for another person to refuse or accept.

They had not yet left. The money had not made tomorrow's route fit, nor healed either protected limb. It had made the cost of choosing honestly visible before the choice was carried any farther.

The deposit reached the booking contact that afternoon.

A thousand rupees received, the contact confirmed, against the vehicle's two-thousand-four-hundred total. Sitara repeated the remaining vehicle balance before the call ended. Fourteen hundred, not another full charge on top of the deposit. The other fixed fees would be paid in full at return.

The fresh fund now had a recorded payment and an untouched five-hundred contingency.

They did not call that a completed journey.

---

Receiving care was accepted before departure became possible.

The chosen Mirzapur contact heard the proposed return, the two travellers' current limits and the accompanying care worker's role. The ward would receive the practical report and both teachers' care at the agreed return within the booking block. The travelling worker's responsibility would end only after that handover was accepted, not when the car entered the gate.

The chair's return check was arranged too.

After his own leg care and rest, Dhruv chose a short call for the practical plan. Six minutes were agreed; all three were comfortable when they connected. He heard the departure, road waiting and latest return conditions, and agreed to hold the practical timings and receive the planned arrival and return messages when available. The receiving contact was the driver's agreed alternative for the same return action if Dhruv was in care.

He was not an unseen fourth traveller.

"Main phone ke paas baithkar din nahi kaatoonga," he said.

"Wahi nahi chahiye," Sitara answered.

They checked the ordinary contact details and ended the six-minute call. Dhruv took his next rest; neither teacher treated that as a reason to find a more obliging leg.

---

Rudra was asked whether he wanted the practical update later.

After his rest, he chose to hear it: one new job accepted for tomorrow, subject to the morning checks and fresh inspection, none of the four students joining the car. Two teachers had chosen their capped contributions. Nothing was being taken from his care payment or silently carried forward from a closed reserve.

He asked whether they wanted him to add anything to the question.

They did not. The public task was already clear.

He accepted that answer without trying to make himself useful by supplying another person's name. The update ended, the phone was put away, and his own supper and next care continued in Mirzapur.

His mother was not made a witness, his friends not charged for a decision they had not chosen.

---

The two hundred and thirteenth morning began with checks, not with the car horn.

Vedant gave the actual night and present sensation in the forearm. Sitara answered about her hand and fatigue. Neither had a new finding which made the agreed plan unsuitable; the separate walking limits and stop conditions stayed in force. Current readiness meant this small journey could be attempted within them, not that either limb had become sound.

They had breakfast, care and rest before the booked start.

At a quarter to eight the new workers arrived. The qualified care worker heard the current plan directly, checked the practical supports and confirmed the limited responsibility for both teachers. The equipment attendant and the care worker checked the hired chair together with its worker: brakes, folds, seat, foot supports and the fittings which would have to remain secure through the ordinary journey.

The support angle was adjusted before they accepted the chair. They checked it again after the adjustment.

No one called it suitable merely because a different chair had once passed through the rock.

---

The two uninjured workers folded and loaded it themselves.

Vedant carried no share of its weight on the protected forearm. Sitara's covered hand remained clear. The ordinary bags and agreed supplies were secured with the chair, not left where a turn would require somebody to catch them.

The car had the five separate belted places promised.

The care worker checked the teachers' actual seated comfort and the space around the protected limbs before accepting the positions. Both gave their own answers. Nobody had to compress the plan around an extra passenger who had appeared because the destination sounded important.

The driver knew the Earth-side waiting limit and the contact action if the agreed return failed. The plan kept time for his own meal and rest after they returned to the approach, before the road journey back. There was no errand added to the spare luggage space or the margin in the clock.

The new job was accepted as it stood.

---

They left at eight.

Mirzapur moved past in shopfronts still preparing for the day, somebody sweeping a line of dust away from a doorway only for a passing wheel to disturb its edge. The driver waited at a crossing without asking the two teachers whether a few lost minutes should be won back recklessly farther on.

Sitara looked at her resting hand.

She had chosen to carry a question. Other people had chosen the work around that choice, and their choices remained visible in the separate belts, the protected space and the time they were allowed to return with nothing more than a failed attempt.

She did not know what name the counter would give.

For now, the honest part of the journey was the road.

The car reached the agreed road stopping place at twenty-five past nine.

A practical arrival message connected from beside the vehicle and was acknowledged through the agreed receiving contact. The driver kept the latest road-departure time with the ordinary contact details. He did not need to know a patient's family history in order to know when the people he had brought should return.

Both teachers gave their current answers to the accompanying care worker before anybody approached the rock.

The road journey had not produced a new finding which stopped the plan. They rested after getting out, drank with the help actually needed and checked the supported limbs. The two uninjured workers handled the chair and bags.

There was still no inquiry answer.

---

The old natural break was where it had been.

It did not require a device to be restarted or a name spoken into it. Ordinary light reached the first stretch of floor. Sitara looked at the entry as something which needed checking today, not a familiar thing which owed her an easy crossing.

They began at twenty-five to ten.

All four walked the inspection together. The equipment attendant carried the folded chair; the care worker stayed available to the two teachers, whose hands and forearm were not doing the carrying. At the first uneven place they stopped to examine the line before using it.

Vedant pointed without bracing against the wall.

The small hollow had not become a new route. There was room to use the same known line, and no loose obstruction was concealed by declaring that somebody had crossed here before. The check took the time it took.

---

At the tested reach, voice still mattered more than the screen.

The brief phone check did not provide a working call beyond the approach. They checked the voice limit at the turn without sending an injured person farther merely to see whether help could still reach. A sentence was clear at one position, indistinct when rock took its place between the speakers.

They came back within the clear reach, agreed on the actual finding, then continued together. Nobody would be promised a phone conversation from the bench.

The care worker had heard the stop plan before accepting the job. He could use it here without needing to defeat the purpose of the trip. If the floor or a body was unsuitable, they would turn back and the fees would remain the fees.

Sitara found that she was glad he knew that.

It spared her the work of sounding brave for somebody whose pay she might otherwise seem to be endangering.

---

At seventeen to ten they reached the far resting place.

Two spaces were free on the familiar shaded bench. Both teachers sat; the uninjured workers opened the empty chair and checked the wheel line and the turning space without loading a hurt limb to make the test impressive. The ordinary fittings stayed secure.

Beyond them, the small court was already at work.

A length of hanging cloth was being folded inwards from its edge. Someone waiting at the window took a step aside to let the bundle pass, then returned to the place he had been keeping. Water could be heard below the paving. No one stopped working because two people who had come before had returned.

Sitara let the bench take her weight.

The eight-minute outward interval had stayed inside her current walking limit. That was useful information, not permission to forget the return. Vedant gave his own present answer; neither teacher used the view to conceal fatigue from the care worker.

They took the ten-minute seated rest they had planned.

---

At seven to ten they began the inspection back towards Earth.

The slope had to be looked at from this side too. The attendant managed the empty chair through the turn with the usable support angle, while the care worker remained free to attend to the teachers. Neither protected hand was put on a handle.

They stopped where the line needed checking and kept the movement ordinary. The bends did not vanish because the far side had been beautiful. The known opening arrived in Earth light at one minute past ten.

All four came out.

The driver saw them return while his accepted waiting time was still ample. Sitara did not tell him the public question had been answered; the inspection was the work actually done so far.

---

The care worker heard both teachers' present answers at the vehicle.

They sat, rested, drank and checked the current supports. Vedant's forearm still had its altered sensation. Sitara's hand was still covered and out of loading. Sixteen minutes of walking had been used across two short bouts, with the intervening seated pause; the next part would have to fit the remaining allowance, not borrow it from the journey home.

The finding was local and limited: the same line could be walked both ways under the present conditions, within today's stops and care. It was not a certificate that the realm was safe or a reason to bring another patient without a separate choice and review.

They told the practical contact that the inspection had returned. The reply was acknowledged before the phone was put away.

Then each teacher was asked whether the limited inquiry should continue.

Both chose yes, after the actual rest. The accompanying workers separately agreed to the remaining accepted task. The driver confirmed the road waiting still available.

At sixteen past ten, they were ready to carry the question beyond the inspected line.

The second outward crossing began within the fresh choices they had just made.

The attendant handled the empty chair. The care worker stayed with the two teachers, heard their present answers and did not ask either protected limb to contribute to the luggage. They used the same inspected line and reached the far resting place at twenty-four past ten.

Eight more minutes in the walking account.

They did not spend the remaining allowance walking around to see whether another office looked more important. The question had a proposed source. The return already had a claim upon the time and movement left in the day.

---

The attendant set the chair in the clear space after asking whether it would obstruct the people moving cloth through the court.

A woman indicated where the wheels could stay without taking the path her bundle needed. Sitara accepted that place. Vedant found room on the public bench, and both sat while the care worker attended to their comfort. The woman's work continued; she had not been recruited as a guide because she had answered a practical question.

At the window, the queue moved a little and stopped.

Somebody had brought a folded sheet which would not lie flat on the ledge. The receiver held one corner while the owner found the part he wanted. The next person waited with a strip of cloth wound around a bundle of papers, tapping its edge against his leg.

Sitara watched without deciding what any of their clothes must mean.

The everyday court was not simpler because she had returned to it with a new question. It simply had more people in it than the question could contain.

---

The equipment attendant asked whether a short public question could be put after the people already waiting, with the teachers resting nearby until it was their turn.

He gave no patient's name and claimed no priority. The man at the window said they could wait for that place in the sequence; he would hear what they wanted to ask when the preceding people were done. He did not promise an answer before hearing the question.

The attendant returned to the chair.

It was a place to wait, not an audience with a Devta or a passage into a different building. They could leave if the accepted return margin became too small. No one had to invent a reason which would make a public queue ashamed to keep doing its own work.

Sitara gave the care worker her actual answer about the hand. Vedant did the same about his forearm and tiredness.

The waiting could remain seated. That was the condition under which it fitted the day.

---

The papers at the ledge changed owners one by one.

Water sounded below the court. A small gust lifted a hanging corner, and the cloth worker pinned it back while continuing a conversation Sitara could not fully hear. A person leaving the window looked irritated; the next one looked relieved before the receiver had finished pointing at the page.

Neither expression told her the whole matter.

She thought of the student's exact request, not a guessed reason for it. Who was named as the present ruler. A public fact, if the office could give it. No reason to make an injured boy appear in person, or to collect a private account the question did not require.

The question did not need a story to make it worthy of an answer.

She could carry it without inventing one.

---

Before eleven, the receiver looked towards their place.

The care worker checked both teachers' readiness before they rose. They had rested through the queue. The short movement to the ledge would be counted too, not ignored because only the passage felt like travel.

The attendant put the empty chair where Sitara could sit without obstructing the work. Vedant had the stool which the receiver allowed the attendant to bring from beside the ledge; it was returned to use here, not carried away as part of the hired equipment.

The teacher's forearm did not hold the stool or the weight of the person sitting down.

The man at the window was the receiver they had dealt with before. His fingers carried an ordinary smear of ink. Recognition gave them no authority over his morning; it did not turn the same receiver into a private official of their own.

"Boliye," he said.

"Haan," Sitara said. "Sarvajanik baat. Agar aapke kaam ki jaankaari mein aati ho."

He waited for the question rather than its pedigree.

The care worker stayed near enough to help, the attendant kept the equipment clear and the road return still had its margin. It was ten forty-eight by the ordinary watch.

Sitara asked exactly what they had chosen to bring.

"Is waqt Swarg ka shaasak kaun hai?"
