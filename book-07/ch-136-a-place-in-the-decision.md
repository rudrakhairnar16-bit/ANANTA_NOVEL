# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX — A PLACE IN THE DECISION

---

> *"He did not ask to be left without protection.*
> *He asked not to be left outside it."*
> *— Ira's later register, a place kept for an answer*

---

"Tumhaare saath baat karke," Rukmini said. "Jab baat kar sakte hain, tumhe hataakar tumhaari hifaazat ka faisla nahi karoongi."

Rudra watched the supported phone.

"Jo khatra samajh aa raha hai, woh bataaoongi," she continued. "Jo raaste dikh rahe hain, woh bhi. Tumhaari baat sunoongi. Sirf apna darr bataakar baat khatam nahi kar doongi."

It was the answer he had asked for. Not another account of the infant who could not have spoken, but a place for the person speaking now.

"Aur main aapse alag sochoon toh?"

"Toh alag soch rahe hoge. Usse tum phir se bachhe nahi ho jaaoge."

He let his eyes close for a moment.

She was not promising that every disagreement would become easy. She was saying it would remain a disagreement between people who could speak, not evidence that one of them must be excluded until he had learned to choose correctly.

---

He knew there could be moments when danger left no time for a long conversation.

He said so before the answer became a rule neither of them could live inside. His mother agreed: an urgent act to keep somebody alive was not always able to wait. That did not make every later silence urgent, or remove the need to explain what had been done as soon as explanation became possible.

"Har baat ko aakhri pal ki majboori nahi keh sakte," she said.

He heard the admission without stretching it into a promise that he would never be frightened by her decisions again.

"Aapke paas jawaab na ho toh bhi bataana," he said.

"Ki nahi hai?"

"Haan. Woh bhi jawaab hota hai. Mujhe baahar rakhne se alag."

At the other end of the line, she took a little time.

"Theek," she said.

The word had work in front of it. He did not need to pretend the work was already done in order to value it.

---

The eight-minute interval reached its end.

Rukmini needed her next rest. Rudra said goodbye before another question could turn that need into an obstacle she must defend. The call closed, the phone was put aside and the finite setup help finished.

His nurse heard the present answers before moving anything. The wrist and elbow were kept from taking the body's weight; the back's familiar discomfort was reported while it could still be attended to, not endured as the proper ending of a serious conversation. Afterwards the right ankle and left knee needed their supports settled.

The old faint remained unexplained. Being heard had supplied no diagnosis.

He lay still while the room became ordinary around him again.

In Varanasi, Rukmini asked for her shoulder support to be checked and rested after the adjustment. She had not agreed to stay available all afternoon because her son had finally asked a question she should have heard sooner.

The connection was finished. The answer remained.

---

Later, Rudra found that he wanted to test it.

Not with the body. He did not attempt a movement beyond the reviewed help or reach for a thing to prove that choice had made the hand reliable. He wanted to find an enormous question and ask it at once, while the promise still felt warm enough that it might be harder to refuse.

He was not proud of the impulse.

He also recognised the fear beneath it: what if the room she had just made for him closed while he was asleep. What if he should have placed every question inside it before she remembered why she had kept them out.

He could not settle that fear by refusing her the rest he wanted for himself.

For tonight, he let the next question remain unwritten. The two old hearing sheets stayed in the cupboard. There was no new message to a teacher, no request that a route be prepared before the person he wanted to consult had been asked whether she wanted that preparation at all.

---

Supper came with a spoon placed at the wrong angle for the help he needed.

He asked for it to be turned.

The worker changed it, checked whether that was better and continued with the meal. Nobody required a speech about gratitude before making the small correction. Rudra ate, accepted the help and found that the appetite which had seemed absent earlier had merely been waiting for the room to stop asking so much of him.

Afterwards he rested.

There had been no full reconciliation. He was not the infant, newly able to forgive a decision from inside its making; he was the young man who had heard its reason and asked what would happen next.

He could let a promise be tested by the next actual decision, rather than inventing a crisis in which to test it tonight.

Outside the curtain, somebody asked for a little more light. The request was heard. The switch clicked, and his own part of the room stayed dim enough for sleep.

Day two hundred and ten gave him no useful crisis in which to discover whether the promise would hold.

It gave him the review, breakfast, a short part of the page and a back which wanted the page put away before his interest had finished with it. He reported the familiar pull, accepted the adjustment and stopped reading. The ordinary rest which followed was not time taken from a more important task.

After lunch and actual sleep, he requested another conversation with Rukmini.

She chose a ten-minute interval later that afternoon, after her own care and rest, if both present checks allowed. He accepted the time. The small phone setup was agreed, and when they connected neither needed an injured arm to hold the device for the other.

"Mujhe aaj ki baat poochhni hai," he said.

"Poochho."

"Swarg mein ab kaun raaj karta hai, iska naya jawaab lena chahta hoon. Aapki kahaani kisi ko sunaakar nahi."

---

His mother did not answer with a picture of a place she could see from the room.

She had told him about the past she was recounting. The present public answer would need a present source. He was not asking her to become that source by force of relation, or to tell him how to turn a private account into a right to enter every office.

"Kyun poochhna chaahte ho?" she asked.

He considered the easiest answer and did not give it.

"Yeh bol sakta hoon ki bas aapke liye," he said. "Par mujhe bhi jaanna hai. Maine naam sun liya toh apne aap aaj ka poora haal sochne laga. Woh nahi karna."

She waited.

"Aur yeh bhi nahi ki aapko bas wohi yaad karne doon jo main sun sakta hoon. Aaj ki baat mil jaaye toh aapko bataana chahta hoon. Aap sunna chaaho toh."

The offer did not put her on a road.

He had not asked her to be ready to return once the information arrived, or declared that a wish to see home must lead to a claim which her son would enforce.

---

"Sarvajanik baat poochh sakte ho," she said. "Mera naam, meri behen, mere saath jo hua—woh bataana zaroori nahi hai uske liye."

"Nahi bataaoonga."

"Meri baat abhi humaare beech rahegi."

"Haan."

He let the permission keep its proper boundary. She had agreed that a public question could be asked without her private history, not given him that history to use if somebody found the question inconvenient.

"Aur jo bataaye, uski baat mujhe dene se pehle usse bhi poochh lena," she added.

He nodded, then remembered the call was audio and said yes aloud.

"Aap sunna chahti ho?"

"Haan. Jab asli jawaab aaye. Jisne suna, usi ki tarah bataana."

She did not need a son who had witnessed a ruler in person if what he actually received was a report from somebody who had asked at a window.

He was beginning to understand how much care there could be in leaving the steps of a statement visible.

---

They did not choose a traveller during the rest of the call.

Rudra said he wanted to ask Vedant how such an inquiry could be made. His mother did not appoint the teacher, or offer the son's body as a reason a different injured person ought to go. An ordinary public answer might still require work, time and a person's willingness; wanting it was not a way to own those things.

"Main aapse bhi poochh raha hoon," he said. "Pehle khud sab tai karke bataa nahi raha."

"Sun rahi hoon," she answered.

It was the first small use of yesterday's promise. Not a test designed to corner her, but a real decision still early enough for both of them to speak inside it.

He had expected inclusion to feel grander.

Instead, it felt like there was less to hide on the way to doing something.

---

At the ten-minute limit, Rukmini said goodbye.

Rudra returned it and let the call end. The phones were put away; both took the next checks, care and rest which had not been cancelled by an agreeable conversation. His wrist and elbow were protected during the assisted movement. The ankle needed its support settled again before he was comfortable enough to stop watching it.

He gave the actual answer when the nurse asked.

Later he thought about the difference between consulting his mother and asking her to certify that every possible consequence would be safe.

She had done the first. Neither could do the second.

He had no departure to announce, no person already enlisted, no new evidence in the cupboard. He had a question he wanted to obtain a real answer to, and a limit on the private life he was allowed to carry into asking it.

For this afternoon, that was a decision rather than a promise about a decision.

Before supper, after rest and the next check, Rudra asked for the two hearing sheets to be brought from the cupboard for a short reading.

The worker agreed to set them on the stand for that task. Rudra did not try to flatten a corner with the uncertain hand. The angle was checked, and he chose to read the question first rather than stare at the answer until it became more than it was.

It was the actual question they had carried.

The account beneath it still distinguished what Ram had reported from what the work-familiar woman had independently said about the failing balance. Neither source had been asked to identify the present ruler. He could not give the pages a missing answer merely because they had come from Swarg.

At the end of his short interval, he asked for them to be put away.

The worker returned both sheets to the cupboard and finished the little setup task. There was no new mark added, no name squeezed into a margin to make the old inquiry seem more complete.

---

He thought of the window man's hands.

Ink on the fingers, a space cleared on the ledge, the practical insistence that a thing heard be carried as something heard. Rudra had liked the advice when it kept his father's account from being dismissed. He needed to like it equally when it prevented his own hope from being called evidence.

A public answer might be disappointing.

It might be brief. Somebody might not want to give it, or might know only how the office named the person at its head. He could not arrange certainty before he had even asked how to ask.

That was not a reason to remain with guesses.

He let the pages stay put away and took the ordinary rest before the next thing he wanted to do.

---

Veer's request for a little company arrived later.

He had finished his own care and wanted three minutes of ordinary talk, if Rudra had room after supper. Rudra accepted after the meal and rest. Both gave their present answers, the finite phone help was agreed and the call connected with Veer's shoulder and knee supported.

"Page aage badha?" Veer asked.

Rudra gave him the small story of his own objection to a paragraph and the next part which had answered some of it. Nothing from the private family conversations was needed to make the reading argument funny.

"Thodi sulah hui," he said. "Usne agle paragraph mein safai di hai."

"Darr gaya hoga."

"Tere radio waale ko bhi bol doon?"

"Woh alag matter hai."

Rudra smiled, gave the nurse the direct answer when she checked the familiar pull, and let the joke stay small enough for the back to tolerate.

Veer did not need him to become well before they could be foolish together.

---

The conversation found its way to the strange indignity of being interrupted by a sentence written by somebody who was not even in the room.

Veer said the trouble with pages was that they always had one more line available after you had already made up your mind. Rudra said that had never stopped Veer making up his mind halfway through a question. Neither offered to read the other's whole work as a new assignment.

For a minute it sounded like college, not because the facility had disappeared, but because their friendship had not been replaced by the facility's schedule.

The schedule remained.

Veer needed to stop at the three minutes he had chosen. Rudra said goodbye. Both phones were put away; the helpers returned to their own work and the men took their next care and rest. No private story had been offered as the price of company. There was no invitation to an outing, no request that Veer listen for political news on a radio which knew nothing about the realm beyond the rock.

---

Afterwards, Rudra found that the little joke had stayed with him longer than the effort of making it.

There had been nothing especially brave about the call. Veer had not talked him through a dangerous hour or volunteered for a journey. They had occupied three chosen minutes and left each other with something ordinary to remember.

He did not want to turn that into a method of recovery either. Some evenings neither would be able to make the other laugh. Some invitations would be refused. Tonight the company had been wanted and the time had ended while both could still be glad of it.

His mother's private life had remained hers through all three minutes. Keeping that boundary had not required him to become silent in every other part of his own.

---

The light was lowered after the next check.

He did not send a late message to Vedant with the urgency of a question which had grown larger in the dark. A request could wait until a person had a chance to answer it in the morning. Nobody needed to remain on call for an inquiry which had not yet been accepted.

He slept for a while, woke for the care actually due and settled again after giving the present answers.

By morning he still wanted to know.

The wish had survived a night without becoming an emergency. He found that encouraging. It meant he could carry it to another person without first pretending the world would end if the answer took time.

On the two hundred and eleventh morning, Rudra asked whether a brief message could be sent to Vedant after the review.

He dictated the request, heard it back and left out the explanation which would have made somebody else's private life its introduction. He wanted to understand how to ask a public question about present Swarg. Could the teacher choose a short time to discuss that, when his own care allowed.

No journey was requested in the message.

The worker sent the version Rudra had approved and put the phone away. The small task ended. Breakfast and rest did not become a waiting room in which he had to remain alert for the answer.

---

Vedant heard the request after his forearm care.

He did not answer while the support was being checked or treat a student's wish for information as a reason to miss the rest he had planned. Later, he chose eight minutes that afternoon, after both current checks, to discuss the question itself.

He had offered a conversation. He had not volunteered Sitara, promised a current political answer or declared the old passage fit for another visit.

Rudra received the limited offer and accepted it.

That left several ordinary hours which neither man owed to the question.

---

In her own room, Aadhya was trying a different way to keep a thought from getting lost between short reading intervals.

After hand care and rest, she asked for help making one brief voice note about her own course work. The attendant had time for the short recording and playback, not a morning of dictation. Aadhya accepted that limit. The phone was supported; her right thumb and index finger were not asked to prove they could manage the buttons safely.

She began too far from the point.

Halfway through the first version, she stopped herself. It was not a pain which required the halt, and she did not invent one. She had simply realised that she was explaining around the thought instead of saying it.

"Wapas shuru kar sakte hain?" she asked.

There was time within the offered task for one more short attempt. The attendant agreed, deleted the first version at Aadhya's request and started the second.

This one said what she meant.

They played it back. She chose to keep that single note for her own study, with no other conversation included. The phone was put aside; the attendant returned to the work she had left, and Aadhya rested without trying to add one more paragraph to the finished favour.

Her hand's sensation was unchanged.

Her thought was no longer waiting on it to become a perfect hand before it could be kept.

---

Ira wanted a quiet interval instead.

The left covering remained where it belonged. The ordinary right eye had its short, limited work and then closed. She did not need to make the room legible all day merely because a friend elsewhere was finding a way to study.

When the attendant asked whether she wanted the course material read aloud again, Ira said not now.

The earlier offer had not become a standing arrangement; this one could be declined without making the help already received seem wasted. She rested, listened to the room grow quieter and let a part of the day pass without proof that she had used it well.

Neither woman's morning had to reach Rudra as a report.

They were not the audience to which he must announce every question about his family, or the committee whose shared recovery would certify that he could ask it.

---

After lunch, he slept before the offered call.

When he woke, the nurse heard the present answers and helped settle the position without making him hold the wrist against the bed to assist. He told her when the back was uncomfortable, waited through the adjustment and did not confuse the anxiety about a question with a reason to accept pain silently.

He was ready for the conversation after the care, not instead of it.

The brief phone help had been accepted. Vedant had taken his own rest and current check too. They would have eight minutes, and an answer about a possible source could remain separate from a promise that someone would go there.

Rudra wanted to keep that separation clear when they spoke.

---

While he waited for the agreed start, he considered how he would describe the need without handing his mother's story away.

He did not have to invent a harmless excuse for wanting public information. He could say what he wanted to know. The present ruler's name was a question about a realm, not a secret obtainable only by giving a clerk a patient's ancestry.

He did not yet know which place could provide the public answer or whether the person there would choose to help. That was why he had asked for the conversation.

There was a difference between keeping a confidence and building another concealment out of it.

He could decline to tell the private part without lying about the public task.

When the helper asked whether he was still comfortable to begin, he answered yes. At the other end, Vedant had made his own choice. Nobody had appointed a traveller by bringing the two voices into the same call.

Vedant's voice arrived with an ordinary question about present comfort.

Rudra answered it before explaining anything else. He was supported, the wrist did not need to hold the phone and the back's familiar feeling had been checked. Vedant had finished his own care and rest; the protected forearm was not being used to make the conversation possible.

Eight minutes had been accepted at both ends.

"Sarvajanik baat poochhni hai," Rudra said. "Jo aaj ka haal bataaye. Purane jawaab ko naya maan kar nahi chalna."

"Kis baare mein?"

"Swarg mein raaj karne waala kaun hai. Uska naam."

He had said the actual task without giving his mother's name, birthplace, exile or the name she had told him privately. The teacher had not been made a recipient of those things merely by being asked about a public question.

Vedant listened.

---

"Abhi kisi ko jaane ko nahi bol raha," Rudra added.

"Maine suna."

The reply was calm enough to make him notice how quickly he had begun defending himself against a refusal which had not come.

"Poochhne ka tareeqa samajhna hai. Uske baad jo karna hoga, woh alag se baat karke."

"Theek."

Vedant did not ask Rudra to begin with a family history before the request could be considered serious. Nor did he promise a trip while another person's name might still be hidden inside the word we.

Rudra felt the difference between a teacher hearing him and a teacher agreeing with everything he might eventually want.

For now, the hearing was enough.

---

He said he had looked again at the old sheets.

Their answer about the failing balance remained what it had been: the pre-birth account Ram had actually shared, the separate work-familiar affirmation, the questions about cause and repair which had not been solved merely because the hearing had taken place. He had found no public ruler-status answer in them because that had not been their question.

Vedant agreed that a new inquiry could have a smaller subject without undoing the old one.

"Ek jawaab ki jagah doosra rakhne ki zaroorat nahi," he said.

Rudra looked towards the cupboard.

He had been worried that returning to a realm for another fact would somehow turn the first journey into a mistake. It had given them what they had actually obtained there. This different question did not need to disguise itself as the completion of that hearing.

He could ask something new without telling himself the earlier work had been worthless.

---

Outside his curtain, a wheel caught briefly against an uneven join.

The worker stopped, corrected the angle and moved on. The sound belonged to this facility, not to the passage Rudra remembered. His body had not been invited into another crossing by the simple act of speaking about Swarg.

He would remain in his present care unless an actual different plan were chosen and reviewed. His friends remained people with their own unfinished days, not the travelling form of his curiosity. His parents had their own rooms and limits in Varanasi.

There was no hidden set of seats filled by the question he had just asked.

He wanted to keep it that way while the request was still only a request.

---

"Agar kisi se poochhna pade," he said, "toh unhe meri har wajah bataana zaroori hoga?"

"Jo kaam maang rahe ho, woh saaf hona chahiye," Vedant answered. "Niji baat maangne ka haq apne aap nahi milta."

The answer did not promise that every stranger would respect a boundary. It told Rudra he did not have to offer the boundary up before anyone had even asked.

He could be honest about wanting information. He could also decline to make another person's pain its supporting document.

For a long time he had treated secrecy and a complete public account as the only two choices. There was a plainer middle thing: tell the person what work was being requested, let that person choose, and keep the private material out of a task which did not require it.

He was learning that late enough to regret it, early enough to use it now.

It was easier to demand a place in a decision than to occupy that place without demanding a good outcome as well. His mother had listened to the wish for a public answer. He could no longer put the whole question back into her hands merely because the next step might disappoint him. Inclusion meant he would have to hear other people's limits too, including the possibility that a teacher could suggest a source without choosing to fetch its answer. He wanted to be the person who could receive that distinction. He did not need to be perfect at it before trying.

---

The nurse's quiet check came before he asked the last thing.

He gave the present answer. Vedant took the pause for his own adjustment and returned ready to continue within the accepted interval. Neither used the interruption to promise an expedition or extend the call beyond its limit.

Rudra's mother had given him a place in the decision. She had not given him every other person's place too. The distinction mattered most at the moment when he might be tempted to speak as if all the help had already been chosen.

He would ask for the source, not issue an instruction to reach it.

The teacher was listening, the question was finally small enough to say clearly, and there was still time left in their call.

"Swarg mein ab kiski hukumat hai—yeh bina kisi ki niji kahaani bataaye kis sarvajanik jagah se poochh sakte hain?"
