# CHAPTER FIFTY-ONE — THE FORM THAT WOULD NOT HOLD

---

> **TRANSMISSION 77 / MIDNIGHT RELAY / DESK OF THE HIGHER COUNTS**
> *"SATWA-CAMP SIGNALS LOST AT GATE SIX. INSTRUMENT READING AT -4 HAS STABILIZED, WHICH IS THE INSTRUMENT'S WAY OF CONFIRMING THE NUMERAL. RECOMMEND: NO ACTION AT GHAT. RECOMMEND: INSTRUMENTS TO THE DISTRICT PROPER. THE DISTRICT WILL DO WHAT DISTRICTS DO WHEN A NUMBER WANTS ANSWERING — THEY WILL TRY TO ANSWER IT IN PUBLIC."*
>
> *— cipher-card received at the Abacus Bureau, folio "CONTINGENT," never answered*

---

The kasba woke before its cocks. The register is empowered to report this because everyone in the kasba had their reasons, and every reason broke cover at the same hour — four-fifteen, the hour that belongs to no one, the hour the district calls **guru-ghari** because the teachers of the world can take attendance in it at their leisure. RV on the mill-pressure, dawn-count, three on baton-board. The storm was four days gone and the thing that had risen to fill the storm's schedule was not, strictly speaking, a schedule — it was a *rhythm*, the register wants you to know, for the count had changed, as all counts do that have to be built back up once their heights have been subtracted and placed in a separate ledger — the count now walked on two legs, as Rudra had promised it would.

*Breakfast* (*first thing*): *the old-haat time, open or shut* — *not applicable* — the kasba's loudest announcement was that there was *no announcement*.

There was, however, a *time-table*. It had arrived on the chauk's notice-pin the evening before, and said (in the three columns the district's keeper of cognates insisted on, and he was a kind of widow for usages; he was a kind of widower of usages), as follows:

**SUBAH — PANI — AASMAAN — ZAMEEN**
**DUPAHAR — KHATIYA — PATH — CHAR**
**SHAM — JAL — GAAV — GHAR**

*(Morning — water — sky — ground. Noon — ledger — path — four. Evening — water — village — home.)*

Which means, in the district's grammar — and the district always understands its own grammars — *training*. The nouns do not have the listing of a noun, because this was not the listing. The nouns are the interrogators of the nouns *before*, the hours in which they must be measured: what the water carries at morning-scale; what the sky admits at noon-scale; what the ground is at night, in the areas in which night is not dark to the local arithmetic. The timetable, therefore, was a *curriculum of counting* — of letting the thing *come to* the count.

It was Vedant who had put it up.

At dawn, punctual as the *karah* chain, they came. Veer came with a bag rolled into a quest—boxer's attitude and never looking quite like a wrestler until he ran first, the way he did, vexably; he never looks like a wrestler until he starts running, and then the running is the wrestling. He ran warm through the town roads at exactly guru-ghari, when the streets were having their second discussion with glory after the first had ended in excess of praise. Ira came with her maps, re-kissed, drawn, crossed out; she folded them under a brass ward the way Ram's letters-of-acknowledgment get folded under the ward of count after count. Aadhya came without forethought and without instruments, which is to say she came with the green and with her thumbs stained, and the register reports that her thumbs were already doing the fourth-beat arithmetic on her knee before her father's tea was poured — the house's own tea, which waited the way the day's first entries wait — and Aadhya's arithmetic ran to the property's edge at four-thirty with the small exactness of a register being closed.

Rukmini stood at the east window with her wick-reeds, watching him come. Her grammar had no modes left for *suprise* — the register remarks, from long acquaintance, that her grammar had not surprised itself since the day the lamp in this window had gone out of its own accord.

The five pupils closed on the deep-walled chamber — which the register calls "chamber" with the reluctance of someone in the spelling room; the kasba's grammar has a better name: **paath-shala** *(path-school,* which is not a "school," and is truer) — the praying pit of the old akhada, whitewashed four days back — the four who had done the whitewash-standing had signed their names into the eastern plaster in the manner of whitewash autographs the panchayat-tree keeps ungraded. At panch-gate turn they halted. The sky was still glistening with the night-counts lit up by their training. Something had selected that particular range of the sky to keep the light off of it until guru-ghari, which is the time teachers arrive, and the time the sky believes the teacher is coming.

Dhruv came not from the bed-platform but from the open outer circuit from the road-train, which is time, the register has determined, and so arrived — as you would expect — a little late, being a little — and the register trusts him — irrecoverably — *by a star*. He was counting under his breath, though nobody could hear him count, because there were no numbers in the count. His students have mentioned this count on Tuesdays before assembly.

The twenty-four bows by twenty-four pipes were knit loosely as the ground's seven swirls into eight so that it may laterally organize and clean. The register permits only so much simile in one row. In plainer grammar: nine bouts had been written into the pit-wall across nineteen years, and a ninth more were owed by the time the nineteenth ran its civic course. There had been one fall this week; there would be a second by Friday.

As for the count *inside* the paath-shala — the count of muscle, of breath, of river-water gulping the insider's count from the leaves above them — the count was seven. It always was.

Dhruv's diction at guru-ghari was minimal. He said:

**"Aaj se ginti poore naamu ki hogi. Ghin do."** *(From today the count is complete in names. Give them.)*

After which, Veer rose. He was not speaking. Veer had been eleven days on the water's arithmetic and four days on the sky's, and the grammar the register has to use to describe the result — *hath-asta-shastra* or *hand-without-arms* — puts into the district's put-together grammar what Vedant would have called the arithmetic of the *teacher's purpose*: *not weapons, but weather*. Veer's putting-together is what the register uses because Veer's putting-together is the kind of thing the kasba's arithmetic registers.

He walked to the center whitewash, knelt, placed both palms flat on the pit's sand, and began the count:

**"Char in. Char out. Char. Ek. Do. Teen. Char. Char."**

*(Four in. Four out. Four. One. Two. Three. Four. Four.)*

And the pit sand underneath him *listened*.

It wasn't that the sand *heard* — the sand is not literate in kasba arithmetic — it was that the sand *moved* in the count. Each time Veer reached *char-char*, the sand under his palms *settled*. Not compacted — *settled*, the way a ledger dries when the book is closed. On the ninth repetition the register notes (because this is what a register does — it counts) that the settling reached the man's elbow notches, which meant he was in it. On the eleventh, it reached his shoulder. On the thirteenth, the register reports — factually, for the first time in these ten chapters — that the sand *stopped*.

Then Veer's hands came up, and the sand stayed exactly where his hands had left it, flat, the way soft things lie after long-pressure (and here the register's parenthesis is shameless, because the register has just lost its only two wail-lamps), exactly as the keeper-of-arithmetic — in the last unpublished work of your life though he is now — was holding the ledger's final pen: *flat against chest* — he had counted, four-in, four-out, four-final-rest, and the *rest* was *here*.

He sat back, palms down, and said, without arrogance — *with exhaustion*, which the register respects more:

**"Ginti mera roop hai. Chaar mera naam hai. Aage likh do."** *(The count is my form. Four is my name. Write onward.)*

The register does so. The register will, when the third student's name is called on Friday, refuse. Its grammar, being the register's grammar, has been specially modified so that it can withstand its own completions. Until then.
