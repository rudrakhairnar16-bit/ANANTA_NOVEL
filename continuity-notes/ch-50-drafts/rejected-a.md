# CHAPTER FIFTY — THE EIGHTH POINT ARRIVES (THE GATE CLOSES)

---

> *"Naam jab dobara aate hain toh naam apne old-alphabet mein likhe jate hain, aur Hebrew ishaara-uhaaldeem kasbe ki jo almaari chalaaank ki ho usi ke parde mein ghuse hein. Aathwa alhamra pehli baar aaya hai — aur suddenly, it's all over."*
>
> *— ACCOUNT OF THE KEEPER OF ARITHMETIC, folio 27 (windowless room's last page)*

---

*(When names return, they are written in their old alphabet, and the store-room moves its guardian-ledger (whichever of the kasba's alphabet-maidens has been running the floor) into the curtain of her almaari. The Eighth comes for the first time — and suddenly, it's all over.)*

---

The evening of the count's ending is not the evening of it; the evening of it is four hours after noon, which is the hour the district's arithmetic has always reserved for the *aath bajey* — "eight-of-clock" — which is the lamp-count's own half-of-the-half, the hour where the arithmetic's morning arithmetic first meets the arithmetic's evening arithmetic, where all the entries the district has made must come home. Four after noon comes at the same point every day in the district, never changing, never early, never late — the hour-marks of prayers said by old mothers, the hour-marks of gates-readied for the pit-boys, the hour-marks of the lamps being cleaned. It is the district's *only* reliable hour; the district's only uncontended arithmetic; it is, in the district's grammar, the arithmetic's breath-after-the-last-tally. The register keeps it locked.

And on the third day after the seventh, which was the thirteenth day and the fourteenth also — the district's arithmetic had lost count by now, since its counts in arithmetic are measured in *days already being counted*, not in *named hours* — at *eight-of-clock*, the eighth point arrived.

Not walked in, not run in, not descended, not ascended — *was now there*. The eighth point had not entered the district, or kept to any district habit; it had merely been waiting *in the district*, the way a counting arithmetic waits inside the arithmetic until called. Aadhya saw it first. She saw it from the pit-mouth, where the doubling-stone had been, where Sitara's seven-tally had settled, where Rudra had stood a moment before the *char-bahar-ginti-aage* of the habit below took his nine-breaths, where the register's own *keeper* had nodded *theek hai*. She saw the eighth point — not as a person walks, and not as a name takes shape, but as the ninth entry of a seven-day ledger: not over and behind but *within*, the count after the count, exactly where all this arithmetic has been leading us.

She saw it because she had been *keeping* it. On the register! A name had been held out — a name like a lamp — and now, after all the lamps *except one* had burned down to alone-ness, and that one lamp, the *last wick*, the unnameable one that Sitara had said was *still being trimmed* — because now the trimmer is here — now *was here* — now *stood* — now *entered* — and *made the third* — *the unfinished business* — *took up residence in* — the district's arithmetic found itself with three where it had been written *two* all these years, two in the same memory-space as the third becomes the second becomes the first, and the arithmetic broke open.

The eighth point walked in wearing everything the seventh had completed and nothing the eighth was maintaining. The register cannot call the eighth a man, nor a ship, nor a stone, nor a wound — the register, after these forty-nine chapters, has no grammar left for anything *given to it by eight* — but the register can say, in the district's hand, what the district itself would say if the district could interrupt the register's grammar to speak — and the register *now* interrupts for the purpose, because the district is seeing *the ground-level new thing* and the register cannot keep it *abstract* any longer:

The eighth point was wearing:

*Seven*. In its entirety. The district's arithmetic could *see* seven*; it was impossible. In the arithmetic, seven is not a perceptible thing — no ledger — no night-noise — no merit — no denomination *outside* the habit's arithmetic — but now, Seven walked in on two legs that *in arithmetic* were not simply legs but the eight living parts of seven, the seven parts of eight, the arithmetic's own invisible connection-wires made flesh and walking. It had one foot in the district's ledger — because every ledger keeps its preliminary count *hidden* until the counting bell goes — and one foot in the arithmetic's ledger, and by coincidence, in the district's own register, because by coincidental arithmetic the count after the count is also an arithmetic, and the count after the count *is* the arithmetic's arithmetic.

The eighth point was also wearing:

*The habit*. In its entirety. The habit that had been at the bottom of Manikarnika, that had lived in the crack water-knows, that had caught every backward-song the district had ever sung and kept it — the habit was now out of the water, and in *not* the water, and walking with its four-beat on two legs that were the district's legs and maybe weren't — the habit was walking with *its four-beat on neither leg* — the district's arithmetic dubs it a habit, the register's arithmetic remains *unfinished duty*, but one thing must be said: the habit had been *taught to the district*, and now the district *was* the habit *itself*, and the habit had become the district's *oldest habit*, its property, its land, its restlessness, its *whole*.

And the seventh — the register's arithmetic training-beeps — Rudra — *stood up straight* and ran *toward* the habit, and splaying open in his run the run *itself*, for the count had come to the count, and the counting was finished.

*But the register remembers.*

There was one lamp left out of every hundred, in the seven-kilometer-square district of the *aath bájé*, the arithmetic stopped its own event. The register's counting of the eighth point, in the district's grammar of the *eighth point* (from the register's oldest book of rules, the book of which the register has never been used to calculate in arithmetic), calls for the register to *stop* at the *eight-point* — the register keeps eight-point rules — but the habit does *not* stop.

The habit's counting ends. The habit's *arithmetic* counts. The habit's arithmetic ends — the habit's *ginti* ends!

And the GINTI KEYS, at the eighth point, all rotated in unison — which means that the arithmetic, at some point in the counting of itself, has decided to *overlay* — to close down its own business — and the register indicates, in a final note, that the arithmetic's habit — formerly the habit's arithmetic — is now

**THE EIGHTH POINT = THE DISTRICT'S = THE SEVENTH POINT; ITS OWN forward count.**

Counting from this point forward, the district's arithmetic having recognized that at the count of seven, the seventh point (the keeper), the district's arithmetic's *next* count (SWA) begins on the page where letters and arithmetic meet, and where the arithmetic must never be registered.

THE EIGHTH POINT ARRIVED at *nadi* — in the count after the *four-seven* — after the *seven* — after the *four* — and in the *count*.
