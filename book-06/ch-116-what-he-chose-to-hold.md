# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN — WHAT HE CHOSE TO HOLD

---

> *"In the first drawing, someone gave him a planted foot.*
> *The worker's account crossed it out."*
> *— Ira's later reconstruction, margin note*

---

The medical driver released the stretcher's brakes and pulled it away from the rising vehicle.

The worker moved with him, keeping Rudra's head end level. The first wheel bumped over loose gravel. Rudra kept the right leg on its support, the recovering wrist against the padding and the other hand open beside him instead of reaching for a tonne of metal.

The man stepped sideways with the ambulance's rear frame in his hands.

Its front tyres dragged across the approach. The rear body swung towards the place the bed was leaving; the open door banged against its damaged check and folded farther back.

"Aur door!" the road constable shouted.

The crew pulled together.

The rear bumper struck the bed's trailing wheel fork as the man let go.

Metal cracked. The stretcher jolted sideways. The worker caught the frame before it could tip, and the driver braced the other end with both hands, shouting for the constable. The officer reached the nearest handle as the ambulance dropped onto its tyres.

The impact shook the gravel beneath all of them.

Rudra's ankle hit its padding hard enough to make his vision whiten at the edges. He said where it hurt when the worker asked. He was still answering; he had not left the bed. He told the worker about the brief whiteness as it cleared.

The vehicle's rear stood diagonally across the widening now.

The space through which they had meant to roll was gone.

---

One stretcher wheel no longer turned inside its bent fork.

The driver tried the empty side of the frame once, saw it drag and stopped. Pulling harder would turn the whole loaded bed. The worker kept Rudra's leg steady while the road constable looked towards the narrow outer strip and the low earth bank beside it.

They could get a person onto that firm ground. They could not force this damaged bed along it without tilting him.

"Sheet abhi neeche hai," the worker said. "Teenon. Chhota move."

The carry sheet remained under Rudra from the earlier transfer. Before the move, the worker put Rudra's phone in a closed pocket of his medical bag and told him where it was. The crew lowered the frame the remaining distance they could safely manage. The constable helped spread the blankets on the firm ground against the bank, then took the side the worker showed him.

Rudra told them he could not stand to make it easier.

"Khada kar bhi nahi rahe," the worker said.

The three lifted by the sheet, keeping the supported leg and trunk together. They moved only the short width between bed and blankets. The driver had to reset one foot before they lowered; the others waited with the load shared rather than dropping their end early.

Rudra came down on the folded support with his upper back against the bank's shallow slope.

The damaged stretcher was empty beside them.

---

The armed constable called to the man from beyond the vehicle's front quarter.

"Bas. Haath dikhaao. Medical madad milegi."

The man's freshly wounded arm hung closer to his side. His other hand gripped the bent rear door. He pulled it against the hinge, stopped with his face tightened and changed his feet.

The gun remained trained towards the gap beside the engine, not through Rudra and the crew. The ambulance's diagonal position hid the man's body from that view whenever he moved back towards its rear. The constable went farther along the firmer inner side, trying to open the angle without walking into the space the door could sweep.

His colleague at the outward road reported the damaged transport and the need for another medical vehicle. The police car could not provide Rudra's protected lying place by wishing a rear seat flat. The first ambulance was still ahead on its receiving journey.

No driver was ordered to bring an ordinary car into the narrowing fight.

The medical worker checked Rudra after the jolted transfer, including his pulse and pressure after the reported change in his vision. Breathing, answers, foot, support, collar: questions and actual hands, while the original road constable kept the immediate approach in view.

The collar covering had a fresh red mark.

The worker applied clean pressure and checked the ankle's position again. Rudra gave the new pain without replacing it with a word about courage.

---

The next few minutes arrived in torn pieces of metal.

The man dragged the door against its hinge again. The remaining check broke with a sharp report; the lower hinge held longer. Each time he pulled, the vehicle shifted on the gravel and the fresh wound marked another part of his sleeve.

The armed officer kept moving for a clear view. The man moved back behind the engine or the rear body when that view began to open, never standing still in the same gap long enough to become an easy answer.

The driver crouched beside the unusable stretcher. He could help the worker move Rudra another short way if they had to. He could not repair a bent wheel while the person who bent it was still tearing at the vehicle.

The last hinge began to separate.

Rudra watched the sheet of metal come away in the man's hand.

The first ambulance reached the receiving entrance before the car.

A nurse and a clinician met Dhruv's stretcher. The travelling worker gave the stone strike, the bleeding, the supported leg and the new ache after he had stopped moving. He included the shoulder recoil and the trembling sound hand, not only the cut which could be seen through its dressing.

Dhruv answered the receiving questions himself.

The leg was examined again. Imaging was arranged; until the examination and investigations were complete, he was not to test it by walking. The nurse helped keep the old hand out of the supported transfer onto the receiving bed.

Vedant's forearm got its own handover.

The clinician checked the altered finger feeling and the tremor after the bottle effort. The arm stayed supported. The old morning's light-use permission was not rewritten over the new symptoms. Vedant did not ask for water to demonstrate which movement had brought them on.

Both men were now with the team which would continue their care.

The crew's account of Rudra ended where it honestly ended: handed to the second worker, awake, injured, awaiting that vehicle's departure. They had no eyes at the road now.

---

The car stopped at the entrance it had been booked to reach.

Sitara unfastened her belt and got out with the bag in the unhurt hand. She told the nurse about the officer in the rear seat before asking where she should leave anything.

The receiving help came with a chair. The constable was assisted through the short move, the dressed calf protected, his account and first-worker findings passed on. The driver stayed with the car. He did not become the person responsible for a painful transfer because he had stopped at the right door.

Once the officer was actually with the receiving staff, Sitara turned back to the booking.

The driver showed her the journey and the two added waiting intervals. She checked them against the recorded agreements: the original return fare, the first ten paid minutes and the second ten-minute block accepted after the new risk report. They had left within that final block. There was no third wait to argue about.

She paid the agreed total from the transport amount she had checked, keeping the other reserved obligations separate.

His phone sounded.

He looked at it, opened the payment and confirmed the amount received.

"Poora aa gaya," he said.

She saved the acknowledgment beside the booking, took the bag clear and thanked him for the work he had actually done.

He checked his rear seat, shut the door and drove away.

The journey and that driver's work were finished.

---

Sitara did not go straight from the car to somebody else's bedside.

She gave the receiving desk her own name and the report of the knuckles striking rock. She said the swelling had increased and that gripping the bag strap with that hand was no longer comfortable.

The nurse found her a seat with the bag on the floor, reachable without a heavy lift.

The hand was examined, the small wound cleaned again and the covering replaced. She was told what further review the persistent pain needed and to keep the hurt hand out of lifting meanwhile. Rest did not make her useless. She leaned back against the chair and allowed it to take the weight she had been carrying through her shoulders.

The injured constable was being examined nearby, his calf no longer hidden by torn cloth. Dhruv and Vedant had their own receiving places, not a shared corridor assembled for a conversation.

For a little while Sitara could hear no stone moving at all.

---

She asked Dhruv and Vedant separately whether their arrival and current care could be reported to the three friends.

Both agreed to that much.

Her message was short: they had arrived, had been handed over and were being assessed. The officer and she were also receiving care. She had no new direct news from Rudra after leaving him with the second crew.

Veer sent back that he had finished his own review. Aadhya would stay with the movement and rest already planned for her hands. Ira replied after her eye-rest interval, asking for the next confirmed arrival rather than a stream of reassurance. None of them came to the road to make friendship measurable in another injured body.

Sitara put the phone beside her and asked for drinking water when the nurse next came past.

This time there was someone to bring it without leaving a fight.

---

The receiving desk got the new transport request through the ordinary medical contact.

The second vehicle was damaged at the approach. Its patient had not left. Police were keeping the outer road clear but could not yet offer a safe loading place beside the damaged vehicle.

The first crew reported their handovers complete and their own present ability to make one further run. Dispatch checked the vehicle and replacement stretcher availability before arranging it. They would go only as far as the outer police holding point until current access was confirmed.

Neither recovering man was put back aboard.

Sitara heard only the functional news the desk could share with her: another transport was being arranged for Rudra. She did not hear a new injury diagnosis or a promise that somebody had already reached him.

She stayed for her hand's review.

At the road, the man swung the freed door towards the armed constable and let it go.

The officer dropped behind the engine's forward corner. Metal struck rock, rebounded against the vehicle and fell with the broken hinges uppermost. He came out on the other side of the corner with the weapon still in his hands.

The man had already turned towards Rudra.

The road constable moved between the head of the empty stretcher and the crew, lathi raised across the next reach. The man's forearm struck it. Wood cracked; the officer released the twisting upper half before it could take his wrist with it, keeping only the piece below the split.

He stepped back towards the worker, still upright, not holding a doorway with his ribs.

"Peechhe. Saath hato."

The medical driver reached for the carry sheet.

The worker was putting his phone away. The receiving contact had given him the two facts he could repeat: the first patients were there, and replacement transport was being arranged. He had ended that call when the door rose. Nobody at the other end could see what came next.

---

Rudra tried to bring his good forearm between the reaching hand and the worker at his head.

His elbow shook before he lifted it clear of his body.

"Main khada nahi ho sakta," he said. "Aap peechhe jaaiye."

"Khada hona bhi nahi. Hum yahin hain."

The worker moved out of the reaching line. He did not demand that the arm remain raised to earn his return.

The man's fingers caught the edge of the blanket instead. He pulled. The protected leg shifted with the fold, and Rudra cried out before the worker and driver could keep the support in place.

The road constable struck the gripping wrist with the remaining length of lathi.

The man let the blanket go and turned that hand towards him.

Rudra's forearm reached it first.

Heat ran from his own chest through the shoulder and along the raised arm. The old red-black outline appeared unevenly at the elbow. Gold remained a separate broken brightness beneath it.

The impact drove the arm back against him.

His upper back pressed hard into the folded support. He lost the count at three and took the next breath without pretending he had reached four.

---

The worker checked that the right foot had not rolled off its support.

Rudra could hear him asking about it. He could answer. The pain was sharp and local; the brief whiteness from the vehicle's drop had not returned. He gave both facts between the effort of keeping the next hand away from his face.

The outline flickered.

He wanted to straighten the elbow and make the man fly backwards.

Beyond that man's shoulder were the bent vehicle, the armed constable changing position and the road which the outer officer was keeping clear. Rudra had not seen the ending of a blast from this low angle. He could not borrow the policeman's earlier view of a rock backdrop and pretend it was his own.

Warmth tightened at the palm.

He closed the fingers before any force left them.

The man did not move because he had wished for a safer choice.

His free hand came down towards Rudra's collar.

Rudra bent the good elbow closer to his side and turned the forearm across that reach, keeping the pressure away from the recovering wrist. The strike glanced along the failing outline and drove his shoulder against the bank.

Pain travelled through the old bruised place in his back.

The ground stayed under him.

He named the new pain aloud. The worker heard and moved round the head end, out of the reaching hand's path, keeping him in view.

---

The armed constable could see the attacker again, but Rudra was directly below the man's chest.

He stopped his trigger hand and moved farther along the bank. He needed distance between bodies, not permission to treat the lower one as less important. The original road constable dropped the splintered remainder and kept the clinical worker's retreat clear while the driver moved the supply bag away from the swinging feet.

Rudra saw only parts of that work.

A boot clearing the bag. The worker's sleeve. The end of the broken lathi no longer in anybody's grip. He would not know how much had been done for him until somebody told him later.

The man caught his good forearm.

Rudra tried to pull free. The outline stretched thin along the shoulder and collar, and he stopped trying to win that way. The right ankle was still lying on the support; the left knee could not push his hip round without the pain climbing into it.

He could not make the body provide the stance he remembered from the yard.

He let the elbow stay bent.

Let the forearm come closer instead of dragging the shoulder open. The man's grip was still there. Rudra did not take its force inside himself. He changed the direction of his own effort, keeping it close along the arm which was actually being held.

The red-black drew tight against his skin.

The outline stopped fluttering at the bend of his arm.

It did not spread away from him in a wave. The red-black thickened where he kept his own effort, close against the forearm, then along the upper arm into the shoulder. When the man pulled again, that density travelled with Rudra's bent elbow instead of thinning across an arm he could no longer straighten safely.

He drew air in a short, unsteady breath.

The inside of the held bell seemed very near. He heard no instruction there. He had the worker's voice outside it, asking him whether he could still feel the foot.

"Haan," he said. "Dard hai. Mehsoos ho raha hai."

He stayed with that answer and the arm in front of him.

The force was his. The hand around it was somebody else's. He did not have to throw one through the other to know the difference.

---

Red-black drew across his chest.

The old outline had come in patches under the blows: elbow, shoulder, a thinning edge along the leg. This was closing into one continuous shape. Its surface lay nearer the body, dark and dense enough that the dust passing before it showed pale. Gold still ran separately beneath and between those darker lines, bright at the sternum, broken at the collar.

The new density reached the hip, then both legs.

Rudra felt the right ankle inside it exactly where the support held it. Nothing in the joint reset. The left knee still hurt when he tried to change its angle. He let the attempt end and kept the leg where the worker had put it.

The shape completed down to his feet without requiring him to stand on them.

The worker took an involuntary half-step back, then came round far enough to keep Rudra's face in view. He did not put a hand into the dark surface to test what it could bear.

"Saans ka jawab dete rehna," he said.

Rudra nodded once.

The collar stung at the movement.

---

The man pulled harder at his forearm.

Rudra did not try to match the pull by locking his elbow straight. He kept the bend and turned his own shoulder a little, no farther than the support and the injured back would permit. The bank took the pressure through the folded cloth behind him. He pressed the trapped forearm up and away from his chest instead of tugging it towards his lap. The man's elbow had to rise with that pressure. Loose grains slid beside Rudra's hip.

He felt the load in his body. It was not being sent down into a realm which would take it for free.

The man had to move his foot.

Only one placement, back from the bank, but a whole placement made because Rudra's arm had not come where the grip wanted it.

The man looked down at that arm.

Rudra saw the change in his face without giving it a thought he could not hear. His mouth tightened; he widened the stance and tried a different angle. The fresh wound marked the hand which had been clamped over it earlier. His arm had not healed because the patient in front of him had changed.

Rudra's own good hand began to tremble inside the dense outline.

He loosened the fingers a fraction, keeping them out of an outward aim, and left the forearm doing the work it could actually do.

---

He knew the old shape well enough now to know this was not merely its brief edge returning.

The darkness held through the movement from shoulder to wrist. The whole body-line remained while he changed the angle, including the painful legs which he was not using as a stance. The effort was larger, deeper and frighteningly present in the space he had decided to keep small.

He had spent weeks discovering how late a hand could mistake itself for an ending.

Now he could feel the beginning asking to become more than he needed.

He did not give it the road.

His gaze went to the worker, then the driver crouched outside the reaching hand. Both were still there. The original constable had a clear strip in which to help them retreat. Rudra could keep the next movement beside his own body, where he could see what it touched.

He said the first word through the torn place in his cheek.

"Unbound."

Then, with ordinary Earth grit beneath his hip and no foot planted in his father's ruined streets, he gave this shape the name of the part of his inheritance he was choosing to carry.

**"UNBOUND: PATAAL."**

---

The man drove his free fist towards Rudra's shoulder.

Rudra met it with the outside of the guarded forearm and turned, keeping the movement short. The blow still went through him. His back struck the folded support harder; the left knee pulled as his hip shifted, and he cried out without letting that cry become another release.

The fist went past his collar instead of into it.

The new outline stayed whole.

That was what this contact proved. Not that he could do it again tomorrow, not that he could stop everything coming towards him now.

The man still had a grip on his arm.

Rudra changed the angle once more, close to his own chest.

This time the man had to take a second step.

The man loosened his grip to reach again.

Rudra's good hand closed on that wrist before it could pass his face. He kept the hold below the fresh upper-arm wound, not on the torn place itself. The man tried to turn out of it, and the dense red-black travelled along Rudra's forearm without splitting away from his shoulder. The fingers closed back against Rudra's forearm.

He could feel how much of himself the movement was using.

A nail edge opened. Blood marked the scraped palm. The tremor had become visible even through the outline. He did not close harder to pretend it was gone.

"Haath se khoon," he said.

"Sun liya," the worker answered. "Gardan bhi check kar raha hoon."

The man had been pushed back far enough for the worker to reach from the head side without putting himself between the two held arms. He checked the collar and put a fresh clean fold against the thin bleed. Rudra held it lightly with the recovering hand. That wrist took cloth, not his body's weight.

The worker withdrew from the next reach.

He did not tell Rudra the new shape made another injury acceptable.

---

The driver and road constable took the sheet's available edges.

They could move Rudra farther along the bank if the gripping arm came free. They could not haul his body sideways while the man still had a hand against the same arm and call the shoulder's turning a necessary part of the transfer.

"Mauka mile toh seedha peechhe," the worker told them. "Abhi kheenchna nahi."

Rudra heard the plan.

He did not need to stay in this place to finish proving what the shape could do. He tried to lower the captured wrist towards the ground beside him, keeping it away from the hands on the sheet.

The man resisted.

For a moment their arms barely moved. The difference was in the ground beneath the man: a heel slid, found another patch and planted again. Rudra's hip pressed harder into the support; the left knee pulled without taking any useful part in the effort.

He reported it.

The clinical worker kept his leg in view. There was no command to put the foot down and give the force a stronger stance.

"Kitni der?" the worker asked.

"Nahi pata."

It was the only honest answer he had for how long this new density would remain. He could keep choosing the present effort. He could not promise the next minute into being.

---

The armed constable had reached the upper edge of the firm bank.

The medical driver saw him and kept his own body out of that line. The officer lowered the muzzle a fraction when Rudra and the attacker's forearms crossed again. He could see the fresh blood on the upper arm; he could also see the boy beneath it. No new outline turned the lower body into a permissible backdrop.

The outer officer stayed at the traffic point beyond the police vehicle. He was speaking to the replacement transport contact, repeating that the loading place itself was not clear. Nothing had arrived behind him to change that report.

The crew beside Rudra still had only their actual hands, a carry sheet and the ground on which to use them.

The driver looked at the ruined stretcher, then back at the sheet.

"Yeh chhod do," the worker told him. "Pehle inko."

He meant the damaged equipment. The driver let go of the thought of bringing it with them.

---

The man drove his free hand towards Rudra's good shoulder again.

Rudra turned the held wrist a little and let his own forearm take the line across, not straight back into the collar. The new density held. The strike still jarred his elbow and shook the cloth against the ankle. He gasped and answered when the worker called his name.

The other hand went past him into the bank.

Soil burst against his sleeve.

The bank had not attacked for him. A fist had struck it where the side of his neck had been.

Rudra kept his grip, breathing hard. The red-black remained close to his body, the gold distinct inside it, every light visible to the people here. There was nothing concealing him from the man whose arm he held. The collar fold was still wet against his fingers.

He could feel his hurt foot resting on its ordinary support.

He left it there.

---

The man's free fingers opened in the fresh hollow his fist had made.

He pulled that hand out, shook the soil from it and looked down beside Rudra. The empty stretcher lay close to the lower edge of the bank, one wheel crooked in its fork, the rest of the frame still joined.

The driver saw the look and moved towards it.

The man got there first.

His hand closed round the lower crossbar. He dragged the frame across the grit with his other hand still clamped against Rudra's forearm. Its damaged wheel scored a narrow line through the place where the crew had been about to put a foot.

The worker pulled his own foot back and kept his hands clear of the moving metal.

Rudra could not push with the ankle to change where his body lay. His good hand was still holding the wounded arm away from his throat. The other stayed against the collar fold.

He saw the wheels turn towards him as the man changed his grip on the crossbar.

The frame scraped once against the bank, then left it.

The empty stretcher rose between them, wheels first.
