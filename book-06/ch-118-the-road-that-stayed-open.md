# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEEN — THE ROAD THAT STAYED OPEN

---

> *"One version gave the road back to the boy.*
> *The medical account gave it to everyone who had kept a place beside him."*
> *— Ira's later register, after the approach was closed to sightseers*

---

Rudra put his guarded forearm beneath the man's two hands and drove them away from his chest.

He did not close his unreliable fingers round the weapon. The pressure came from the bend of his own arm, through the hands trying to turn the constable's wrist, up and towards the empty face of the bank.

The muzzle passed his shoulder.

The officer's free hand reached the weapon. He brought it against his own bent arm and turned with the pressure, keeping its mouth towards bare earth above the sheet.

There was no shot.

The man's wounded hand slid down the sleeve. He tried to bring it back. The effort pulled his mouth open, and Rudra felt the other grip tighten against the constable's wrist instead.

His own elbow felt as if it were being forced apart.

He kept pushing only until the gun no longer pointed into the place where he lay.

Then he shortened the movement.

---

The constable pulled his wrist free.

He stepped back across the furrow his heel had made, taking the weapon with him. The torn door caught against his boot again. He went round it this time, both hands on the firearm, his face pale beneath the dust.

The man turned after him.

That turn brought his upper body close to Rudra's raised forearm. Rudra pressed against the side of the shoulder before the reaching hand could find the weapon again.

The bank took the reaction through his back.

He cried out. The worker called his name, and he answered without letting the sound become another push. The pressure stayed in the arm which was touching the man, short and hard and entirely his own.

The man took a sideways step.

Then another.

His foot came down on the loose clod torn out by the frame. It broke beneath the heel. He tried to widen the stance; the wounded arm lifted for balance and dropped again.

Rudra turned the forearm only far enough to keep that shoulder going away from the sheet.

The man's knee struck gravel.

---

Rudra stopped pressing.

There was space between his arm and the man now. The red-black stayed around his body; it did not follow the falling shoulder through the air. He had made the space with contact. He could not hold somebody in it merely by wishing the distance to remain.

The armed officer used it.

He moved to where the bank and the folded stretcher were beyond the kneeling man, not Rudra. The weapon stayed in his hands. His colleague at the sheet could see the new line too.

"Haath saamne," the officer said.

The man tried to rise.

One foot planted. The other knee lifted a little. He reached with the fresh-wounded arm and failed to make it take the weight he wanted. The hand slipped on the blood at his own sleeve.

He sank back, breathing through his mouth.

The old-grazed fist closed.

Rudra watched it from the sheet. He had no strength to lend the constable and no right to promise that the next strike would stop on the new outline as the earlier ones had.

He kept his arm close.

The fist opened again.

The man put that palm on his thigh, fingers spread, where the officer could see it. His other hand stayed against the bleeding upper arm.

"Bas," he said.

It was the first word Rudra had heard him give the people he had been hitting.

---

The worker did not leave Rudra to find out what that word meant.

He checked the boy's breathing first. Rudra could still speak; he could not make a deep breath comfortable. He gave the back pain, the elbow and the weakness in the fingers again, not as old information the next person should already know.

The driver held the leg's support while the road constable stepped clear of the sheet.

The officer took the cuff case from his belt. He approached from the side his armed colleague could keep in view, asking for the other hand. The man moved it away from the wound slowly. Blood ran from the elbow to the bare gravel.

The constable did not call that compliance a healed injury.

He brought the wrists together in front, allowing room for the hurt upper arm, checked the restraint and stepped away from the man's reaching distance. His colleague kept the gun ready. Neither treated the small metal join as a promise that somebody who had lifted a vehicle could never break it.

For now the hands remained together.

The road constable called to the outward point that the assault had stopped, the weapon was secure and they needed medical help for two injured people, with separate lying transport for the bleeding man.

He gave what he could see.

He did not give the road a declaration that it would never be dangerous again.

The outward constable did not wave the returning crew into the bend on the strength of one sentence.

He repeated the report, asked where the armed officer stood and waited for the road constable to come far enough out to show the cleared strip. The man who had said he would stop was seated now, hands restrained in front, the firearm still covering his side of the bank from a distance.

The road officer returned to that watch.

The first medical crew brought the fresh bed only as far as the narrow shoulder would safely allow. Its driver set the brakes there. His colleague walked the remaining few paces to Rudra and crouched beside the worker already treating him.

They knew each other from the earlier transfer.

That did not spare the new account.

The vehicle's drop. The damaged wheel. Ground transfer. New elbow swelling, palm bleeding and weaker grip. The pain through the back when he tried a deeper breath. The earlier visual change which had cleared and not returned. The still-supported ankle and the knee which could not push him round.

Rudra added what the two workers had not felt for themselves.

His breathing was checked while he spoke. The first worker asked about the earlier faint after exertion as well as what had happened today.

Nobody put the old handover date over the new injuries.

---

Rudra let his good forearm lower onto the folded cloth the worker placed for it.

The dense line followed it down.

He could feel the effort which had kept it close, larger than the old one and still his. There was nobody touching the guarded arm now. He did not need to keep that effort going for the sheet to carry him.

He stopped feeding it.

Red-black loosened first at the chest, then along the shoulder. The outline over the legs thinned. The nearer density drew away from his skin and went out without leaving a second body standing where it had been.

The gold remained a separate, faint brightness.

His elbow still throbbed. The right ankle hurt where its support touched it. The mouth's torn place caught when he tried to wet his lips.

He told the worker the shape had ended.

The worker checked him again before moving him. Rudra answered, took the next breath without chasing a number and let somebody else hold the collar fold while his recovering wrist was settled clear of the lift.

"Chalna nahi hai na?" he asked.

"Tumhara chalna nahi," the driver said. "Hamaara."

Rudra let his head rest back.

---

The second clinical worker passed the current notes and stayed within the first worker's hearing while the lifting places were chosen. At Rudra's request, he took the phone from his bag's closed pocket and passed it to the first worker, who settled it in the soft fold beside Rudra's head.

Then he went to the seated man with his bag.

The armed constable warned him to keep space until the restrained hands were still. The man looked at the clean material and at his own arm. He let the worker come beside the bleeding without reaching for him.

The worker asked about feeling below the wound, looked at the blood loss and began the care which could be given on this ground. He told the officer why the man needed prompt receiving treatment and lying transport of his own.

His description contained no word about deserving it.

The officer repeated the request outwards. Another medical vehicle and additional custody help were requested through the ordinary contacts; acknowledgments came back, not arrivals.

The second driver joined the first crew at Rudra's sheet.

Three people lifted with the same supported alignment they had just checked. The new bed waited a few paces away on firm ground. They lowered Rudra onto it without asking him to bridge the last distance on his injured feet.

His good arm remained on its folded support. The recovering wrist did not take his weight.

The driver who belonged to the damaged ambulance helped until the transfer was settled. Then he stepped back to his colleague and the other injured man.

His keys stayed in his pocket.

---

The first worker secured Rudra on the new bed and checked the leg support after the straps were fastened.

The first driver released its brakes. They rolled only once the road constable had shown the clear line back to the outer point, and stopped when a wheel met loose gravel rather than jolting him through it.

Behind them, the other clinical worker kept pressure at the man's upper arm.

The gun stayed with its constable. He was holding it differently now, keeping the strained wrist as still as the task allowed. He told his colleague that the wrist hurt and his boot had taken the door's edge. The second worker heard him and included his own check among the care still needed before that officer could call the shift ordinary again.

Rudra saw neither the cuff inspection nor the new dressing being finished.

The bank passed out of his view as the bed went round the bend.

At the holding point, the first driver brought the ambulance round before the bed was loaded.

The outward constable kept the diversion closed and showed him the clear turning space. The clinical worker stayed with Rudra throughout it. When the rear doors faced the waiting bed and the exit lay ahead of the engine, the driver got out and came to the frame.

They checked the loading line together.

Rudra watched the door.

It was straight. Its check held it where it had been opened. The ordinary difference was almost enough to make him cry.

"Pehle lock dekhenge," the worker told him.

He had seen where Rudra was looking.

The driver guided the bed in; the catch engaged, and they checked that it had. The straps and the leg's position were checked once more after the change of height. Rudra was inside this vehicle, not beside it with a loading plan still waiting to happen.

The worker took the travelling seat and fastened his own belt.

The outward constable came to the door only after the patient was settled. He asked whether Rudra could give more of his account now.

"Pehle receiving," the worker said.

The constable looked at the supported arm and accepted it. He confirmed the destination through the ordinary medical contact, not by asking Rudra to name a place he had never reached.

The rear doors shut.

The driver took his seat. The officer opened the controlled exit, watched the ambulance leave and closed the approach to ordinary traffic again.

---

The bank remained behind with people still working on it.

The damaged vehicle's driver called its owner after his colleague had the bleeding controlled under the dressing. He gave the rear impact, the torn door and the unusable stretcher, and said he was not driving that ambulance away without inspection. He still had its keys.

The owner asked for the vehicle's position and the visible damage, not for a promise that somebody else's family would pay before a patient was allowed care.

The driver recorded what he could see without disturbing the folded frame or the door's last position. Recovery and inspection were being arranged. No recovery truck had arrived.

The road constable kept the approach to the restrained man in view while his armed colleague's wrist was checked. The clinical worker did that check once the dressing held and the second driver could keep the injured arm supported for those moments. The officer reported the pull and the boot impact. He was told to keep the wrist out of lifting and get the continuing pain assessed properly when relieved.

Additional custody help was still on its way, not standing behind him.

The man under watch asked for the blanket to be brought higher over his shoulder.

The worker adjusted it without loosening the check on the bleeding.

There was no name yet to put beside the person receiving that care. His injuries and the acts which had brought the police here did not need to wait for one.

---

In the moving ambulance, Rudra felt every change in the road through a different part of his body.

The worker checked him when a breath caught and asked whether it was the same place or a new one. He made him distinguish the back from the chest and waited through the shorter answers. Rudra's foot remained supported. The phone travelled in its soft fold, its screen dark.

He did not call the people who loved him from a moving vehicle to offer proof that he could still sound like himself.

For a little while he listened to the worker report the current findings to the receiving contact.

No one said he was unhurt.

The road beyond the rear window turned away from the place where the metal had fallen. A patched wall passed, then a bicycle being steadied at a shopfront. The rider waited for the ambulance instead of becoming part of its emergency.

Rudra let his eyes close until the next question.

When the worker spoke, he opened them and answered.

---

The receiving entrance did not look like an ending.

There were two people waiting with a place prepared for him, questions already following the call ahead. The driver opened the rear doors and helped bring the secured bed down. The nurse came to Rudra's eye level while the travelling worker began the handover.

This time the account included the whole journey from the bank.

The ankle jolt and cleared whiteness. The swelling elbow and the grip which had become unreliable. Back pain with breathing after repeated impacts. The earlier exertional faint in his history. Collar and palm bleeding, the support under each injured limb and what had changed during the ride.

Rudra gave the answers the clinician asked him directly.

The crew stayed until the receiving team had taken responsibility and the supported transfer was finished. Then their one further run was complete. They were not assigned another return trip at the door.

The driver took the vehicle out of the receiving lane.

Rudra remained with the people whose questions had just begun.

The clinician examined the elbow before asking Rudra to show what the hand could do.

Rudra said the grip had been failing while the dark outline was still there. He had not waited for it to end before the weakness began. The clinician checked feeling and circulation, the swelling and the places which hurt with movement, then left the arm supported rather than repeating a strength trial through pain.

The back and the pain on breathing received their own examination.

Rudra answered about the blows, the old injuries and the earlier faint. Pulse, pressure and breathing were checked again. Imaging was ordered for the injuries which needed it; the absence of a picture yet was not a clear picture of the bone.

The ankle support was adjusted after the foot had been examined. The knee stayed out of weight-bearing. The recovering wrist was not asked to help him change beds.

The nurse cleaned and covered the palm and the torn nail edge, then checked the collar wound and replaced the bloodied material. Rudra let go of the fold he had been holding for so long. The same hand was put on a small support where it could rest.

He had become used to being useful with the least injured part of himself.

Here, for a little while, even that part had a place to be put down.

"Aaj raat observation mein rahoge," the clinician said.

Rudra asked whether that meant the earlier faint had been explained. The answer was no. The new pain and the old history both belonged in the assessment; staying where they could keep checking him did not settle a cause by itself.

He asked who he should call if the breath changed before the clinician came back. The nurse showed him the reachable call bell and made sure he could use it without lifting the hurt arm. He tried it once with her there, then let his hand rest. Needing someone would not have to wait for a louder injury.

---

The phone came from the travelling bed's soft fold before the padding was removed.

The nurse asked where Rudra wanted it. He asked for the pillow's side, screen facing him, and for Sitara to be told he had arrived. Only that arrival and his being with the receiving team, for now.

She checked which Sitara he meant through the existing receiving contact.

When Sitara came, it was after her own hand review, with the bag carried in the unhurt hand. Her fingers remained covered. She had not been cleared for lifting because the next person who needed help had arrived.

She put the bag down where the nurse showed her.

"Aa gaye," she said.

Rudra tried a nod, stopped at the pull in the collar and answered instead.

"Haan."

For a moment neither improved the sentence.

Sitara looked at the newly supported arm, not at a photograph of the force which had moved it. He told her he was being examined and that the other man had stopped attacking when he left the bank. He could not give her the next condition of that wound from a bed elsewhere.

She asked whether the three friends could be told his confirmed arrival.

He agreed to the same narrow update. The examination was still going on. He did not want to invent a clean result merely because she had somebody waiting for it.

Ira had asked for the next confirmed arrival.

Sitara could give her one now.

---

The charger in her bag fitted his phone.

She checked the connection with the unhurt hand and left the lead where the nurse said it would not cross a transfer path. It was a small, ridiculous thing to be grateful for after the sight of a vehicle's underside: a dark screen showing an ordinary charging mark, nobody needing to hold it up to keep the connection alive.

"Phone bach gaya," Rudra said.

"Phone ko kal exercise nahi karni."

He nearly laughed. The pull in his back made him stop and tell the nurse why. Sitara waited until the answer had been given before picking up the bag.

She was going to sit where her own review required her to sit, not take the place of the worker at his side.

The message went to the three friends before her screen was put down again: Rudra had arrived, he was with the receiving team, and they were assessing the new injuries. There were no pictures. She passed no detail from a call she had not heard.

Veer acknowledged it without asking for a live account. Aadhya sent that she would wait for what Rudra chose to tell. Ira's reply was one short line saying the arrival had been entered, not the injuries guessed.

Rudra did not see all three answers. Sitara told him only that they had received the message before she left.

---

At a tea counter on the diverted road, the ambulance had already changed hands in the telling.

One voice gave its whole weight to a boy standing under it. Another added a broken road, then a light no one could quite agree on. A rider who had been turned back before the bend said he had seen none of that.

"Toh suna hoga," somebody told him.

He had seen an ambulance leave later. That was all he could give the story.

The tea seller set another glass on the wet counter. By the next glass, the boy was standing again.

Inside the receiving room, Rudra asked the nurse to move the support rather than try to lift his own leg.

She did. He thanked her and lay still for the rest she had asked him to take.

The rest lasted long enough for the light at the window to change.

Rudra was taken for the ordered images with staff and supports, not a walking test to see whether the journey had done him good. He came back to the same receiving place. The films would need the clinician's review; the nurse settled him for continued observation and the prescribed care while that was arranged.

He slept in pieces.

When he woke, the first thing he checked was whether the next breath hurt in a new place. He gave the answer when asked. The hand remained weak and covered, the foot supported. He did not try the force again to find out whether the dark shape would return on command.

The nurse brought his phone nearer only after the next checks.

He asked whether a short call would get in the way.

"Sawaal honge toh pehle yahaan jawab," she said.

He agreed. His head stayed on the support, the low earpiece beside it, neither hurt hand lifting the screen.

---

Ram answered from Varanasi.

Rudra asked whether both parents could listen for a few minutes. His father asked Rukmini, waited for her answer and told him they were together after their own evening care. They had no new account from the road beyond the call which had ended beside the stretcher.

Rudra started there.

The rear of the ambulance had been lifted after their conversation ended. It had swung and dropped. His bed had been damaged. The crew had moved him to a supported place against the earth; he had not stood or used the recovering wrist to catch himself.

He gave the changed injuries before the part which might sound like a victory.

"Kohni sooj gayi hai. Haath ki pakad kam ho rahi thi. Peeth mein saans ke saath dard hai. Dekh liya hai yahaan. Tasveeron ka poora jawab abhi nahi aaya. Aur gaadi ke jhatke par kuchh pal nazar safed hui thi. Foran worker ko bataaya."

Rukmini asked about the brief change in his sight.

He told her it had cleared and been checked, had not returned, and that he had remained able to answer. He did not make that the same as a promise it could never happen again.

Ram asked where he was now.

"Receiving mein. Pehli crew waapas aayi thi. Unke saath aaya."

He described the other man stopping, the police weapon staying with its officer, the separate medical help requested. After he left, he could give them no continuing view of that care.

He could give his own arrival.

---

"Roop badla tha," Rudra said.

There was a small change in the sound at the other end, a body settling nearer the phone without either parent speaking over him.

He explained what he had felt: his own effort kept close instead of the outward blow he had wanted to send, the dense line holding through a movement which had broken the old one apart. He gave the whole shape, the name he had chosen and the painful legs which had remained painful inside it.

"Unbound: Pataal," he said, more quietly than at the bank.

No one repeated it like a diagnosis.

Rukmini asked whether the shape was still there.

"Nahi. Band kiya. Phir uthaaya unhone."

"Aur dard?"

"Hai."

The answer did not disappoint her into asking for the form back.

Ram let a breath out slowly.

"Apni chot chhupaa ke mat samjhaana isko," he said.

"Chhupa nahi raha."

"Sun raha hoon."

Rudra looked at the supported fingers. There had been a time when the second sentence would have arrived as another instruction to do better. This time his father left it as listening, and the nurse's next question came through the room before either of them tried to use the quiet.

Rudra answered her first.

---

They did not build the next journey out of the few minutes left in this call.

His parents remained in Varanasi with their own care and the limits which had not disappeared because a son had come through another afternoon. His mother asked to be told when the investigations had been explained, not sent a brave message in their place.

Rudra agreed to that much.

He wanted to say something ordinary before the call ended. Ask whether they had eaten. Complain about the pillow. Tell his father the charger had survived more sensibly than he had.

The ordinary things mattered. They were not enough to keep the other question out merely because he wanted a few minutes in which his parents could stop being afraid.

He had heard the fear before he had known there was a history to attach it to.

Now he knew about the real fight in 2020, about Adhira defeated and a father returning to a house where the boy had been left to guess why the mornings had become harder.

That victory had not brought the fear home smaller.

Rudra kept his voice low enough that the torn place in his cheek would let him finish.

"Papa, aapne Adhira ko haraa diya tha."

"Haan."

"Toh uske baad aap aur kis se darr rahe the?"
