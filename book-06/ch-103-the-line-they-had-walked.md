# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND THREE — THE LINE THEY HAD WALKED

---

> *"The line had been inspected in both directions.*
> *That did not mean nobody else could reach it."*
> *— Ira's later register, first account of the attack in Pataal*

---

"Jaana chahta hoon," Rudra said. "Pehli baar adhoori nahi thi."

Dhruv waited for the rest.

"Tab har cheez mein Papa ke liye kuchh dhoondh raha tha. Baad mein samajh aaya ki jo dekha, wahi kaafi tha bataane ko. Ab raaste ko raasta dekhna hai. Kis jagah ghoomte hain. Kahaan ruk sakte hain. Sirf tasveer ke baahar wali cheez samajhke nahi."

He was not asking to find the missing house in a second visit. He wanted a little knowledge of the ground which did not have to become an answer about his family before it counted as knowledge.

The wish could be considered. It did not make the road clear, his body ready or his friends willing.

Dhruv said he would help ask the questions they had named. Rudra said that was the help he wanted now.

They ended the conversation in the day-ninety-two room, with the chair under him and the wrist still protected.

---

The practical request reached the other three as a request, not a date.

Rudra told them he wanted another short look along the same known stretch if the route, their own wishes and their care actually allowed it. He gave his reason without placing it inside their mouths. He would not need the three to want the same thing in order to have asked honestly.

Veer was interested, but wanted to know how much of the day the knee would have to manage. Aadhya wanted no experiment hidden inside the visit, no hands expected to produce green because the place had none where they had stood. Ira wanted the spoken line and the option of keeping the ordinary eye rested; an earlier view did not make it limitless now.

They wanted the current facts before answering.

That evening, food and care interrupted the planning in the ordinary way. Rudra slept, woke once for help with the leg and did not turn the waiting hours into a private walking trial.

---

On day ninety-three, the treating people heard the request and the old route account.

They asked about the exact help which would be needed at the uneven lip, the time in supported seats, the return before the next care and what had happened after the first outing. The unknown conditions beyond the inspected line could not be certified by an examination of an ankle.

Nobody pretended they could.

There would be current trials of the ordinary functions before any answer about another short outing. A body able to sit through a story had not thereby demonstrated the transfer, the road or the short supported effort at the turn.

Rudra found the questions less disappointing once they were about things he could actually report.

He said how the knee had pulled. He said what the closer chair had changed. He admitted that thinking about leaving made him want to describe the same effort more generously than he had described it the day before.

The therapist asked him to keep yesterday's facts.

He did.

---

His first trial was not longer walking.

It was the assisted transfer with the positions agreed before he rose, the ankle supported, the good hand's permitted aid ready and the braced wrist out of the work. The helper waited while Rudra found whether the knee wanted the small adjustment. It did.

They adjusted. He sat and gave the account.

Later, after rest, they checked how the supported seat held the limbs and how he would ask for the position to be changed. His breathing remained settled. The ankle still tired. The result was useful without becoming a declaration that the whole outing had already been earned.

Veer's own examination kept the shoulder out of lifting work. His short aided movement was tried with a seat before he needed it; he reported the knee's response rather than borrowing confidence from the fact that Rudra had managed a transfer.

The girls had their own reviews, not signatures added to the boys' result.

---

Dhruv and Sitara went to the passage while those assessments were taking place, with the care arrangements covered and a receiving contact expecting their return.

They examined the Earth approach, the bends, the uneven lip and the inspected Pataal stretch. They went through, looked from the return direction and came back by the same line. They did not enter the broken fronts or treat an empty visible road as an account of everything beyond it.

Their findings reached the four after rest.

The line was usable under the same assistance limits. Loose grit still needed watching. A resting place was available at the broad turn, with the known street beyond. No new device was needed, and nobody's body would be asked to keep the passage open.

It was a report from that inspection.

It was not a promise that a world would remain still until the next person chose to enter it.

The next day was shorter than Rudra wanted.

He had slept badly in the latter part of the night. When he said so at the morning review, the plan changed with the fact instead of requiring the fact to be improved. Day ninety-four held care, the necessary supported movement and more rest. There was no extra effort to pay back an outing which had not yet happened.

On day ninety-five, after a better night and another examination, the transfer was tried again. The knee needed less adjustment. The ankle still tired, and the wrist remained out of the work. Rudra sat in the arranged vehicle seat for a short ordinary road trial, with the supports in place and help at either end.

The ride was not to the passage.

It went far enough to find the ordinary difficulty: a position which had seemed comfortable while stationary began pulling after the vehicle had moved for a while. They stopped, changed it within the plan and returned. The receiving people heard the actual account. Rest followed the trial, not another trial hidden inside the satisfaction of having managed one.

Veer's own supported seat and short movement were checked separately. The shoulder was still not for lifting; the knee still needed the pause. Aadhya had enough comfortable ordinary activity for the limited visit being considered, with the hands protected and no gripping task. Ira's ordinary right eye could be used briefly, then rested; the left stayed covered. Spoken guidance remained part of the arrangement.

None of the four had become a combat plan.

---

The practical help had to be asked for again too.

The attendant who had helped at the first visit was available for the limited interval proposed, and agreed to the actual work, including the return. He was not someone who had been kept waiting since the previous journey. Another fit helper was arranged on the Earth side, where there would be a spare folding chair and assistance for the approach and road transfers. Dhruv's hand was not the answer to every piece of lifting left unnamed.

Two suitable vehicles and their drivers were confirmed for the run, with the pauses and return understood. Vedant would remain Earth-side with the receiving contact, the transport and the extra help. His forearm had its own review and its own limits. He would not be both the coordinator outside and an unseen rescuer already waiting inside Pataal.

The cost and the hours were settled from what had actually been kept aside for travel. If the plan had needed more help than could be arranged, it would have needed changing.

Water, the existing care items, the supports and a place to rest before the road return were included. Nobody put the useful bag in Aadhya's hands merely because she was not using an aid to walk.

---

Dhruv and Sitara checked the route again before the final clinical decisions.

They returned with the report of the same inspected line, the lip and the broad turning place. The farther broken fronts remained outside it. They had seen no one on that stretch during their inspection. They did not know who might be somewhere beyond it.

Rudra listened to that difference.

The first visit had not made absence a permanent inhabitant of the place. A road could be clear when looked at and still belong to a world larger than the look.

He told the others the same limited purpose he had given Dhruv. They gave their own answers, separately, after hearing the current help and route arrangements.

Veer wanted the short look, not a job carrying his friends. Aadhya wanted to go for what could actually be seen along the chosen line, not to prove the hands useful by producing anything. Ira wanted the option to receive most of it through the single spoken guidance she had chosen. Rudra wanted the road as ground, with its turns and places to stop.

All four could still stop at the Earth approach after the final check.

---

Day ninety-six came after another actual night, breakfast, care and individual reviews.

The permissions concerned this short supported outing under the conditions described. They did not concern an expedition into unknown buildings, renewed power training or a fight. Any changed symptoms at the approach would change the decision.

Rudra informed his parents before leaving, in a brief contact which fitted their own care. They heard the limited plan, the help, the intended return and the remaining restrictions. They were not asked to come, identify a ruin or supply a reason which would make the journey worth taking.

Ram wanted an accurate message once they were back on Earth. Rukmini said it could be short; they could talk after the receiving checks and rest if both sides wanted to.

Rudra agreed.

There was no new photograph to send yet and no promise to bring one.

---

They travelled through a morning which had other uses for the road.

A bus stopped where the driver of their vehicle wished it had not. A man with stacked steel containers waited for room to cross. Veer complained mildly about a cushion, got it adjusted and stopped complaining when the change helped.

At the planned pause, Rudra reported the ankle's position before it became the only thing he could think about. The help came, the support was checked, and the vehicles went on when the four were ready for the next part, not when the word departure had acquired too much pride to be interrupted.

At the Earth approach, arriving did not become an instruction to continue.

They rested, drank and gave their actual answers about the road. Ira wanted the shade and a little quiet before deciding. Veer needed the knee's position changed. Rudra had the ankle support checked. Aadhya said the hands were settled and would remain out of carrying work.

The extra helper stayed with Vedant, the spare chair and the transport. The attendant who would accompany Rudra checked the folding chair and the help needed at the lip. Nobody quietly reassigned that task to Veer while the bags were being put down.

Dhruv and Sitara went through the passage again.

The four waited until both returned, with the report of the line they had actually walked and the way back they had used. The bends and turning space were clear. The farther territory remained unexamined. Their visible stretch had been empty during the check.

Each of the four was asked again about this present, limited visit.

Each said yes.

---

They crossed by the old natural passage.

At the uneven lip, the movement was the one already considered: help and limited protected loading, good hand where it was allowed, injured wrist clear. The attendant supported Rudra while Dhruv moved the empty chair within the hand's limits. Rudra sat again before anybody moved on. His position was checked after the transfer, not assumed correct because he had reached the seat.

Veer kept the aid and his own effort. Sitara gave Ira the single spoken line she had chosen. Aadhya protected the hands while finding the ordinary places for her feet.

No body held a gate open. No light needed to remain joined around Rudra's chest to get the others home.

They came into the broad turning place on the Pataal side with time to stop there.

The light on the broken stone was not the light from the vehicle windows. Rudra felt the old difference without asking it to tell him who he was.

---

This time he looked first towards the return.

The clear line led back to the rock bend and, beyond it, the uneven lip. To face the visible street from the turning space, the chair needed to be moved at the wider patch, not turned sharply where the edge narrowed. The attendant showed him the actual movement by making it slowly, with Rudra seated and the supports kept clear.

He could see why the broad patch mattered.

The attendant asked him to say where the foot support lay in relation to the wall. Rudra looked and corrected his first answer: the wheel cleared it before the support did. They moved back a little before turning. Knowing which part arrived first was different from simply knowing the chair would fit.

Beyond it, the known street ran beside a low broken wall and bent towards the farther outlook they had reached before. Openings in the ruined fronts lay off that line. They had not been inspected inside, and nobody took a doorway's width as permission to pass through it.

A gap in the wall on the right gave a narrow view of more broken ground beyond the street. The ground could be seen. Its connections could not be supplied by a glance.

Rudra asked where they would turn back if he wanted to stop before the outlook.

Here, Dhruv said, and showed the place without requiring him to reach a farther point first.

---

They moved only a short way along the inspected street.

Veer chose the rest available at a broad level stone once its surface and approach had been checked. His aid stayed within reach. Aadhya stood where she did not need to steady herself with a protected palm against the broken wall. Ira used the ordinary eye briefly for the nearby line, then closed it and asked Sitara to describe the bend ahead.

There was no need for her to look until it hurt in order to have visited honestly.

Rudra watched the attendant turn the chair a little so he could see the return line again. The same wall concealed a different part of the view from this side. He had passed it before while watching somewhere else.

"Yeh dekhna tha," he said.

Dhruv nodded. A thing could be worth coming to see without yielding a secret.

They had brought water and time to pause. They used both.

---

The first unfamiliar sound was a short scrape beyond the low wall.

Rudra turned his head. So did Dhruv.

A man stood farther along the street, beyond the part the group had chosen to use. Dust lay on his lower clothes. One hand held a short shaft with a dull iron head. He did not have the look of an apparition: he shifted his feet on loose stone, and his next breath was visible in the movement of the cloth across his chest.

Dhruv asked him to stay where he was.

The man looked at the seven people on the inspected line, at the chair and at the bend leading back.

Then he raised the iron-headed shaft.

The shaft struck the chair's outer rim.

Iron rang against metal. The chair jolted. Rudra's good hand closed on the permitted support while the injured wrist stayed against its brace, useless to the violent instinct which wanted both hands available at once.

The iron head skipped past the wheel and hit stone.

"Waapas," Dhruv said.

He did not wait for the man to explain the throw.

The attendant held the chair steady. Sitara turned Ira towards the return line with a hand she could follow and one clear instruction. Aadhya moved with them, keeping the protected fingers away from the wall.

Veer had already begun rising from the resting stone.

The knee caught. He stopped the rise before it became a fall, caught the aid in the good hand and looked for the place where his next movement would actually help.

The stranger bent, seized a loose piece of stone and threw it at the people turning.

---

Veer opened the good hand.

A small pale curve took the stone off its straight line. Not a wall across the street, not the full strength he had once reached in another emergency. A narrow live defence, for the space it could actually cover.

The stone broke against its edge. A fragment passed the weak part and struck the low wall behind Aadhya. She ducked from the chips without putting the hands out.

The curve snapped away.

Pain pulled through Veer's shoulder and into the ribs before he could pretend the arm had acted separately from them. He kept the aid instead of reaching after the defence to make it larger.

"Chal sakte ho?" Sitara asked.

"Madad ke saath."

That was the answer she used. He did not get assigned the chair because he had managed one brief piece of protection.

---

Rudra saw Dhruv move the fallen shaft clear with his foot.

He stooped for it with the sound hand, brought it up and stood between the advancing man and the chair. The tender hand remained close to his body. The reach on one side of the improvised guard was shorter. Rudra could see it. So could an opponent willing to look.

The man drew a shorter blade and came towards that side.

Dhruv yielded a step along the clear stone rather than meet the blade with the hand which could not grip properly. He struck the weapon's wrist with the shaft. The blade did not leave it, but the next cut went wide enough for the chair to begin turning.

The attendant checked the wheel with one quick movement. The rim had a fresh nick; it still rolled. He kept his hands where they were useful and brought Rudra around at the broad part instead of wrenching the chair across the narrowing edge.

Another stone struck nearby.

A sharp chip cut the attendant high on the arm. He flinched, looked at the torn sleeve and said he could still hold the chair. Blood marked the cloth. It was not a harmless noise to be forgotten once the wheel moved.

"Sabko leke lautna hai," Dhruv said. "Inhe haraana nahi."

---

Aadhya looked for the line Sitara had described before the attack.

The broken wall. The wider patch behind them. The rock bend beyond it, where the lip lay out of sight. There was a place for the chair to move, if the people with it could keep that place clear.

She named the loose shaft end under the attendant's next step. He saw it and changed the foot's position without losing his hold.

Her hands wanted something to do. There was no living root here which she had examined and could safely ask to take a load. Wanting green did not put it under the stone.

She used the view she had.

Ira kept the left cover. The uncovered eye opened briefly to the nearby feet and closed against the flying grit. Sitara gave her the next ordinary step. She took it, then stopped when the voice said stop.

Nobody asked a wounded eye to become a view of the whole street.

---

Rudra felt the colours stir beneath the loosened collar.

The old wish arrived with them: stand, reach, make the distance answer to the force inside him. He could not stand without the knee and ankle. He could not throw a hand forward without the wrist attached to it.

The body did not disappear because someone had given it a reason to be frightened.

He told the attendant where the ankle support had shifted in the jolt. There was no time for the careful comfort of the room, but the man could keep the leg clear of the wheel while moving them back. Rudra accepted that smaller help.

Ahead, Dhruv knocked another cut aside. The sound was ugly and close.

Behind the low wall on their right, stone grated under a different foot.

Rudra turned towards it.

He saw the edge of a second man's clothes moving through the gap they had looked past on the way in. This man was not facing the first attacker.

He was looking towards the broad turning place.

"Peechhe," Rudra said. "Mod pe doosra aadmi."

The attendant looked before pushing the chair another length. That look kept them from arriving at the turning place as though it were still empty.

The second man carried a longer staff with an iron ferrule. He stepped out of the gap, crossed the visible strip of broken ground and took the line towards the rock bend. No wall opened for him. No darkness carried him from somewhere Rudra could not see. He used a piece of ground the visitors had not walked.

The first attacker called something sharply.

"Peechhe roko!"

The second man planted his staff across the approach to the bend.

There was an answer in the movement which needed no name for the men. The first throw had not merely tried to hurt whoever stood nearest. While the group turned, somebody else had gone for the way they would use to leave.

---

Sitara stopped before the staff reached Veer's legs.

Ira stopped with her. Veer set the aid where it could take the weight the knee would not confidently take alone. Sitara had Ira following one forearm; her other hand steadied Veer for the moment he needed to settle.

Aadhya kept her body clear of both, close enough to see the staff and say where it lay. She did not put the hands between iron and a friend's knee.

The man looked from the aid to the covered eye, then at the chair coming towards him more slowly than the people with it wanted.

"Yahin," he said.

He struck the ferrule against stone.

It was not a warning which made an injury elsewhere. It made a sound. The danger was the arm holding the staff, the distance it could reach and the feet already standing where the return line narrowed towards the bend.

Aadhya could see those things.

"Aage nahi," she told the attendant. "Daaye haath mein lamba hai."

He brought the chair short of the sweep.

---

Rudra wanted the sound of the other fight to stop.

Behind him, blade and stolen shaft struck together again. Dhruv had not been left unharmed by the work of protecting the group; one blow forced the tender hand against the shaft before he could draw it back. Rudra heard the breath leave him. The grip on that side did not return.

He changed the angle of his body instead, keeping the sound hand and the length of the shaft between the blade and the boy.

"Ghoomo mat abhi," he called. "Jitni jagah hai, wahi rakho."

There was no request to win both fights with a body which could barely use the aid without assistance. Rudra heard a task he could actually do: tell the attendant not to spend the last clear space trying a turn which would expose the chair's side.

He said it.

The attendant checked the wheels and held their position. The blood on his upper sleeve had spread a little. His hands were still steady enough for the work, but the wound would need care, not a story about how ordinary helpers never noticed pain.

---

Ira asked Sitara where the passage lay now.

"Saamne mod. Uske pehle aadmi. Hum usi taraf dekh rahe hain."

She gave the distance in ordinary near places: the edge of the broad patch, the low break, the start of the bend. She did not supply an unseen path behind the ruins because a patient had asked for a way out.

Ira opened the ordinary eye long enough to find the nearest known edge, then rested it again. The covered left remained covered. There was no live defence still standing for her to return even if the eye had been fit to attempt it. Veer's brief curve had gone.

She told Sitara she understood where they were.

That was useful too. The next instruction would not have to cross the whole distance from confusion before a foot could move.

---

Rudra could see the rock bend beyond the second man's shoulder.

They had come through it minutes before. The lip was still there, the same ordinary obstacle which needed help, not a locked mouth asking his blood to be fed. The vehicles were on the other side. Vedant and the extra helper would still be expecting a short visit to end within its time.

They had no account of this fight yet.

Nobody could call to them by wishing the distance shorter.

Rudra's good hand tightened, then eased before it could pull the injured side into work. The red-black at the collar sharpened. Gold lay inside it, raw and of no use as a wall around the people with him.

The man with the staff glanced at the colour and shifted his grip.

Rudra could not tell what the man recognized. He could tell where the next movement would go.

The ferrule lifted. The attendant set his feet for the chair's weight. Sitara brought Ira one careful step back; Veer held the aid instead of throwing himself forward to make his body another thing which would have to be carried.

The staff came down towards the front wheel.
