# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND TWELVE — THE WEIGHT OF THE NEXT STONE

---

The earth bank took the red-black below its middle.

A dull crack came through Rudra's soles. Dust lifted along a short groove, then fell. The force did not emerge from the other side or turn into something waiting to be used again. Where it had struck, a little tongue of loosened soil slid down over the sand.

"Khatam," Sitara said. Her open hand was already raised.

Rudra kept his good hand closed until he had made sure there was nothing still gathering there. Then he let the fingers loosen without pointing them down the strip again.

Vedant came by the clear way behind him.

"Baith sakte ho?"

"Haan. Saath rahiye."

They went to the seat slowly. The knee held through the turn. Nobody stepped across the disturbed ground to measure a result before the person who had made it was sitting safely.

---

His good hand had begun to shake.

Rudra put it where they could see. A red bead had appeared at one nail edge. He gave the answers about breath, chest and dizziness before looking back towards the bank; none of those earlier symptoms had returned. The ankle felt unchanged. The knee was tired rather than newly painful.

The hand was enough reason to finish.

Sitara helped clean and cover the small bleeding place. Vedant called the receiving contact, gave what they had seen and heard and followed the advice for the return. There would be a proper examination there. No further effort would be made to see whether stopping sooner might produce a prettier groove.

Rudra looked at the closed fingers.

"Maine yahaan roka tha."

"Haan," Vedant said. "Jo nikal chuka tha, usko yahaan rukte nahi dekha."

The distinction was small enough to say and too large to ignore.

It did not mean they now knew everything his force could never do. It meant this release had crossed his mark after the gathering at the palm ended. The bank had supplied the ending his hand had not supplied.

"Achha hua mitti thi," Rudra said.

"Isliye rakhi thi."

---

Sitara inspected the damaged place after Rudra was settled and no power remained active.

She went round by the flank, checked the ground beyond and returned. The groove ended inside the bank. Nothing had reached the boundary behind it. No person or vehicle had entered the empty ground during the trial.

She took a photograph for the occupier, with permission to keep an account of this actual damage rather than making the closed morning disappear as an uneventful favour. The clay and the far sand mark were in another picture. Neither image could show what the effort had felt like inside Rudra's chest.

He asked for that limitation to stay with them.

The occupier received the damage report. He would have the loosened patch made safe before the yard returned to ordinary work; the actual cost would follow that work, not a guessed amount paid to make the conversation end.

The unused clay stayed broken.

---

The car came back along the same open road.

Rudra rode belted, the supported limbs given room, with the covered finger resting on his lap. The driver did not become a stretcher attendant at the receiving entrance. Staff met the arranged handover and helped with the short transfer as needed.

The examination checked the hand, joints and the earlier warning symptoms. The small nail-edge injury was dressed. The tremor eased with rest, but the team kept him for the observation they considered necessary after this new effort. There was no second faint and no chest pain. Those were reassuring findings, not an explanation for the event at the road bend weeks before.

The next day's added exercise was cancelled.

He could keep the comfortable ordinary movements already allowed, with help where needed. He would not try force again until the new injury and the exertion had been reviewed.

---

Vedant checked the actual journey and waiting time with the driver.

The agreed payment went through. The driver confirmed receiving it, collected his belongings and left for his own day. Nothing had been added to the booking under the name of being a good man.

Later, after food and another check, Rudra sent Ira a short answer.

The morning had not been dull. The bank had stopped the release. His hand had shaken, a nail edge had bled and he was resting. There would be no further trial today.

He did not send her the photographs without asking whether she wanted them.

She replied that she wanted to know he had actually come back.

"Aa gaya," he wrote.

For once, he left the sentence that size.

Day one hundred and twenty-three contained no lesson hidden inside the cancelled exercise.

The finger was checked. Rudra ate, slept and used the small ordinary movements which had remained comfortable. He told Veer about the groove in the bank when his friend asked, including the part where the hand stopped and the release did not.

"Target gaya?" Veer asked.

"Neeche se toota. Phir ghoom gaya."

"Toh usko bhi baad mein samajh aaya."

Rudra laughed, carefully. Veer let him finish the account without supplying either admiration or a verdict about what sort of person a shaking hand made him.

They discussed a badly made film instead. It was a relief to dislike something which neither of them was expected to improve.

---

The nail edge closed through its own small course of care.

After the rest and review, the therapist restored only the ordinary work which had been reduced. On the hundred-and-twenty-eighth day, Rudra made the uneven strip and the return at the shorter distance. He paused at the seat, described the knee before rising again and got back without the different pull which had stopped him before.

The afternoon check mattered as much as the walk. There was no new heat or swelling waiting after the apparent success.

They repeated the ordinary route on a separate day before discussing more.

The wrist could manage a little more light gripping now. It still was not to catch his body. When the aid was no longer required on the short level stretch, the therapist watched that change too; it did not disappear from the plan merely because Rudra wanted his hand free.

He did not take the freedom straight to a fist.

---

The occupier sent the completed earthwork bill.

The loosened patch had been cut back and compacted. Sitara saw it before the cost was settled; the worker's actual charge came out of the same accounted money, and the receipt stayed with the yard record. A repaired bank did not certify the next morning's emptiness.

There was enough for one further limited practice run now, after the other outstanding costs had been kept aside. They chose it together rather than booking a sequence whose later journeys would have to be paid by someone's embarrassment.

The new proposal was not to launch anything at the clay.

Rudra had already known a whole-body outline once, under an immediate threat to his mother's life. Remembering that it had happened was different from making it arrive on demand. He wanted to see whether a beginning could be recognised without driving it towards the force that had frightened everybody.

Nobody asked him to make the yard feel like that old danger.

---

On day one hundred and thirty-two, the current examination and ordinary exertion review allowed consideration of the small attempt.

The treating team heard about the earlier hand tremor and the old collar wound. There was still no promise about what a form might do. The same stop arrangements applied, with no waiting to see whether a new bleed would become impressive.

The yard's permission was renewed. Sitara and Vedant walked the bank, boundary and ground beyond again. The return route, car, driver, phone signal and receiving contact were checked for this morning, not borrowed from ten days before. The new limited booking had its own agreed work time and pay.

Rudra rested after the journey.

The repaired bank stood behind the empty strip. No target had been put there to tempt a stronger answer. The two observers took their protected places only after the final walk was finished and both were back.

He began with empty hands.

---

Nothing happened quickly enough to call obedience.

Warmth came and went at the palm. His attention followed it too greedily, and he lowered the hand before the effort turned into a demand. After rest, with the body still comfortable, he tried once more within the morning's proposal.

He did not hold his breath.

"Unbound: Jaagrut," he said, quietly, naming what he was trying to recognise rather than ordering the name to do the work.

The red-black thickened along his arms. Gold appeared between its darker reaches, separate points and lines which would not become the same colour because he wanted simplicity. The outline spread over the shoulders and down to the feet.

For a brief interval, he was inside the whole shape again.

The ankle had not healed into another ankle. He could still feel the recovering knee beneath it. He stayed in the ordinary stance instead of testing how much the outline might let him ignore.

Then the old place above the collar stung.

"Ruk raha hoon," he said.

He let the effort go.

The density thinned from the arms and broke away from the shoulders. No stroke left the hand. Sitara signalled the end as a thin red line appeared above the shirt. The form was gone before anybody could ask it for one more second.

---

He sat with ordinary help nearby, not with his body flung into a spectator's arms.

The shallow bleeding place received clean pressure and a covering. Vedant gave the current findings to the receiving team. Rudra stayed alert, breathing comfortably, with no new joint pain; that did not turn the bleed into a harmless price he could keep paying.

They returned for the examination and observation. The wound was small, required care and ended further practice. The newly booked driver was paid for the completed work, confirmed it and left.

Later, Rudra described the result without improving it.

"Roop aaya tha. Phir maine chhoda."

He looked at the fresh dressing before adding the qualification.

"Dobara waise hoga, yeh abhi nahi pata."

The next days belonged to the dressing and the ordinary work which had not caused the wound to reopen.

Rudra did not practise the outline in his room to find out whether the yard had been necessary. The collar was reviewed, the shallow opening closed and the care instructions changed with what the skin actually did. Comfortable walking returned to its previous place in the plan. Force did not follow it there by implication.

On the hundred-and-thirty-sixth day, he asked Dhruv a different question.

"Aap log kitni taaqat ke saamne isko taiyaar maanoge?"

Dhruv sat without putting the slowly recovering hand under his weight.

"Ek baar aaya hai. Ek baar tumne chhoda. Abhi usse muqaable ka vaada nahi nikaalunga."

"Jo Pataal mein hukm dete hain—unse?"

"Pad aur Kul ko alag rakho pehle."

He gave Rudra two words without a procession of new faces behind them.

Devta. Maha Devta.

The second stood above the first in the hierarchy they were discussing. Neither word was another name for a Kul. The power attached to those ranks was a reason not to turn one successful outline into a comparison Rudra's body had never survived.

"Toh jiske saamne kamzor lagein, usko Maha Devta bol dein?"

"Nahi. Apni haar se kisi ka naam ya ohda nahi milta. Jo karte dekho, woh bataa sakte ho. Kaun hai, uske liye aur jaankaari chaahiye."

Rudra looked at the ordinary hand on his knee.

The words made the possible scale larger. They did not give him a list of people he was now required to hate.

---

Veer had a good movement day and used part of it to complain about rehabilitation being boring.

The shoulder could do more permitted work now. He had needed the therapist's correction on a loaded reach, and his knee still wanted the planned rest between longer intervals. He was not secretly preparing to stand behind the next release so Rudra could feel trusted.

Aadhya asked Sitara for help placing the book, then managed the page with the movement her current hand plan allowed. She could do it slowly. The right thumb and index still gave an unreliable account of pressure; doing a little more did not let her stop looking at what they touched.

Ira's ordinary right-eye intervals had lengthened after review. She chose a shorter one that afternoon because it had begun to ache. The left covering remained where it belonged. There was no mirror trial disguised as checking whether the eye was better.

Their days contained things Rudra did not attend.

He was beginning to understand the comfort in that. They could remain his friends without making every improvement useful to his next attempt.

On day one hundred and thirty-nine, his parents chose a call after their own care.

Rudra asked whether they wanted the small practice account now. They did. He told them about the bank first, then the later outline, the collar bleed, the actual examinations and the fact that he had not repeated it.

Ram was quiet long enough that Rudra could hear a spoon touch a plate at the other end.

"Chot band hui?"

"Review mein band thi. Phir bhi force waali practice abhi band rakhi hai."

"Theek."

There was room inside the word for his father's fear, but Rudra did not have to fill that room by offering a demonstration over video. His mother asked whether he had eaten. This time he could describe the food without using it to change the subject before she heard the injury.

They finished before her shoulder grew uncomfortable.

After the call, Rukmini rested her good hand against her own wrist. The ordinary pulse was quick. She asked for help changing the pillow, waited until the supported arm was settled and took the medicine which belonged to her evening.

No sensation crossed the distance to tell her what her son had not said.

He had told her himself.

The statement constable's next request arrived on the hundred-and-fortieth day.

Some positions on the Earth approach still needed clarifying: the chair after the crossing, the damaged car's place, the move towards the road bend. Photographs could be used. If Rudra wished and his current ordinary function permitted it, a short visit to the Earth positions could be arranged instead.

"Earth waali jagah tak," he said.
