# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND FIVE — THE DOOR STILL OPEN

---

> *"The first shield on Earth belonged to a man paid to drive.*
> *It was a door he had opened for someone else."*
> *— Ira's later register, the account of the approach*

---

The driver dropped behind the door.

"Neeche!" Dhruv shouted, a fraction after the man had begun to move.

The spear struck the upper rim. Glass burst inward. The point tore across the seat beyond, leaving the shaft caught through the broken window, its butt shaking outside above Veer's empty foot space.

The driver put a hand to his forehead. It came away red.

He was alive. He was crouched beside the rear wheel, and the door he had been holding for Veer could no longer close.

"Idhar mat laana!" he called.

The other driver stopped pushing the spare chair.

Veer kept his good hand inside the armrest. A shard landed on the ground where his foot would have been during the transfer.

---

The vehicles stood along the rough approach, noses towards the road, with a short stretch of bare ground between them. The nearer one had taken the spear. Rudra's chair was beside the vehicle farther out; beyond its bonnet lay the bend towards the accessible road. The girls' seats were in the shade to its other side.

The extra helper pulled Rudra's chair back from the gap.

"Pair baahar hai," Rudra said.

The man looked down before changing direction. The supported leg cleared the rear wheel; the chair came around to the side away from the opening. Aadhya saw them disappear behind the body of the vehicle and followed Sitara's voice towards the same cover with Ira.

No one ran across the gap to pull the spear out.

The man who had thrown it was running towards it himself.

---

Dhruv caught the back of the folding seat left at the edge of the shade.

He dragged it into the man's line with the sound hand. Its legs scraped, caught a stone and held long enough for the attacker to kick it aside instead of reaching the open door.

Dhruv gave ground to the vehicle's rear corner.

The torn sleeve pulled at the cut beneath it. His other hand stayed close against the body, refusing to become useful because he wanted it urgently.

"Gaadi ke peechhe," he told the driver on the ground.

The driver shifted along the wheel, low, keeping the vehicle between himself and the attacker. He could see through the blood only when he wiped it away. Each time it returned.

The spear man reached for the shaft through the window.

Dhruv struck his reaching wrist with the folded seat's edge.

The man swore and jerked away. His free hand caught the seat. For a moment they both held it, one sound hand against two.

Dhruv let go before the pull could take him down.

---

The attacker stumbled backwards with the unexpected weight.

It was a little stumble. It gave the unhurt driver time to get Veer past the bonnet of the struck vehicle and into the space behind the farther one.

The chair's small front wheel hit a rut. Veer's knee jolted. He made a sound he could not disguise as an instruction.

"Roko. Bas."

The driver stopped, changed the angle and went around the rut. Veer watched the man's hands holding the chair and hated the useless certainty that he should have been holding something for him instead.

From the other side of the vehicle came Ira's voice.

"Veer?"

"Aa gaya."

He could give her that much without pretending he had arrived untouched.

The driver brought him beside Rudra, leaving a little space between the supported legs. Sitara settled Ira where she could rest the ordinary eye against the light. Aadhya stayed with her, facing the gap.

"Aankh band rakho," she said. "Main dekh rahi hoon."

---

Vedant was on the phone again.

He gave the changed danger in the ordinary voice he used for water deliveries and late vehicles: armed man now on the Earth approach, spear thrown, driver bleeding, two patients unable to walk out unaided. He repeated the accessible road turn and asked that responders be warned before driving straight into the open space.

The engine still running nearby made him repeat the last sentence.

He did not lower the protected forearm to help push a chair. There were hands already doing that. There was no one else making this call.

The first attendant had the cloth against his upper arm and a view around the other side of the bonnet. He told Vedant when Dhruv moved back another step.

Rudra tried to turn enough to see.

The pressure under his breastbone tightened. He stopped with the breath unfinished.

"Mat ghoomo," the helper said. "Woh yahin hain."

It was about Dhruv. It was also about the chair's handles, still held by a man who had not let him go.

The folding seat hit the side of the struck vehicle.

The spear man had thrown it at Dhruv. It glanced off the bodywork as he moved towards the gap, leaving a white scrape in the paint and a ringing in the enclosed strip of ground.

The attacker pulled at the shaft again. The point had caught through the upholstery. He changed his grip, put a foot against the sill and tugged.

Dhruv could have gone in while both hands were occupied.

The wounded driver was still beside the wheel.

"Ab," Dhruv said to him. "Meri taraf."

The man came around the rear corner, bent low, with his hand over the bleeding forehead. Dhruv stayed between his body and the open door until he had crossed the gap. His own shoulder was beginning to stiffen where it had hit rock.

He could not cover that distance a second time as quickly.

---

The unhurt driver left Veer's chair braked beside Rudra's.

He brought the small fire extinguisher from beside the front seat of his vehicle and stood at the rear corner with it. He looked at the pin, then at Dhruv. There was no confidence in his face that the cylinder had been manufactured for this use.

The first attendant made room for the wounded driver in the narrow shade. He gave him a clean fold from the first-aid supplies rather than the bloodied cloth held against his own arm.

"Aankh mein kuchh?"

"Lag nahi raha. Dikh dhundhla raha hai. Khoon aa raha hai."

The answer stayed an answer, not a clearance to drive. The man pressed the fold to the forehead and sat where the attendant pointed. His keys lay across his knee.

He kept looking at the damaged vehicle.

"Gaadi…"

"Baad mein," the other driver said.

"Maalik—"

"Pehle tum."

It was the first thing either had said to the other which did not concern the job.

---

Aadhya saw the blade before the face behind it.

Another man was coming out from the rock. He had to step around the shaft Dhruv had left inside; for a moment his leg was visible at the opening before the rest of him followed. Then he stood on Earth, the blade down beside the thigh.

"Doosra," she said. "Chhuri waala."

Ira did not open the ordinary eye to make the count her own. She held the number Aadhya had given her and listened to where the voice went next.

The blade man looked at the spear caught in the window. He said something sharp to its owner. Aadhya could not hear all of it over the engine.

She heard the answer.

"Phans gaya."

The man with the blade moved to the struck vehicle's rear tyre.

He crouched. His shoulder worked. A hiss began under the engine's noise and went on after he stood.

---

Rudra heard it from behind the other vehicle.

The sound went through him more cleanly than the bursts of light had. Somebody had come across a ruined street, followed them through the passage and put a knife into a tyre while people were bleeding beside it.

He saw only the top of the man's head over the bonnet. He saw him turn away from that small completed task towards the vehicle whose engine was still running.

The gold at Rudra's collar was bare.

For these few moments the man was looking elsewhere.

Rudra could not know what he would do next or whom he had come to kill. He could see that preventing departure mattered enough to spend time on it while their bodies were within reach.

"Tyre kaata," he told Vedant.

The words took two breaths.

Vedant passed them on.

---

The voice on the phone asked about another exit to the road.

Vedant looked at the approach instead of supplying one because a question had made it desirable. The bend beyond the farther vehicle was the route they had used to come in. Rough ground fell away beside it; the chairs could not simply be taken straight across the stones.

"Wahi sadak," he said. "Gaadi ke aage mod hai. Patients ko wahaan tak le jaana hoga."

The person on the line repeated the turn back to him and said that the responding crew had been warned. Vedant asked for the clinical crew's present position. He listened. They had passed the last road junction. The turn into the rough approach was still ahead of them.

He set the phone on the bonnet with the speaker on. Sitara brought water. He drank one mouthful from the bottle she held, then bent to give the next answer. His throat hurt from speaking over the engine; it would hurt more if he stopped being understood.

---

The blade man came past the damaged vehicle.

Dhruv backed towards the corner where the driver stood with the extinguisher. The spear man left the shaft in the window and followed on the other side of the folded seat.

Two directions now.

The strip beside the running vehicle was only wide enough for one chair at a time.

Sitara took the handles of Veer's chair.

"Ab Aadhya ki awaaz," she told Ira. "Main Veer ko aage karti hoon."

Aadhya moved a step ahead of Ira, where she could describe the ground without making her reach for an injured hand. The extra helper had Rudra. The first attendant and the bleeding driver could walk; they would follow the chairs towards the road bend.

Vedant picked up the phone from the bonnet.

There were only a few metres to gain. They were the wrong few metres to hurry blindly.

"Seedha," Aadhya said. "Ab chhota gaddha hai. Mere rukne par rukna."

Ira followed the sound with her ordinary eye closed. Her right hand trembled against her own supported forearm. She concentrated on the next word, not on making the landscape appear through the pain.

---

The blade came around the rear corner.

Dhruv stepped away from its point and struck the attacker's forearm with the heel of his sound hand. The contact hurt up his own arm. It turned the first thrust, not the one which followed.

The driver pulled the extinguisher's pin.

"Hato!"

Dhruv went sideways. The driver discharged the cylinder low across the man's approaching body, towards the space they had just left rather than the people moving towards the road.

White powder struck cloth and rose into the face.

The blade man turned his head. He swung through the cloud anyway. The edge scraped the vehicle, bright and short, and the driver jumped back from it with the cylinder still hissing.

He released the handle.

They had bought less ground than the whiteness made it look like.

Dhruv caught his sleeve with the sound hand and brought him away from the corner before the next blind slash could find it.

---

On the other side, the spear man stooped for a stone.

Aadhya saw the shoulder draw back.

"Sir neeche!"

The stone hit the bonnet. It skipped across the metal and fell between the two chairs.

Rudra flinched. The injured leg moved against its support. The tightness below his ribs became a flat, difficult weight, as though the next breath had to lift a stone of its own.

The extra helper stopped at his first broken sound.

"Chaloon?"

Rudra looked at the bend, the narrow strip ahead, the second man looking for something else to throw. He could feel heat in the good palm. He could also see the back of the helper's head directly in the line his hand would take.

He closed the fingers against the armrest.

"Mod tak," he said.

The chair moved again.

It was not the choice between using strength and having none. It was the choice not to put another body's shape inside a force he had failed to stop at a wooden staff.

---

Veer heard the cylinder scrape against the ground behind them.

The driver had put it down to get both hands around its handle again. He was shaking. Dhruv stood a little to one side of him, breathing with the effort it took to keep the attacker in view.

"Chhod do usse," Veer called.

For a moment the driver looked at the damaged vehicle, misunderstanding which thing he was being given permission to leave.

"Cylinder," Veer said. "Uthaa ke mat aao."

The man left it on the ground.

He came towards them empty-handed, fast enough to catch the side of the chair when Sitara needed to steer around a stone. Veer felt the steadier movement before he saw the hand.

The aid was still with the chair. Nothing else needed to be carried for the journey to count as a return.

---

The blade man came through the powder with his sleeve over his mouth.

His companion had moved along the vehicle's other side. They could not both be watched from behind one corner anymore. Dhruv gave ground towards the road bend, keeping his body angled rather than trying to meet two directions squarely.

Aadhya looked back once and saw the red spreading below his elbow.

She wanted to tell him about it. He already knew. She gave him the thing he could use instead.

"Patthar. Aapke peechhe."

He changed the next step.

Behind her, Ira counted that single pause in Aadhya's directions, waited for the voice to return and moved only when it did. Her ordinary eye watered down the cheek. She left it closed.

Then an engine sounded beyond the road bend, louder than the one they had left running.

A siren broke off.

---

The ambulance stopped short of the open approach.

A man in a medical vest got down on its protected side, looked towards the movement beyond the bend and stayed beside the vehicle. Behind it came another engine and a brief hard voice calling for the weapon to be put down.

The responders had not mistaken a call for medical help for a promise that the ground was clear.

The first attendant began waving with his good arm.

"Yahaan! Patients yahaan hain!"

Rudra tried to make out the vest beyond the turning stone. The edge of it would not stay sharp.

The police vehicle stopped behind the ambulance.

Two constables came forward on the road side, using the bend instead of walking into the space between the attackers and the chairs. One kept speaking to the man with the blade. The other directed the ambulance's driver towards the people coming out of cover.

"Hathiyaar neeche. Wahin raho."

Dhruv moved back. The first constable saw the torn sleeve, the empty sound hand and the two men beyond him. He told Dhruv to come towards the road, slowly, with his hands where they could be seen.

Dhruv obeyed.

Behind him, the spear man picked up the abandoned cylinder.

He swung it once and threw it across the bare strip. It struck the running vehicle's rear panel, fell and rolled. The sound made Ira recoil against her seat.

The blade man went backwards towards the damaged vehicle. He kept the blade raised. His companion followed him, empty-handed again.

Neither put a weapon down.

---

The constable did not follow them between the vehicles.

He used the bonnet for cover and repeated the order while his partner got the last people around the road bend. From there Dhruv could see only the upper part of the rock opening and the strip leading to it.

The blade disappeared first.

The spear man looked back once, at the shaft still caught through the broken window, then went after him into the passage. Dhruv saw the dark cloth at his waist pass out of the light.

He told the constable what he had seen.

"Andar gaye. Dono. Gaal pe chot waala ek aur aadmi pehle se andar tha. Aakhri baar wahin dekha tha."

"Raasta kahaan jaata hai?"

"Pataal. Abhi andar mat jaaiye. Mod ke baad dikhega nahi."

The officer asked the question again in another form, about the approach, the bends, any other ordinary way out he knew. Dhruv gave the part he had actually walked. He did not try to settle the man's understanding of the whole distance while his own sleeve was filling with blood.

The other constable called for support and kept people back from the approach.

---

The ambulance driver helped the extra helper bring Rudra's chair behind the bend.

The medical worker was waiting there with the bag open. Vedant gave the chest symptoms first, then the leg's jolts, the old wrist injury and the burst of force after which Rudra's breathing had changed. The man listened without asking the force to become an ordinary fall for the form to accept it.

He crouched beside the chair.

"Naam?"

"Rudra."

"Saans lene mein dikkat kab se?"

Rudra told him. Short pieces, the pain under the breastbone, worse when he tried to turn, the leg which had stopped accepting weight. The worker checked his breathing and pulse, put the small clip on an available finger and asked for space around the chair.

Aadhya stepped back with Ira. Sitara stayed close enough to answer where the old supports were, not close enough to crowd the hands which had come to help.

The man asked Vedant which receiving place had already been contacted. Vedant gave it. The worker made his own call.

---

Veer was assessed in the spare chair while Rudra's first observations were being relayed.

He could answer in sentences. The knee needed to stay supported. The shoulder and ribs hurt; the brief curve and the hurried movement had made both worse. He could not promise a standing transfer merely because he had managed the uneven lip with two people helping him.

"Chal ke dikhaana nahi hai," the worker said. "Abhi baithe raho."

Veer let the good hand rest against the armrest.

He had been holding it ready for so long that putting it down felt like a decision somebody might question.

Nobody did.

The first attendant sat when he was asked. His cloth had stuck where the blood was drying at the edge. The ambulance driver brought another dressing from the bag and followed his colleague's direction. The wounded vehicle driver stayed beside him, his own fold red against the brow.

"Main gaadi nahi chalaa paaunga," he said.

The words made the next arrangements harder. He said them again so there could be no useful misunderstanding.

---

The constable came back far enough to see the injured people without losing the approach.

One vehicle was damaged, its driver hurt. The other was intact, with its driver present. Two chair users needed supported transport. There were more injured adults than one crew could examine and take at once.

He gave that account over the radio and asked for another medical vehicle. The medical worker confirmed the need through his own dispatch, naming the urgent patient first.

No one started putting people into seats to see how many could be made to fit.

The unhurt driver turned off his engine when asked, took the keys and came around to be checked for the extinguisher powder and any injury he had not noticed during the retreat. He said his hands were shaking. He could see clearly. His breath was settling.

He looked past the bend towards the vehicle with its window broken.

Then he put the keys in his pocket instead of offering to go back for it.

The extra helper still had the chair's handles.

When the ambulance driver asked him to step a little aside, he did it without taking the brake off. Rudra watched the small adjustment with an absurd clarity. The man's thumbnail was split at the edge. Grit had settled in the fold beside it. It was the same hand which had come back through the passage after being told what waited there.

"Aapka kaam…" Rudra began.

The man bent closer.

"Abhi baat mat karo."

Rudra had meant that the promised work had ended before the fight began. He had meant money, the cut on the other attendant's arm, the driver sitting with blood on his forehead beside a vehicle somebody else owned. There was no sentence short enough for everything he was trying to put outside the excuse that they had chosen to help.

The man understood enough of the failed attempt to answer it another way.

"Sabka number inke paas hai," he said, indicating Vedant. "Koi chhup ke nahi jaa raha."

For now, that was the part Rudra could keep.

---

The medical worker asked about the old injuries while his colleague brought the stretcher around the bend.

Rudra named the wrist, the ankle, the knee. He told him the chair had jolted twice inside. On the approach he had flinched when a stone hit the bonnet, and the supported leg had moved again. He did not know how to separate the pain of the raw release from the pain which had followed it. He pointed with the good hand to the pressure under the breastbone instead of pressing the sore place to prove its position.

The worker watched his face through the short answers.

"Aapko pehle le jaayenge," he said. "Transfer hum karenge. Aap khud uthne ki koshish nahi karna."

He asked the extra helper to remain for that movement, then checked the vehicle side with the ambulance driver. There was room to bring the stretcher alongside without turning Rudra's leg across the rut. The men named their holds before taking any weight.

The chair was going to be left for another pair of hands to bring. Rudra would not have to keep the object with him by keeping his body in it.

---

Aadhya gave the constable the part of the attack she had seen.

The stone, the staff at the wheel, the red-black striking farther than the staff, the spear through the window. She pointed out which driver had ducked and which had taken the extinguisher. When the officer asked about the third man's arrival inside, she said she had already gone around the bend.

"Woh Rudra ne dekha. Veer ne bhi."

Veer said he could give his short account once the urgent patient had been moved. The officer wrote down the distinction and took the two words which had been shouted in the street as words the boys reported hearing.

Ira listened with her eye closed.

She had wanted to put the account in order. For once she let someone else's pen do work which did not need her hand or her sight. Later she could say whether her own part had been heard correctly. Later did not have to mean that the truth had been abandoned.

The constable asked that nobody go back for property while the approach was still uncertain.

The wounded driver nodded before anyone could answer on his behalf.

---

Vedant's phone moved against his palm.

A reply from Rukmini to the earlier message had come through. He asked Rudra whether he wanted it read. Rudra nodded.

Only a few ordinary words. They had received the news. Let the checks happen first. Call when he could.

There was no demand in it to make the account sound better.

Rudra looked at the stretcher and asked Vedant to tell her the ambulance was there. Tell her about the breathing too, because otherwise the first person she spoke to might say it without the sentence which belonged beside it: help had reached him.

Vedant sent the update as asked.

He did not begin a call over the medical worker's instructions. Rudra could hear the faint taps on the phone and understood, with a tired gratitude which had nothing impressive in it, that somebody else could put the right words in the right order for a while.

---

Sitara brought Ira farther from the road's glare.

Aadhya remained where Rudra could see her without turning. Her supported hands rested against her own body. He looked at them, then at the stretcher, and for a moment she thought he was going to ask whether she could help with the transfer.

"Door mat jaana," he said instead.

"Yahin hoon."

"Gaadi mein…"

"Pehle tum chalo. Hum bhi aa rahe hain."

It was not a promise that she would fit in the ambulance or reach the same room at the same instant. It was the answer she had to the thing beneath his unfinished sentence.

The medical worker moved into Rudra's line of sight and told him what would happen next. The extra helper came to the named side. Beyond them Veer's chair was still, the aid against it, and Dhruv had finally sat on a stone while the unhurt driver held a clean dressing to his sleeve.

Rudra tried to answer that he was ready.

The edge of the stretcher went grey. He heard his name, close by, and tried to follow it back to the face saying it.

His good hand slipped off the armrest.

When the man said his name again, he did not answer.
