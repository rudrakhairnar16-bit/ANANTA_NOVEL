# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR — THE TURN BEHIND THEM

---

> *"They said we held the passage.*
> *We held a little room in which to leave."*
> *— Ira's later register, correction to the first fighting account*

---

The attendant pulled the chair short.

The ferrule struck stone where the front wheel had been. A chip flew against Rudra's trouser leg. The staff rebounded, and the man holding it drew the end back towards the wheel again.

Rudra opened the good hand.

The red-black broke out before he had finished deciding what shape to give it. It caught the middle of the staff and struck the wall beyond. Wood split. Stone spat grit. A piece of the shaft struck its owner's cheek; another hit the ground beside Veer's aid.

The force did not stop neatly at the thing Rudra had wanted moved.

The chair kicked against the attendant's hold. Rudra's ankle jarred inside its support. Pain climbed through the knee and caught under the breastbone with the breath he had meant to use for a warning.

He got out one word.

"Hato."

Then there was only enough breath to keep breathing.

---

The staff man put a hand to his cheek.

Blood ran between the fingers. He tried to bring the broken weapon up with the other hand and found it shorter than the distance he had been controlling a moment before.

Sitara used that distance.

She took Ira along the clear side of the broad patch, with Aadhya following where she could see the pieces which had fallen. Veer moved the aid clear of the splinter beside it. The chair came after them, more slowly, with the attendant keeping Rudra's leg away from the wheel.

"Pair?" the man asked him.

"Dard. Zyaada."

"Haath mat lagaao. Main rokta hoon."

He steadied the support before the next push. The cut on his arm hurt when he did it. Rudra saw the flinch and knew the small service had not been performed by a body without costs of its own.

Behind them, Dhruv's recovered shaft met the blade again.

The sound kept the withdrawal from becoming a rush.

---

Aadhya heard something crack against the wall above the chair.

She could not tell whether it was loose stone shaken by the burst or another piece thrown by the first attacker. She named where it fell. The attendant kept the wheel to the clear part. There was no need to solve its whole history before avoiding it.

At the start of the rock bend, Sitara stopped long enough for Ira to find the new direction with the ordinary eye, then let her rest it again.

"Ab lip ki taraf," she said. "Meri awaaz pe."

The two girls went with her around the bend.

Veer wanted to keep pace. The knee would not accept the hurried turn. He shortened the movement, set the aid again and stayed clear of the chair instead of falling into its path in an attempt to be first out of danger.

Rudra could still see the broken staff in the attacker's hand.

The man stepped towards him, stopped at the sound of the blade meeting Dhruv's shaft and looked past the chair to the fight behind it.

For a moment his face was simply a hurt face, trying to decide where the next blow might come from.

---

The burst had gone.

Rudra had no new layer around the collar, no certainty that repeating the same desperate opening of the hand would produce the same brief space. The first had thrown splinters near his own friends. His breathing still would not let him speak a whole sentence.

The attendant asked whether he could move the leg at all without adding to the pain.

Rudra tried the smallest change and stopped.

"Abhi nahi."

That answer made the lip ahead more difficult. It did not make the ankle available to force a better answer out of it.

The attendant heard him and kept the chair still for the moment it took to settle the leg. They were nearly at the bend; the low stone beside it could keep a direct throw from reaching the chair once they were around. It could not carry the chair over the uneven lip by itself.

Veer looked towards that cover and then towards Dhruv.

"Pehle is mod tak," he said.

It was a distance he could name without promising the next one.

---

Dhruv gave ground on purpose.

One step brought him nearer the turning place. Another kept the blade from finding the side where his tender hand had stopped helping him. The iron head of the recovered shaft stayed between the attacker and the people withdrawing.

Rudra saw the man with the cut cheek move back towards the first attacker instead of following the chair immediately. He put the broken staff up to catch a blow aimed at his companion. His bloodied hand slipped on the wood.

The blade man caught his sleeve before he fell.

They had given the group a moment by refusing to let one of their own go down alone.

Dhruv spent it on the way back.

Veer reached the chair as it came around the rock bend.

He did not catch its handles. The shoulder would not make a reliable handle for somebody else's weight, and the attendant already had the chair under control. Veer pointed with the good hand to the place where the foot support would catch if they turned too sharply.

"Thoda peechhe. Phir ghoomna."

Rudra saw it too. The wheel could pass before the supported leg could. The attendant made the little reverse movement they had examined in peace, then brought them clear.

For a moment, the low rock kept the direct line of the attack off Rudra's chest.

He used the moment to breathe.

---

Farther along the street, a third figure came into view.

He carried a plain short spear. The blade man pointed him towards the companion with the bleeding cheek and broken staff.

"Atala waale, ghaayal lo! Sutala waale mod pe!"

Rudra heard both names. He could not put a birthplace inside a stranger from an order shouted in a fight. He could see the third man take the injured man's elbow, pull his weight into a steadier position and keep the spear's point turned towards the people leaving.

The blade man came forward again.

Whatever the labels meant between them, the work was being divided. One held the hurt man. One went after the return. There might be others beyond the visible street; the four had no count of the broken fronts.

Rudra repeated the two names quietly, enough for Veer to know he had heard them.

"Suna," Veer said. "Ab pehle nikalte hain."

---

Dhruv drew back to the bend.

The blade touched the recovered shaft and slid down it. He changed the sound hand's grip before the edge reached his fingers. The tender hand would not close. He kept it out of the next exchange rather than pretend pain was the only thing stopping it from being useful.

The opponent glanced past him at the split staff and the fresh mark in the wall. He did not rush blindly into the space Rudra's burst had crossed.

Dhruv used that caution. He did not try to turn it into a victory.

"Sitara!" he called. "Lip pe ek aur aadmi chaahiye. Pair pe nahi aa paa raha."

She was a short way ahead, still near enough for an ordinary voice to reach around the rock. Her answer came back that she had heard.

Then her next directions went towards the Earth side, with the girls.

No warning had reached Vedant by another route. It would arrive with people who had walked out carrying it.

---

The attendant moved the chair while the space was available.

Rudra could hear the man's breathing over his own. The cut arm left a smear when the sleeve brushed the chair. There was no time to clean it properly here; there was time to say that the man needed help too and not let the plan silently use him as though he had none of the group's injuries.

At the lip, Rudra tried to say what had changed.

"Jhatke ke baad… pair nahi."

The attendant understood. They stopped short of the assisted stand which had worked on entering. An earlier permission did not make the weight-bearing possible now.

The chair's brake held. The man checked that the support was not trapping the leg against the rim, then kept Rudra settled behind the turn in the stone. They would need the second fit person, not the nineteen-year-old with the strained shoulder offering to become one.

Veer said he could wait where he was without leaning on the chair.

It was an actual offer, in a size he could give.

---

A stone hit the bend behind them.

Dust fell. Dhruv's shaft struck the blade twice in quick succession. He had room to yield only until the next part of the passage; behind that were the people whose slower movement he had chosen to protect.

Veer lifted the good hand a little, felt the pull under the ribs and lowered it without trying to call back the pale curve. His first defence had cost something which the need for another had not repaid.

"Abhi nahi hoga," he said.

Rudra nodded. He had nothing better to offer by insisting that the answer change.

He looked at the rock, the support, the hands holding the chair still. The way out existed. The help needed to use it was taking time to arrive. He could not make that time disappear by naming his power more grandly.

He kept the injured wrist against its brace and told the attendant when the pressure at the ankle changed again.

---

Beyond the bend, the blade man called towards the street.

Someone answered. The words did not reach Rudra clearly enough to repeat. He heard a voice, not a secret order which the scene owed him the ability to understand.

Dhruv shifted his feet.

His breathing had grown louder. The sound hand still held the shaft, but the length of iron and wood was beginning to move with the effort it took to keep it raised.

He was not a wall. He was a man making the next few moments usable.

Sitara had heard Dhruv's call before the rock took the view away.

She brought Ira and Aadhya across the uneven place first, one instruction at a time. Ira used the ordinary eye for the near edge, then closed it against the grit and the change in light. Aadhya kept the hands clear while following the line Sitara had checked.

They came out on Earth without the other four.

Vedant was where he had agreed to be.

He saw the three, stood and asked the question before trying to complete the absent people in his head.

"Baaki?"

"Lip ke peechhe. Hamla hua. Do aadmi dekhe. Aur kitne, pata nahi. Rudra pair pe nahi aa paa raha. Ek aur aadmi chaahiye."

The information arrived with a person who had walked the distance. It was the first account of the fight anyone on this side had received.

---

The extra helper looked towards the opening.

He had agreed to help at the approach and with the road transfers, not to enter a fight somebody had neglected to mention. Sitara told him that the facts had changed: there were weapons on the Pataal side; Dhruv was holding the line behind the group; the immediate task was at the lip, to bring Rudra across without making him put weight on the ankle again.

There was no promise that it was safe.

He asked how far and whether Sitara would show him the place. She answered. He looked at the girls, at Vedant's protected forearm, then checked that the spare chair was ready on this side.

"Chalo," he said. "Dikhaa dena kaise pakadna hai."

He chose the changed task. Nobody made his earlier agreement do the work of this answer.

He went with Sitara.

---

Vedant stayed with the girls and the actual arrangements outside.

One driver helped place the seats farther from the direct line of the opening, with the shade Ira needed. The other began preparing the vehicles for a return which would no longer be the ordinary end of a short visit. There was room for the supports. There would need to be help at the receiving end, not a hurried unload onto the road.

Vedant contacted the receiving team from Earth.

He gave the limited, urgent facts he had: armed attack, patients returning, Rudra unable to repeat the entry transfer as before, possible fresh injuries and his own injured forearm still a limit. The people still inside would give their own accounts when they could. He did not report seven unchanged bodies merely because that would have made the transport easier to describe.

The Earth-side location and the armed danger were passed through the ordinary emergency contact too. A request for medical help and an appropriate local safety response was not a public broadcast of the patients' lives. Nobody was asked to solve the whole unseen territory before coming to the accessible approach.

---

Ira said the ordinary eye was watering and uncomfortable after the flying grit.

Vedant listened, kept her in the shade and followed the care already in place while arranging the clinical contact. The left cover stayed protected. There was no attempt to press a new kind of sight into use to find out what had happened behind the bend.

She knew the temptation. If she could see more, perhaps the waiting would stop being waiting.

Instead she said what she had actually heard and what she had not. Dhruv had called for help at the lip. Sitara had answered. There had been a brief sound of force, something breaking, people moving. She could not turn those sounds into a picture of every person still inside.

Aadhya gave the account of what she had seen: the second man taking the return, the staff's reach, the pieces thrown against the wall. Her hands had not struck out or taken a load. They still needed their supports, and she wanted them checked when the actual care was available.

The driver helped place the support without making her hold its weight.

---

Ira heard feet on the approach and opened the ordinary eye just enough to find the line from which they came.

Not the four yet. Another person moving an item needed for their return. She let the eye rest again and gave the waiting back its actual size.

Inside, Sitara and the helper passed the known bends on foot.

They did not find another route or ask the passage to become shorter. At the lip she called before coming into the cramped working space, so the people beyond knew whose movement they were hearing.

"Hum hain. Doosra aadmi aa gaya."

The attendant answered.

Then she could see Rudra in the chair, the leg held clear, Veer with the aid beside the rock and Dhruv a little farther back, using the recovered shaft to keep the blade out of the space they needed.

The extra helper went to the side the attendant named. He looked at the protected wrist before putting a hand anywhere near the boy's weight.

The second pair of hands had arrived by walking.

They agreed on the movement before making it.

The two fit men would take the weight which Rudra's ankle could not take now. The injured wrist would stay clear. Sitara would move the empty chair across the uneven place while they supported him, then they would settle him before the next part of the return.

Rudra heard the plan. He asked for a breath before they began.

They gave him that breath.

It was not enough to make the pain go away. It was enough for him to say he was ready for the help instead of being lifted while still trying to explain why the old transfer would not work.

The attendant and the extra helper moved together. The cut arm trembled with the effort; its owner said so and shifted the hold with the other man's help, not with Veer's shoulder. Rudra kept the bad wrist close while the supported leg stayed out of the weight it had refused.

Sitara moved the empty chair.

They lowered him into it and stopped long enough to check where the ankle lay.

Behind them, iron struck rock.

---

Dhruv had put the recovered shaft across the blade's next line.

The man could reach the stone turn. He could not reach through the length of the guard without risking the iron head. He tried the lower side, then the side where the tender hand no longer gripped.

Dhruv turned with the sound hand and the feet he could still place. The old hand injury had not become a useful weakness to be overcome once for applause. It stayed a limit at every exchange.

His boot slipped on grit.

He caught himself against the rock with the shoulder, not the open hand, and felt the impact run through him. The guard sagged. The blade scored his sleeve and caught the skin beneath before he brought the iron head up again.

The man drew back from the point.

For a moment both breathed, looking at the distance between them. Dhruv's blood marked the torn sleeve. He did not use the pause to chase.

He listened for the people behind him to finish moving.

---

Veer came over the uneven place with the extra helper's support and his own aid.

There was no rise to impress anyone, no last attempt to put his body between every other body and the world. He named the knee's difficulty before it became a stumble, changed the angle with help and let the next few steps be slow.

The attendant had Rudra's chair again. Sitara kept the movement clear for both boys.

"Aa gaye," she called back to Dhruv. "Ab tum."

He gave another step.

Beyond the blade man, the third attacker was coming forward with the spear. The man with the cut cheek remained nearer the broad turning place, holding the shortened staff and pressing cloth against his face. He was upright. Dhruv did not have a census of everyone farther down the road.

The spear could reach into a space the blade had not been able to control.

The little advantage of the recovered shaft would not last against that change.

---

Dhruv withdrew before the two weapons could work together at the bend.

He struck the shaft against the approaching spear once, hard enough to turn the point aside. His sound forearm took the shock. The tender hand did not return to help. He let the recovered weapon fall diagonally across the narrowest part as he stepped back, buying only the time another person needed to lift a foot over it.

It was wood and iron, not a seal.

He went after the others by the route he had checked. He did not know what the men would choose once they reached the discarded shaft. He knew what happened if he remained to find out while the people ahead still needed the passage clear.

He kept moving.

---

Rudra came out on Earth with the attendant holding the chair.

Veer followed with the aid and the extra helper. Sitara was there, and beyond her the two girls in the shade, Vedant, the drivers and the actual vehicles. People who had been absent from one another for a few minutes became countable again without anyone pretending those minutes had been harmless.

Aadhya saw Rudra's face and did not ask him to stand to show he had made it.

Ira heard the aid placed, then Veer's voice saying the knee needed the seat now. The driver brought the spare chair into use. It had been kept for an ordinary possible need; the need which arrived did not make the preparation less ordinary.

Vedant asked the attendant about the blood on his sleeve before asking him to do more work. The extra helper took the chair's hold while the wound was looked at and the first available care begun.

Rudra heard footsteps at the opening and turned his head.

Dhruv came through on his own feet.

He had no captured weapon to raise and no claim that the ground behind him belonged to them. He had a hand which would not grip, a cut beneath the sleeve and enough breath to say that everyone needed to move away from the opening.

They moved away from the opening.

The extra helper took Rudra's chair towards the vehicles. A driver brought Veer in the spare seat, with the aid placed where it would not catch a wheel. Sitara stayed with the girls, giving Ira the changed line and keeping Aadhya out of the small crowd of people trying to be useful around the same door.

The first attendant held the cloth over his cut arm with the hand which could do it. Nobody gave him the next heavy task simply because he had not let go of the chair inside.

Vedant's forearm stayed protected while he checked what could be checked and passed the changed account to the receiving people. The emergency help already called was not instantly present. The drivers and the helpers in front of him were the people available for this minute.

Dhruv kept looking back.

The sleeve around the new cut was darkening. His tender hand still would not grip. A folded cloth was given to him; there was no attempt to make him demonstrate that he could now hold a weight with it.

---

Rudra said the pain at the ankle had increased after the chair's jolt. The knee had pulled. The tightness under the breastbone had followed the burst, and his breath had not returned to its easier rhythm yet.

He said it in short pieces, with pauses.

Vedant treated those as reasons for urgent assessment, not the expected price which could be left unpaid once a boy was back on Earth. The receiving team heard them. The move into the supported vehicle seat would wait for the help and positions which kept the injured limbs out of another rough transfer.

Veer gave his own account: one brief defence, shoulder and ribs hurting, knee no longer reliable for the next standing effort. The spare chair remained under him. A little pale light had not bought an obligation to manage the rest unaided.

Ira said the ordinary eye was still uncomfortable and watering. Aadhya wanted the hands checked after the grit and the hurried movement, even though she had not used them to carry or strike. Their existing protections stayed in place while the appropriate care was being arranged.

No one reported four unchanged patients.

---

Near the vehicles, Veer repeated the words he had heard.

"Atala waale, ghaayal lo. Sutala waale mod pe. Yehi bola."

Vedant listened. The girls were close enough to hear this account, not suddenly to have heard the order inside Pataal for themselves. Dhruv confirmed the division he had actually seen: a third man taking the hurt one, the blade coming after the return, a spear replacing the shorter reach.

Two names called in one fight were not a complete history of the people using them. The ordinary movements did establish something. These had not been two unrelated strangers each throwing at the nearest body. They had made room for one another and divided the work of keeping the return under pressure.

That was what could be said now.

Rudra did not give the men a master he had not heard named. He did not turn their shouted labels into a verdict on everyone who might belong to either place.

He asked that the words be kept as words heard, with who had heard them attached. The fuller account could wait until the bodies had been examined.

---

He wanted his parents told that all four had returned to Earth.

Not that they were unhurt. Not that everything was finished. There had been an attack; first help was being given and urgent checks were being arranged. He could not speak to them properly yet. There would be an update after the receiving care.

Vedant heard the request and had the short message sent from Earth while the drivers and helper prepared the supported move. No one woke another patient for a long call or sent a photograph of blood as a substitute for an accurate sentence.

The small ordinary message could travel where the people could not yet go.

Rudra let that part leave his hands.

---

The road was still there.

One vehicle's engine was running. The other driver stood by the open door, checking that the approach for Veer's transfer stayed clear. Beyond them a branch moved in ordinary air. The world on this side had not become gentle because it was familiar, but it still contained the seats, supports, receiving people and actual places to which their need could be carried.

Rudra wanted those places fiercely.

He had no wish to turn back and win the strip of stone they had lost. He wanted the hurt people moved, the cut arm attended to, the breathing examined, the eye kept from spending more than it had. Wanting those things did not feel smaller than the old wish to stand last.

At the opening, Dhruv shifted towards a sound.

A spear-point cleared the rock.

The third man came through behind it, the same dark wrap at his waist, the same plain shaft Dhruv had knocked aside. He had followed the ordinary line the group had used. There was no signal to the ward, no name called, no impossible knowledge of where the vehicles would go after this approach.

He could see the vehicles now.

His arm went back.

The spear left his hand towards the driver holding the door.
