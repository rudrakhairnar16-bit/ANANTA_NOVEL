# CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN — THE MAN WHO MOVED THE WALL

---

> *"The first account recorded the paving as his weapon.*
> *Then the wall came away in his hand."*
> *— Ira's later register, correction to the narrow-passage account*

---

Dhruv struck the flying stone from below and to one side.

White light snapped from his sound palm. Tarak hit near the slab's corner, not across its whole face. The paving tilted. Its upper edge passed his shoulder instead of his chest; the lower corner clipped his shin as he turned behind the near rock.

Then it hit the lowered timber.

Wood burst against the wall's foot. One of the two restraining blocks jumped outwards and fell flat. The stone itself split, one piece stopping in the broken wood, another skidding streetwards into the space the man had just crossed.

Dhruv stayed on his feet.

The glow went out at his hand. His shoulder had pulled hard with the turn, and blood began wetting the cloth below his knee. He had not stopped the slab in the air. He had changed which part of him it could reach.

"Chalte raho," he told Sitara.

She was already clear of the timber's fall. The near flank towards the lip remained open.

---

The man stepped over the skidding piece.

He had not flinched from the white flash. Dust struck his wrap and the skin of one forearm. He looked at the place Dhruv had moved to, put a hand against the broken wall beside him and pushed.

A whole joined section shifted out of its bed.

Sitara heard the deeper crack beneath the small falling fragments. This was not a loose stone waiting for a careless heel. Mortar tore along an old course; pieces still fastened to one another came away together. The man's arm stayed straight while the wall moved.

Dhruv took a backward step.

The clipped leg accepted it badly. He could move the foot, could feel where the cloth stuck, but he could not turn the pain into an assurance about the bone beneath it. He kept the sound side nearer the rock and shortened the next step.

Sitara came towards him.

"Jao," he said. "Baahar bataao. Yeh line abhi khuli hai."

"Aap—"

"Aa raha hoon. Mere peechhe kisi ko bina bataaye mat aane dena."

He did not ask her to fetch a student for a lesson which had become difficult.

---

The man shoved the loosened wall section across the old street edge.

Dhruv drove another short Tarak burst down into its advancing lower corner. The light came from his own effort, bright at the palm and costly through the shoulder; the surveyor's ring hung untouched against his shirt. The corner shattered. The larger mass yawed into the now-empty patch by the wall instead of closing the near return.

The recoil forced Dhruv against the sound shoulder of the passage.

He gasped. The old hand remained close to his body, useless for catching that weight. His sound palm had begun to tremble.

The man kept coming.

There was no bright line left for him to absorb or steal. The burst had ended. He simply stepped through the dust it had made, shortening the distance with each ordinary placement of a foot.

Sitara turned and went for Earth.

---

She did not find a new way out because the known one was frightening.

The lip. The unequal stone before the pinch. The place where the old shaft still leaned, beyond the reach of her passing elbow. She crossed each part in turn, keeping the phone secure instead of trying to make its silence carry a warning.

Behind her, stone struck stone again.

She could not see that impact from here. She did not improve it into knowledge as she moved. What she knew was already enough: a new armed man, a hurled slab, Dhruv hit below the knee, the near return still passable when she left him at its shoulder.

At the pinch she slowed rather than fall into it. A lost second spent keeping her feet could still carry a report. A body wedged sideways could not.

The mouth opened ahead into ordinary Earth light.

The constable stepped clear to let her out, then put his lathi across the approach behind her without entering blind.

"Doosre kahaan?"

"Dhruv peechhe hai. Naya aadmi hai. Bada patthar phenka. Chot lagi hai. Andar koi mat bhejna."

Her breath would not let the whole report arrive in one sentence. She made him wait for the rest instead of supplying a comforting word while she recovered it.

"Main poori line se abhi lauti hoon," she said. "Raasta uske paas tak khula tha. Wahaan hamla chal raha hai."

This was the return account now.

It cancelled the photographs, the old clear view and every quieter purpose for which they had come.

Sitara called Vedant only after she was outside.

Rudra saw the change in his face before the phone was offered on speaker. He heard the report from her Earth-side voice: not a return to finish the old account, a new man throwing masonry, Dhruv hurt, the passage she had just used still open as far as his position.

The statement constable shut the notebook.

He called for medical help and gave the ordinary road approach, the new injury and the armed danger beyond it. His partner at the mouth requested police support. The acknowledgements came back. No vehicle had reached them yet.

"Main yahin tak dekh paayi," Sitara said when Rudra asked where Dhruv was now. "Jab chhoda, rock ke kandhe ke paas tha. Abhi ka agla qadam nahi dekh sakti."

Vedant went to the mouth. Rudra remained at the road seat with the constable while the new facts were put together, instead of following a frightened expression into a place nobody had described.

---

The driver heard the changed report from the road officer.

He chose ten further paid minutes behind the bend while the ordinary road remained clear, with the right to leave if it did not. The officer kept the approach free of other traffic and told dispatch where the car stood. Nobody asked the driver to bring it up as a wall against the mouth.

Sitara took the first-aid bag from the car.

Vedant brought his own full one-litre drinking bottle. The water in it was the water available to him. There was no basin waiting inside, no channel they had quietly decided must still be flowing because they needed one.

He told Sitara what his forearm could not do.

"Bhaari pakad nahi. Usko uthaana pada toh main dono haath nahi de paaoonga."

"Pehle dekhenge kaise aa sakta hai."

They would not drag an unseen injured leg out by deciding its owner was already fit to walk.

The mouth constable heard their limited purpose: reach Dhruv by the return Sitara had just walked, assess what ordinary help he could accept, withdraw. Not resume the photographs or send Rudra in after them. The constable stayed on Earth, keeping the approach and the calls.

Sitara led. Vedant followed with the bottle in his sound hand, the hurt forearm clear.

---

Dhruv had moved while she was away.

Not far. Far enough to bring the next rock shoulder between his body and the straight reach from the street. The man had to come round the projecting stone to reach him; the loose wall section he had shoved lay partly across his own easier footing.

Dhruv used that obstruction instead of trying another full burst.

He kept the sound hand against the rough rock, took his weight through the unstruck leg and moved the other foot only as much as the next placement required. Pain climbed below the knee. He did not ask it to become courage. He was trying to leave.

The man caught the wall section's upper edge.

Stone scraped over stone. He pulled the mass towards himself and out of the way with an arm which had already thrown the paving. Dhruv saw fresh grit spill down the forearm, saw a narrow dark scrape where the torn stone had met skin. The body was a body. It did not follow that his own next blow could stop it.

He looked back once towards the lip.

Sitara appeared before he could decide whether to risk the turn alone.

---

She stayed outside his injured leg's path.

"Pair pe kitna le paa rahe ho?"

"Kam. Neeche laga. Ghoomte waqt badhta hai."

He could answer. The foot moved, the toes felt his own touch through the torn cloth, but none of them called that an examination of the bone. She opened the bag on a clear stone, took a dressing and put pressure over the bleeding place without pressing the hard, swelling part beneath it.

"Peechhe jaa sakte hain," he said. "Saath chalna."

Vedant had reached the near flank.

He saw the displaced block, the split timber and the joined section which the man had hauled aside. He saw what the last Tarak strikes had broken and what they had failed to keep from advancing.

The distance to Dhruv was now short enough for help to matter. The distance from the man was short enough to make that help urgent.

Vedant set the bottle on sound rock within his reach.

"Meri taraf dekho," he called to the approaching man.

The man turned his head.

Vedant opened the cap with Sitara's brief help, poured the water into the shallow hollow at his own feet and left the empty bottle beside it.

Then he lifted his sound hand.

Hydra gathered from the water that was actually there.

The water struck the man's eyes in a narrow rising arc.

He turned his face and brought the free forearm across it. That was all Vedant bought: a blink, a changed angle, one step which did not come towards Dhruv. The water fell down the wrap and soaked into dust. It did not return neatly to the hollow.

"Ab," Vedant said.

Sitara walked beside Dhruv, keeping the dressing in place while he took the slow turn. He used the rock with his sound hand, the unstruck leg carrying most of what he could manage. At the uneven place, they paused and changed his foot's angle before continuing. She did not pull him through the pain to preserve Vedant's timing.

A fragment struck the rock behind them.

Vedant had thrown a second, smaller arc across the man's face. He had seen the arm lifting another broken piece and interrupted the aim, not caught the stone in water. The piece went wide and broke on empty rock.

The hollow was almost dry.

---

Dhruv reached the lip with Sitara.

He looked back. Vedant was withdrawing too, keeping the last shallow movement of water between his own feet and the broken ground where he had stood. The man stepped across that wet patch without being held by it.

Then he took the shorter line round the shifted wall section.

Vedant had to yield away from the passage instead of into it. The man's next step put his body between Vedant and the near return. The old inspected strip was no longer an empty way for everyone who needed it.

Sitara stopped.

"Isko baahar," Vedant said. "Abhi."

The words reached her because she was still within that last visible stretch, not because Earth had suddenly become able to hear him.

Dhruv could not take a fighting turn. She took him through the pinch, told him where the middle was dry and slowed when his leg caught against the torn cloth. Behind them, the last water splashed onto stone.

She could not carry two men out by refusing to finish bringing one.

---

They reached Earth together.

The mouth constable helped clear a place behind the outer shoulder of rock, off the direct opening. Dhruv sat with the leg supported on the bag's folded outer cloth, not bent under him. The dressing had held most of the bleeding; its edge needed fresh pressure.

The officer used his own phone to reach the medical contact his partner had supplied. He gave the changed findings and followed the instructions for ordinary first aid and watching Dhruv's responses. A clinician was still coming. Nobody there claimed to have ruled out a deeper injury.

Rudra had come only as far as this Earth-side cover when he saw them emerge. He had used the permitted short walk, not run on the ankle because fear could rename a load.

"Vedant?"

Sitara gave him what she had actually seen: the man cutting off the near strip, Vedant forced to its streetward side, the passage to the lip clear during her own latest return, water spent. The route to Earth had worked again. The route from Vedant to it was contested.

Not a clear inspection. A different fact.

---

Dhruv was breathing hard.

"Chhota mat samajhna," he said. "Deewaar ko ek haath se nikaala. Do Tarak se bas line badli."

He waited for the breath before continuing.

"Maha Devta ke darje ki taaqat lag rahi hai. Ohda nahi keh raha. Naam nahi jaanta."

It was an estimate from the force they had just encountered, not a title the stranger had announced. The stone, the torn courses, the little that Dhruv's own effort had purchased: those were the reasons he could give. He could not give the man's allegiance, birthplace or the person who had sent him.

The mouth constable did not turn that uncertainty into an order to send the boy.

He kept Dhruv under the first-aid instructions. His partner remained at the road, maintaining the ordinary approach and the support calls. The driver still waited beyond the bend within the new agreed interval. No pair of unseen hands had arrived to take any of those jobs.

Sitara rose. She was breathing harder now; the bag strap had left a red track across one palm. She took one spare wrapped dressing into her pocket, then left the kit within the officer's reach.

"Main waapas jaa rahi hoon."

Rudra looked down the actual opening.

"Main saath. Unko nikaalna hai."

"Yeh roop aazmaane ki jagah nahi."

"Aazmaane nahi."

It was not an assurance that his body could do everything the emergency might ask. He knew the comfortable walking he had actually done; he knew the outline had come once and cost him. Neither could promise a battle.

He told the constable before following her. Sitara showed him where to stop behind the lip's shoulder while she looked. If the way beyond had changed again, they would not charge the unknown street. The short walking he had earned did not make the force ahead of them safe.

She led, checking each changed view herself.

---

Rudra crossed the pinch with the recovering wrist kept out of the rock.

The ankle knew a short, careful walk now. At the unequal place he still had to shorten the step; beyond it his good hand hung empty. He kept his eyes on Sitara's actual footing.

At the lip, Sitara stopped him behind the sound shoulder.

He could see the empty bottle on the far rock. The hollow beneath it held wet grit, no usable pool. Vedant's retreat had brought him nearer; the other man had come nearer too, standing across the line he needed.

Vedant saw them.

"Peechhe raho. Woh—"

The man drove a fist into the rock shoulder's streetward edge.

Stone burst across the place where Vedant would have to step.

Vedant threw himself towards the gap the shattered edge had left.

The man's arm came across it.

Rudra raised his good forearm before he knew whether the outline would answer. One small piece struck above his cheek. He felt the cut, the sting of dust, the solid terror of having made the movement with nothing larger than his own body behind it.

Then red-black caught along the arm.

Jaagrut arrived unevenly, a density at the wrist, a broken reach across the shoulders, gold remaining distinct within it. For an instant the feet were outside the whole shape. Then the outline reached them too, and the man's sweeping arm met Rudra's instead of Vedant's neck.

The contact drove him sideways.

He did not stop that strength. He changed its line by less than a hand's width while the man was already moving. The stone edge beside them lost another chip. Rudra's ankle took the correction, and pain flared through the support.

"Ab!" he shouted.

He did not need the man defeated to need Vedant free.

---

Sitara caught Vedant at the upper arm, away from the hurt forearm, and guided him through the space.

He could take his own weight. He could not make the turn quickly enough to pretend he had needed no help. He came behind Rudra with the injured arm close and a breath which would not settle on the first try.

The man shifted his feet to follow.

Rudra pushed at the near wrist with his good hand. The outline tightened. The arm scarcely moved. What yielded first was the gravel beneath Rudra's heel.

He let go before the grip dragged him after it.

The man's knuckles scraped the wall where his head had been. More stone fell. Rudra stepped towards the lip, trying to leave with the others rather than turn the saved interval into a challenge.

The next blow cut across that step.

He took it on the outer forearm. A hard sound passed through the outline into the elbow and shoulder. The red-black held long enough to keep the fist off his face; his own back hit the rock behind him.

His mouth filled with the taste of a bitten cheek.

---

"Rudra, peechhe!" Sitara called.

"Koshish kar raha hoon."

He had no spare breath for an impressive answer.

Vedant tried to raise the sound hand. Nothing rose from the hollow. The last wet patch had sunk into the grit. He lowered the hand; there was nothing left there he could use.

His hurt forearm was shaking now. The fingers would not settle comfortably, and he reported the changed feeling when Sitara asked. She took him behind the next shoulder, away from the immediate striking line. Neither of them called the new tremor an ordinary continuation of the morning's light work.

Rudra could hear them going towards the pinch.

Then the old place at his collar burned.

He said it aloud.

Sitara came close, unwrapped the spare dressing from her pocket and pressed the clean fold against the new thin bleed. She had taken it before leaving the bag with Dhruv. Rudra held it lightly with the recovering hand, without leaning on that wrist or asking it to catch him. The other arm remained between his face and the man.

"Baahar aate hi check," she said. "Abhi nikalna hai."

He nodded. The cloth was care, not permission to keep the shape indefinitely.

---

Heat gathered at the good palm again.

Beyond the man lay the broken street. A wall hid the next strip of ground. Rudra could not see through it; nobody had walked its other side for this fight. It was not the bank at the yard, with a closed boundary and empty earth beyond.

He wanted the man away badly enough to make the unknown place disappear from the decision.

He saw himself doing it.

"Peechhe ka nahi pata," he said, more to his hand than to anyone else.

He closed the fingers before anything left them.

No outward streak crossed the street. He kept what effort remained in the failing body-outline and moved his own feet instead. The man came after him with nothing held now, the wall and the paving already spent, both empty hands available to strike.

Rudra could not turn that small refusal into victory.

The next contact folded his guard towards his own chest.

He yielded with it because staying rigid would put the load through the recovering wrist behind the cloth. The knee bent farther than he wanted. Pain travelled up from the ankle, and the outline flickered along that leg as though a torn sleeve had opened to the air.

He stayed upright against the shoulder of rock.

---

Beyond the pinch, Sitara and Vedant reached Earth.

The mouth constable saw them before asking for Rudra. Dhruv remained seated behind the outer cover, dressing pressed, alert enough to answer. The support vehicles had not yet reached the road turn. The driver's new waiting interval had not expired.

Vedant gave the immediate facts: water spent, new man still at the lip, Rudra inside and trying to withdraw, his own forearm newly changed after the effort.

The officer helped settle him beside Dhruv without putting pressure through that arm. The medical contact received the new symptoms. There was ordinary first aid available, not an examination completed by a voice on a phone.

Sitara looked back into the mouth.

"Main uske paas jaa rahi hoon. Raasta yahin hai."

She did not wait for the boy to become a report in the past tense.

The medical vehicle reached the road turn before Sitara went back in.

Its driver parked behind the bend. One clinical worker got out with him: two people, carrying their own bag and bringing the stretcher only after the road officer described the protected Earth approach. Neither entered the passage.

Sitara gave the handover at the outer cover, then told the mouth constable about Rudra's collar, ankle and position. She went back into the passage as the clinical worker knelt beside Dhruv. She took neither member of the crew away from the patients.

The worker examined Dhruv's lower leg, checked the foot's warmth, movement and feeling, and replaced the blood-marked covering with a proper dressing and support. The leg still needed urgent assessment in a place which could examine the injury fully. He would not be asked to walk the road because he had managed the passage under attack.

The medical driver brought the stretcher alongside on the clear Earth strip. They secured its wheels before helping the controlled move, keeping the injured leg supported. Dhruv lay back, alert and answering, his old hand kept out of the transfer.

The clinical worker then checked Vedant's changed forearm and finger feeling. The arm was supported, further gripping stopped and the new symptoms added to the receiving call. He too needed proper review, not another attempt to see whether a bottle could prove the hand still worked.

Both were now with the actual crew. The ambulance had not departed.

The mouth constable kept the approach; his partner kept the road. Inside, Sitara had only her own feet and hands to bring back to Rudra.

---

Rudra's outline was failing before he chose to let it end.

He tried one more ordinary step towards the return. The ankle would bear a careful placement; it would not accept the turn the man's next reach demanded. He brought the foot back rather than let the knee twist under it.

The blow hit the rock's projecting side.

Rudra used that moment to slide behind what remained of the shoulder, not to answer with a greater force. The red-black had thinned along the recovering leg. Trying to drag it whole again sent a sharp pull from the collar into the neck.

He stopped that effort.

The rest of the outline went out.

No new shape waited underneath it. His cheek bled, his mouth hurt and the cloth at the collar was wet against the fingers holding it. The arm which had taken the blows shook when he tried to keep it high.

He had got Vedant past the man. He had not got himself out.

---

The man came round the broken edge.

Rudra moved the good hand off his guard long enough to find the rock behind him. He did not put the recovering wrist there instead. The support would have become the same mistake in a different place.

His palm found grit, then a firmer hollow.

He eased his weight back a little. The unhurt side could take more of it if he could move the right foot without turning the knee. The therapist had made that sequence boring in a corridor. He wanted its dullness now more fiercely than he had ever wanted an audience.

A footfall sounded behind the pinch.

"Rudra. Main hoon."

Her voice came from the known turn. It was close enough now that he could hear how hard she was breathing between the words. She was not already at his shoulder. He had to keep the next few steps possible until she got there.

"Ankle phir lagi," he said. "Roop chhod diya."

"Sun liya. Jaldi ghoomna mat. Main dekh rahi hoon."

She came towards the lip and looked at what the blows had done to it. The old photograph could tell her where the clear strip had been. It could not tell her which fresh fracture would hold a foot now.

She stayed on the firmer middle and kept her hands free.

---

The man's fingers closed around the torn wall-end beside the shoulder.

He pulled.

A joined length of masonry came away. It dragged against the rock with a sound deeper than the loose fragments falling at its sides. Rudra saw the scrape on the man's arm open again; saw him shift his grip rather than lose the load.

Not weightless. Not effortless enough to become a story about somebody for whom stone was air.

Strong enough that the difference did not help yet.

The wall-end struck the ground between them.

Rudra pressed back into the hollow. Dust blew against the wet cloth at his neck. The impact sent a new line through the already-broken base of the shoulder, towards the uneven lip beneath his feet.

Sitara stopped before putting her weight across it.

"Baayein waala hissa," she said. "Meri taraf. Dheere."

He began shifting towards the sounder middle.

---

The first thing he felt was not a fall.

It was a small settling under the right sole, as though the stone had changed its mind about being flat. Grit poured from a line he had thought was only a scratch. The ankle support held the joint in its ordinary way. It could not hold the ground together.

Rudra tried to move his weight left.

The good palm scraped across the hollow behind him. His recovering hand stayed at the collar, fingers slick against the dressing; he would not throw that wrist into a blind catch and call the damage necessary before it happened.

Sitara was coming along the middle. He could see where her next foot would land. There was still a way out behind her, the pinch and the ordinary turns towards Earth. Only a few turns, and a man could sit down with someone whose work was to hear where he hurt.

He needed to put his foot on the part which remained.

The crack under him widened with a dry, intimate sound. The man's weight came forward on the far side of the broken wall-end.

Rudra lifted his heel.

The lip sheared away beneath his right foot.
